# United States ex rel. Touhy v. Ragen, 340 U.S. 462 (1951)

STUB — created during the fact-reconcile wiki audit for the "DHS Showed 11%. FOIA Showed Everyone." flagship (Operation Metro Surge). The article links the phrase "Touhy regulations" to this URL as the doctrine's primary legal authority. ## Why it's cited
Supports the draft's doctrine-definition claim: the Touhy regulations, originating in this 1951 Supreme Court decision and resting on the Housekeeping Statute (5 U.S.C. §301), let a federal agency head decide what records/testimony are released to outside parties (including states). See the wiki concept page [[Touhy Doctrine]] for the full analysis and the Metro Surge application (DHS asserting "Touhy doesn't apply").

## Core holding (for ingestion)
A subordinate DOJ employee could not be held in contempt for refusing to produce records when obeying a valid agency regulation that centralizes release decisions in the agency head. The decision is narrow and procedural: it channels *whom to ask and how*, and expressly reserves whether the agency head himself can refuse and whether sovereign immunity ultimately shields the agency.
