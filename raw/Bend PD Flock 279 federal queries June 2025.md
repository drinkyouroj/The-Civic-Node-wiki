# Federal Immigration Officials Made 279 Queries into Bend's Flock Safety Data in its First Three Weeks

**By Peter Madsen, The Source — Bend, Oregon**
**Published: May 6, 2026**

Bend's police department inadvertently allowed federal immigration authorities to access its automated license plate reader (ALPR) database 279 times during a three-week period in June 2025. The breach occurred due to a user error that left a data-sharing function in its default "National" setting, enabling queries from Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE), Customs and Border Protection (CBP), and Homeland Security Investigations (HSI).

## The Technical Issue

Captain Brian Beekman, who oversaw the Flock Safety camera program, explained that the department didn't initially understand what the "National Lookup" feature meant. He noted: "What we didn't know is that National Lookup is a reciprocal sharing feature" that allowed outside agencies to query their system.

When Beekman discovered the setting on June 25, 2025 — three weeks into the program — he immediately disabled it. The Oregon Law Center later identified that CBP made 118 of those queries, with an additional 161 queries from third-party law enforcement agencies acting on behalf of federal immigration authorities.

## Query Breakdown

- **279 total queries** by federal immigration officials — June 4–25, 2025
- **118 queries** by U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP)
- **161 queries** by third-party, out-of-state law enforcement agencies acting on behalf of ICE, CBP, and HSI
- Bend PD lacks evidence that data was actually extracted; Flock's audit logging at the time was insufficient to determine what was retrieved

## Official Response

Police Chief Mike Krantz and Mayor Melanie Kebler characterized the incident as a configuration error rather than a privacy breach. Kebler stated that "'privacy breach' implies the overriding of security systems or someone gaining unauthorized access," which did not describe what occurred.

Catalina Sánchez Frank, executive director of the Latino Community Association, noted: "The timing represents a concerning overlap, as June 2025 was an intense period of immigration law enforcement in our area."

On January 7, 2026, Bend City Council voted to immediately shut down the Flock cameras, allowing the contract to expire on May 1.

## Legislative Response

Oregon Senate Bill 1516, signed March 31, 2026, restricts ALPR data sharing to Oregon agencies only and requires agencies to retain non-investigative data for no more than 30 days, with mandatory public audit reporting every 30 days.

## Key Architectural Detail

The "National Lookup" feature is reciprocal by default: enabling it to query other agencies also enables those agencies to query you. Captain Beekman: "when you turn that on, yes, you can query outside your state, but that actually turns on the ability for other agencies in the country to query information from your agency."

Bend PD deployed four Flock cameras for seven months without fully understanding this capability.
