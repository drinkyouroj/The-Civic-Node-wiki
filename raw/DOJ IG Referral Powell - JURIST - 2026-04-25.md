DOJ closed its criminal investigation into Fed Chair Jerome Powell on April 25, 2026 (announced April 24), transferring the matter to the Federal Reserve's Inspector General.

## Underlying probe
Examined whether Powell made false statements to Congress about Fed headquarters (Eccles Building) renovation costs — specifically luxury features he denied existed. Charge would have been under 18 U.S.C. § 1001 (false statements to Congress), max 5 years.

## Pirro's statement
US Attorney for DC Jeanine Pirro announced the closure, said she would "not hesitate to restart a criminal investigation should the facts warrant doing so." Language treats the referral as a pause rather than permanent abandonment.

## IG's role and precedent
Inspectors general are independent investigators within agencies examining mismanagement, waste, fraud. The Fed IG previously audited the renovation project in 2021, identifying improvement areas but finding no evidence of criminal conduct or fraud — establishing precedent that cuts against the criminal theory.

## Contributing factor
Chief Judge James Boasberg quashed DOJ's grand jury subpoenas in March, ruling the government provided no evidence of criminal conduct beyond "displeasing the president," significantly weakening prosecutorial leverage.
