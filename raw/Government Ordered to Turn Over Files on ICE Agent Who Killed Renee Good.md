# Government Ordered to Turn Over Files on ICE Agent Who Killed Renee Good

A federal magistrate judge has ordered prosecutors to release critical documents related to ICE agent Jonathan Ross, who fatally shot Renee Good on January 7, 2026, in Minneapolis. The order emerged from a separate case involving Roberto Carlos Muñoz-Guatemala, whom Ross attempted to apprehend in June 2025.

## Key Facts

- Ross shot and killed Good, a 37-year-old observer, during an immigration enforcement operation in south Minneapolis on January 7, 2026.
- Muñoz-Guatemala was convicted on December 12, 2025 of assaulting a federal officer after allegedly dragging Ross with his vehicle during a June 17, 2025 traffic stop.
- Judge Jeffrey M. Bryan issued the directive requiring federal prosecutors to surrender materials by May 1, 2026.
- The DOJ had previously refused to open a criminal investigation into Good's killing, declaring it an act of self-defense.

## Documents Ordered Turned Over

- Ross's personnel and training files
- Records of statements Ross made within 60 minutes before and during Good's shooting
- Witness statements regarding the killing
- Medical records concerning Ross's fitness for duty
- Cell phone data extraction records
- Body-worn camera footage of the incident

Materials will first go to a magistrate judge for review and necessary redactions before reaching the defense. Information potentially presented as mitigating factors may become public record unless sealed by protective order.

## Quotes

> "My client is entitled to a full hearing and to review these documents to determine whether there's any basis for a new trial." — Defense attorney Eric Newmark

> "This judge is effectively doing the investigation that the United States has turned its back on." — Defense attorney Shauna Kieffer

> "Renee Good's family has been forced to wait for answers while DHS and ICE closed ranks." — Rep. Becca Balint (D-Vt.)

## Context

Good's death sparked significant protests in Minneapolis and calls for accountability. The White House had characterized Good as a domestic terrorist, a claim rejected by local leaders. The discovery order represents a rare judicial intervention into the handling of the case, compelled by a defense motion in the Muñoz-Guatemala assault case — not by any government accountability initiative.
