A features-style assessment of what reopening the Strait would actually require, surveying insurance, mine clearance timelines, and the conditions international maritime bodies say must be met before normal shipping can resume.

Key data:
- Strait handles 20% of world's oil and LNG during peacetime
- ~2,000 ships remain stranded in the Gulf
- US estimates 6 months required to clear mines believed to have been laid by Iran
- War-risk insurance premiums could rise from pre-war 0.25% of hull value to 5% of hull value
- Pre-war premium on $100M vessel: ~$250,000; post-reopening estimate: up to $5M
- IEA described closure as "the largest oil supply disruption in history"
- Insurers require sustained normal vessel movement before pricing responsibly — not just isolated transits

Quotes:
- Oscar Seikaly (NSI Insurance Group CEO): "If the situation changes by the hour, the risk becomes almost impossible to price responsibly." Insurers "require normal vessel movement to resume over a sustained period, not just isolated transits."
- Jakob Larsen (BIMCO maritime security head): "the mine threat is of particular concern" when reopening shipping routes.
- Munro Anderson (Vessel Protect director): "There must be an explicit commitment by all parties to enforce freedom of navigation through established and internationally recognised shipping channels."
