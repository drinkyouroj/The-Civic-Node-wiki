Human-cost focused piece quantifying the seafarer toll of the Hormuz standoff, with IMO and UN data on attacks, deaths, and the near-total collapse of normal transit volumes.

Key data:
- 20,000 seafarers stranded on hundreds of vessels unable to transit
- ~1/5 of global oil and LNG normally passes through the waterway
- Traffic April 13-19: ~80 vessels, down from 130+ daily transits before the war
- At least 10 seafarers killed since the war began (UN data)
- Dozens of ships attacked during the conflict
- Iran fired on ships and seized two cargo vessels
- US maintained blockade of Iranian ports despite extending ceasefire indefinitely
- Iran reportedly placed sea mines in the waterway
- Iran claimed the strait is open to vessels it deems non-hostile

Quotes:
- Captain Arunkumar Rajendran (stranded ~8 weeks): "Seafarers are the backbone of global trade, yet we are often the most affected by regional geopolitical conflicts."
- Arsenio Dominguez (IMO Secretary General): "There is no safe transit anywhere in the Strait of Hormuz."
