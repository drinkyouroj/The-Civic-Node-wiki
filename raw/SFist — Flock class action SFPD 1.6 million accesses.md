# Lawsuit Says SFPD's Flock Cameras Were Accessed by Federal Agencies 1.6 Million Times

**Author:** Leanne Maxwell | **Published:** February 28, 2026 | **Source:** SFist

## Key Claims

Oakland-based law firm Gibbs Mura filed a class-action lawsuit alleging Flock Safety's license plate readers improperly shared data with federal and out-of-state agencies over **1.6 million times during a seven-month period** via SFPD cameras.

Los Altos cameras also reportedly shared information with out-of-state agencies more than a million times.

## Alleged Violation

**California state law** prohibiting sharing of California driver information with out-of-state law enforcement and the federal government — NOT a contractual prohibition. The lawsuit is grounded in California state law, not Flock's terms of service.

## Specific Access Incidents

El Cerrito Police Department audit confirmed access by:
- FBI — June–August 2023
- ATF (Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, and Firearms) — June–August 2023
- GSA Office of the Inspector General — June–August 2023
- U.S. Postal Inspection Service — September–November 2023
- Veterans Affairs Police — May 2025

## Court Named

**No specific court named** in the article.

## Quotes

> "The problem that we allege, is that Flock is sharing this information on California drivers with out-of-state law enforcement..." — David Berger, Gibbs Mura partner

**Flock response:** The company stated it "takes privacy, legal compliance, and data security extremely seriously" and intends to "vigorously defend" against the allegations.

## Notes

The article confirms 1.6 million accesses and seven-month period but does NOT name a court. Any reference to "San Francisco Superior Court" in external coverage is unverified by this source.
