# Don't count on rate cuts just yet

## Key content
The AP article examines whether Kevin Warsh's confirmation as Fed Chair will deliver the rate cuts Trump expects. Several constraints are highlighted:

- Inflation at 3.3%, well above the Fed's 2% target
- Warsh: "we have a short window to try to bring inflation back down to where it should be"
- Warsh is one of 12 FOMC voters; the March vote was 11–1 to hold (Miran the lone dissenter)
- Wall Street prices minimal probability of cuts before October 2027

## Bessent / Miran / Warsh coordination signal
- Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent: "if the Fed wanted to wait for some clarity before cutting rates, I understand that."
- Economists interpreted the Bessent comment as "providing political cover for Warsh to maintain current rates for several months" — i.e., letting the new Fed Chair establish credibility before delivering the cuts the administration wants.
- Warsh will replace Stephen Miran (whose term ended; Miran had voted to cut at every meeting he attended, dissenting five consecutive times).

## What this is and isn't
The AP frames the Bessent statement as strategic latitude rather than evidence of a coordinated playbook. Rugaber explicitly notes "no explicit coordination between Bessent, Miran, and Warsh is documented in the piece" — the Bessent cover-giving is the only concrete behavior, and it can be read as either coordination or independent flexibility.

## Quote: Jon Faust (former Powell adviser)
"Warsh comes in with essentially none of the gravitas that Greenspan had. Instead, Warsh comes in with the baggage that Trump has really loaded on him."
