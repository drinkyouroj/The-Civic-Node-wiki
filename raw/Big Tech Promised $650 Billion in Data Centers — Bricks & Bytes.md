Bricks & Bytes analysis of the gap between announced and actually-built AI data center capacity in 2026.

Headline figures:
- Big tech committed ~$400 billion to AI infrastructure in 2025
- $650 billion planned for 2026 (60% year-over-year increase)
- Only 5 GW of the 16 GW global 2026 data center pipeline is under construction (per Sightline Climate tracker)
- Sightline Climate expects 30-50% of the 2026 pipeline to slip or cancel outright

Specific cancellation: Oracle and OpenAI scrapped a planned 600 MW expansion of their Abilene, Texas facility in March 2026 after financing talks collapsed. (Corroborates [[Stargate Data Center Expansion Cancelled — Oracle and OpenAI]].)

Component bottleneck (corroborates Wood Mackenzie figures):
- Power transformer lead times average 128 weeks
- Generator step-up (GSU) units at 144 weeks
- Prices up 70-150% since 2020

Energy cost layer:
- Electricity prices in Virginia: increased 267% over five years
- US natural gas: roughly 60% higher than 2024

Financial reframe — Michael Burry argues hyperscalers depreciate AI hardware over 5-6 years when actual economic life is 2-3 years; understated depreciation through 2028 amounts to ~$176 billion.

Direct quotes:
- Sightline Climate analyst (per Bricks & Bytes attribution): "If one piece of your supply chain is delayed, then your whole project can't deliver." (NOTE: this same quote is attributed to Andrew Likens of Crusoe Energy in [[Almost Half of US Data Centers That Were Supposed to Open This Year Slated to Be Canceled or Delayed — Futurism - 2026-04-02]]. Bricks & Bytes attribution may be in error; verify before quoting.)
- Article assertion: "The gap between announcement and shovel is now the biggest signal for anyone building, financing, or investing in data center capacity."

Central thesis: the binding constraint has shifted from semiconductor availability to electrical grid capacity and equipment lead times; the announcement-to-construction ratio is the critical metric for assessing actual AI infrastructure buildout.

⚠️ Reconciliation note: Bricks & Bytes claims 5 GW of 16 GW global 2026 pipeline under construction. Sightline Climate data via Bloomberg/Futurism (Apr 2026) says 4 GW of 12 GW US pipeline. Different scopes (US vs. global) reconcile the numerical difference; cite both carefully.
