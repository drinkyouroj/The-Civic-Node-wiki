# Data Centers and Customer Rate Protection (DTE Energy official claims)

*DTE Energy corporate page — captured June 3, 2026*

## Rate-protection commitment
DTE states: "**data center development will not increase customer rates**" and intends to "**pause future electric rate requests for at least two years** as data centers come online."

## Cost allocation
DTE claims data centers "**will cover all new costs required to serve them**" through special service agreements, so existing customers won't subsidize data center infrastructure.

## Legislative safeguard
Michigan law — including the recent (2024) data center legislation — "**ensures our customers will not subsidize data center rates**," per DTE.

## Financial claims
- DTE's two data center contracts are projected to contribute **nearly $9 billion** toward electric-system improvements through **2045**, which the company says will "reduce the total amount needed from other customers."
- For the Saline Township project specifically, DTE estimates **$300 million in annual investment** to improve the electric grid, potentially reducing pressure on residential bills.

## Renewable requirement
Under Michigan law, new generation serving data centers must come from clean energy sources, which DTE frames as accelerating its path to **50% renewable by 2030** and **60% by 2035**.

---

*Ingest note: These are DTE's assertions. They are directly contested by AG Dana Nessel, the Citizens Utility Board of Michigan, and energy consultant Douglas Jester (who estimates a 1 GW data center could raise residential rates 5–10% absent protections). The wiki files DTE's claim and the critics' rebuttal side by side under [[AI Cost Incidence]] — the contested-causation core of the dispute.*
