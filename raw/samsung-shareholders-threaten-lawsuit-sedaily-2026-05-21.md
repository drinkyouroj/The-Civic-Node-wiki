# Samsung Shareholders Threaten Lawsuit Over Wage Deal, Calling It Illegal

*Seoul Economic Daily — May 21, 2026 — Lee Seok-jin*

Samsung Electronics (005930.KS) defused the risk of a general strike late Monday night by reaching a tentative wage agreement with its labor union, but shareholders have now declared legal action, calling the deal an "illegal agreement that damages shareholder value." As the labor-management dispute over performance bonuses winds down, a new legal battle is emerging between the company and capital market participants over shareholder rights protection.

The Korea Shareholders' Movement Headquarters, a minority shareholder group of Samsung Electronics, held a rally at 10 a.m. on the 21st near the Hannam-dong residence of Samsung Electronics Chairman Jay Y. Lee in Seoul, expressing total opposition to the tentative labor-management agreement on performance bonuses.

The tentative agreement reached the previous day partially reflected the union's long-standing demand to institutionalize performance bonuses, including a provision to pay a special performance bonus to the semiconductor (DS) division for 10 years, funded by 10.5% of business performance.

Min Kyung-kwon, head of the Shareholders' Movement Headquarters, argued in Hannam-dong that "a labor-management agreement allocating a certain percentage of pre-tax operating profit is clearly illegal." He added, "Since this matter is directly tied to shareholder property rights, it is legally null and void unless it goes through a shareholder meeting resolution procedure under the Commercial Act."

The Shareholders' Movement Headquarters warned that if a board resolution is brought forward to ratify the tentative agreement, it will file a lawsuit with the court to confirm its invalidity and simultaneously exercise its right to seek an injunction against illegal acts to block the company from paying out the bonuses.

The shareholders' backlash stems from concerns that the agreement, which adds long-term fixed costs such as performance bonuses, will deplete dividend funds and reduce corporate value. The Shareholders' Movement Headquarters demanded a written explanation from the company on the legal basis for the bonus calculation and its consistency with distributable profit limits, while also beginning to consolidate forces to invoke minority shareholder rights.

Through the shareholder collective action platform "Act," the group has set a target of securing the 1% stake required to file a shareholder derivative lawsuit under the Commercial Act. "We will immediately begin a nationwide rally of shareholders starting today," an official from the Shareholders' Movement Headquarters said. "We will also send letters to domestic and foreign institutional investors urging them to consolidate shareholder rights, in order to bring about a joint response."
