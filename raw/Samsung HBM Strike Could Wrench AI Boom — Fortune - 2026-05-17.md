# A 45,000-person labor strike at Samsung's memory chip plants could throw a wrench into the AI boom

Nearly 45,000 unionized Samsung Electronics workers plan to strike for 18 days beginning May 21, 2026 — the largest work stoppage in semiconductor industry history at a critical juncture for AI infrastructure.

## Samsung operations

- Produces approximately one-third of global DRAM
- With SK Hynix, accounts for roughly two-thirds of global DRAM market
- Operates 12 fabrication lines across three complexes in South Korea
- Employs over 260,000 people worldwide
- Investing $73 billion in semiconductor capex and R&D in 2026

## HBM market share (Q1 2025)

- Samsung: 17%
- SK Hynix: 62%
- Micron: 21%
- Samsung reclaimed overall DRAM leadership by end of 2025
- HBM4 production run "already sold out"

## Labor demands vs. counter-offer

- Union seeks: 15% of operating profit to bonuses, removal of 50% bonus cap, 7% wage hike, formalization of bonus terms in employment contracts
- Management counter: 13% of operating profit as one-time 2026 payment only
- SK Hynix settled at 10% of annual operating profit, translating to $460,000–$477,000 per worker in 2026
- ~200 Samsung employees reportedly departed for SK Hynix over four months (union chairman Choi Seung-ho's claim)

## Strike impact estimates

- April 2026 one-day walkout: foundry output dropped 58%, memory fabrication fell 18%
- Potential losses from full 18-day strike: 30 trillion–100 trillion won
- JPMorgan estimate: 2.1–3.5 trillion won operating profit impact (labor costs plus production losses)
- Labor cost impact alone: 7–12% downside to 2026 operating profit

## Market context

- Samsung Q1 2026 operating profit: nearly eightfold increase to record levels
- Combined Samsung/SK Hynix projected 2026 operating profit: ~500 trillion won
- Apple negotiated emergency memory contracts with Samsung, accepting 100% price increase

## Negotiations status

- May 13 National Labor Relations Commission session (17 hours) failed to reach agreement
- Samsung proposed direct dialogue; union agreed only if co-CEO Jun Young-hyun presents concrete proposals
- No deal reached as of article publication (May 17, 2026)

## Direct quotes

- Choi Seung-ho (union chairman): "Roughly 200 Samsung employees have left for SK Hynix over the past four months."
- Shin Je-yoon (Samsung Chairman): "Worried about losing market leadership amid fleeing customers and falling competitiveness" (regarding strike scenario)
