Sen. Thom Tillis (R-NC), the Republican who had effectively blocked Kevin Warsh's nomination as Fed chair, said Sunday April 26 he is dropping his opposition after the DOJ ended its criminal investigation of Fed Chair Jerome Powell on Friday April 24.

Tillis told NBC News he had received direct assurances from DOJ officials that the probe was "completely and fully ended" and that reopening would require a criminal referral from an inspector general. He stated: "I needed to feel like they were not using DOJ as a weapon to threaten the independence of the Fed."

His stated concern was not the Eccles Building renovation numbers but whether DOJ was being used as a tool against an independent institution.

## Committee math
The Senate Banking Committee has 13 Republicans and 11 Democrats. A single GOP defection would have deadlocked the panel and blocked Warsh from advancing. With Tillis on board, Warsh advances on a party-line basis.

## Timeline
- Senate Banking Committee vote: Wednesday April 29 at 10am ET (same day as the FOMC decision).
- Floor vote: expected before Powell's term as chair ends May 15.

## Democratic posture
Every Democrat on the committee is expected to vote no. Sen. Elizabeth Warren (D-MA), ranking member, called the DOJ probe closure "an attempt to clear the path for Senate Republicans to install President Trump's sock puppet Kevin Warsh as Fed Chair."

## Other Republicans
No other public GOP holdouts identified beyond Tillis. With Tillis flipped, the path to confirmation in committee and on the floor is clear on Republican votes alone.
