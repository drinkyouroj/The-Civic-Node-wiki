## Topline

President Donald Trump is mulling government oversight over new AI models before they are publicly released, the New York Times reported Monday, a reversal in Trump’s approach to the technology after a fallout between the Trump administration and Anthropic.

## Key Facts

The Trump administration is considering an executive order to establish a working group on AI featuring tech executives and government officials, including plans for a formal government review process for new AI models, the Times [reported](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/05/04/technology/trump-ai-models.html "https://www.nytimes.com/2026/05/04/technology/trump-ai-models.html"), citing U.S. officials and people briefed on the talks.

The review process reportedly may be similar to one under development in the U.K., which has [tasked](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-10003/ "https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-10003/") several government agencies to review safety standards for AI.

White House officials held talks with executives from Anthropic, Google and OpenAI about some of the plans under consideration, according to the Times.

In March, Trump [appointed](https://www.forbes.com/sites/saradorn/2026/03/25/trump-taps-zuckerberg-huang-and-ellison-for-white-house-ai-panel-report-says/ "https://www.forbes.com/sites/saradorn/2026/03/25/trump-taps-zuckerberg-huang-and-ellison-for-white-house-ai-panel-report-says/") 13 members to a new AI advisory panel, whose members reportedly included Meta’s Mark Zuckerberg, Oracle’s Larry Ellison, Nvidia’s Jensen Huang and Dell founder Michael Dell. Trump said in January the group would advise the president on “matters involving science, technology, education and innovation policy.” The appointments came as the Trump administration issued a legislative framework it wanted Congress to implement, creating a uniform national policy on AI that would supersede state rules.

Trump [signaled](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BrTT7dX0mcQ "https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BrTT7dX0mcQ") a preference for relaxed oversight of AI last year, saying in July, “We have to grow that baby and let that baby thrive,” adding, “We can’t stop it with foolish rules and even stupid rules.” He said some regulation could be necessary, but noted any rules would have to be “more brilliant than even the technology itself.” Trump’s opinion on AI appeared to shift after Anthropic and Pentagon officials entered a contract dispute in February. During the talks, Anthropic said it turned down a request from the Trump administration for unrestricted access to the AI models, after which Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth said he would designate Anthropic as a supply chain risk to national security. Anthropic filed a lawsuit to block the Pentagon’s move, but a federal appeals court earlier this month refused a temporary pause on the designation while the lawsuit plays out. Despite the dispute, Anthropic cofounder Jack Clark [said](https://www.forbes.com/sites/zacharyfolk/2026/04/13/anthropic-is-talking-to-us-government-about-mythos---despite-tensions/ "https://www.forbes.com/sites/zacharyfolk/2026/04/13/anthropic-is-talking-to-us-government-about-mythos---despite-tensions/") the company was in talks with the federal government about its AI model Claude Mythos, which the company claimed could exploit security flaws in “every major operating system and web browser” and warned it was too dangerous for public use. The White House has feared possible repercussions of an AI-enabled cyberattack in recent weeks, the Times reported.

The Pentagon last week announced agreements with OpenAI, Alphabet, Nvidia, SpaceX, Microsoft, Amazon and Reflection for their AI tools to be used on the agency’s classified networks for “lawful operational use.” AI capabilities from each firm will be integrated into the Pentagon’s networks to streamline data analysis, “elevate situational understanding and augment warfighter decision-making in complex operational environments,” the Pentagon said.
