# Eric Holder 'Too Big to Jail' Testimony — Senate Judiciary Committee, March 6, 2013

Transcript of the exchange between Senator Chuck Grassley (R-Iowa) and Attorney General Eric Holder during a Senate Judiciary Committee oversight hearing. The exchange became the defining public articulation of the Obama DOJ's position that some financial institutions were too systemically important to be criminally charged.

## Grassley's Opening

Senator Grassley expressed concern about a "too big to jail" mentality spreading across federal enforcement — from fraud cases to terrorist financing to money laundering cases. He cited HSBC, which had admitted to laundering money for Mexican drug cartels, helping rogue regimes evade sanctions, and assisting Saudi banks with ties to terrorism. HSBC had not been criminally indicted; it had paid a fine and entered into a deferred prosecution agreement.

Grassley:

> "I'm concerned that we have a mentality of too-big-to-jail in the financial sector."

## Holder's Response — The Defining Quote

Holder:

> "I am concerned that the size of some of these institutions becomes so large that it does become difficult for us to prosecute them when we are hit with indications that if we do prosecute — if we do bring a criminal charge — it will have a negative impact on the national economy, perhaps even the world economy. I think that is a function of the fact that some of these institutions have become too large."

When Grassley pressed further, Holder added:

> "The concern that you have raised is one that I, frankly, share."

## On Individual Accountability

Holder acknowledged that prosecution of individuals — rather than institutions — would have greater deterrent effect:

> "The greatest deterrent effect is... to prosecute the individuals in the corporations that are responsible for those decisions."

In practice, no senior HSBC executives were prosecuted. No senior executives at any major Wall Street bank were prosecuted for conduct leading to the 2008 financial crisis.

## Context

This testimony came two months after PBS Frontline's "The Untouchables" (January 22, 2013) documented that the Obama DOJ had declined to investigate or prosecute senior Wall Street executives. Holder's testimony effectively confirmed the premise: the institution had decided, as a matter of policy, that criminal prosecution of systemically important banks was too risky to attempt.

## Later Backtracking

Holder and DOJ spokespeople attempted to walk back the "too big to jail" framing in subsequent weeks. Frontline covered the backtracking separately ("Eric Holder Backtracks Remarks on 'Too Big To Jail'"). The walk-back did not change the underlying enforcement pattern: no senior Wall Street executives were criminally prosecuted during the Obama administration for 2008-era conduct.

## Newsletter Relevance

This testimony is the cleanest public statement by a Democratic Attorney General that structural considerations — institution size, economic impact — override individual accountability in prosecution decisions. For any argument that institutional non-accountability is cross-partisan and structural rather than a Trump-era innovation, this testimony is foundational. Grassley's concern about a "too-big-to-jail mentality" spreading from fraud cases to terrorist financing to money laundering is itself an early articulation of what would later be called institutional gaslighting: the use of procedural justifications to prevent accountability across multiple categories of harm.
