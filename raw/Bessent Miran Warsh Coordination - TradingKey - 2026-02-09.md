# Warsh's Fed Era Signals a Death Knell for QE

## Warsh-Bessent alignment
- Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has stated the Fed would not rapidly reduce its balance sheet — a position consistent with Warsh's framework.
- Both Bessent and Warsh have argued that post-2008 QE violated the spirit of the original 1951 Treasury-Fed Accord.
- Warsh's proposed new accord would link Fed balance-sheet size to Treasury debt issuance plans.

## Analyst reactions cited
- Krishna Guha (Evercore ISI): the proposal grants Treasury "veto power over quantitative tightening decisions."
- Tim Duy (SGH Macro): characterizes the framework as "resembling Yield Curve Control, explicitly connecting monetary policy to deficit financing rather than inflation management."
- Deutsche Bank rates desk: projects the Fed could shift holdings toward short-term Treasury bills, with bills' share of the portfolio rising from under 5% to as much as 55%.

## Why it matters for the coordination claim
This piece predates Warsh's confirmation hearing by two months but already documents (a) public alignment between Warsh's stated framework and Bessent's stated preferences, and (b) a concrete operational mechanism — Treasury issuance plans dovetailing with Fed balance-sheet runoff — that would require ongoing coordination between Treasury and Fed leadership.
