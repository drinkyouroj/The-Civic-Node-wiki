# Warsh Fed Chair Confirmation Hearing — Composite Coverage

Hearing of the Senate Banking, Housing, and Urban Affairs Committee, April 21, 2026. Composite of available reporting; full C-SPAN video at https://www.c-span.org/event/senate-committee/pres-trumps-fed-chair-nominee-testifies-at-confirmation-hearing/442207

## Warsh on independence and presidential pressure

> "Fed independence is essential."

> "I will be an independent actor, if confirmed."

When Sen. John Kennedy (R-LA) asked whether he would be the president's "human sock puppet," Warsh replied:

> "Absolutely not."

On private conversations with Trump:

> "The president never once asked me to commit to any particular interest rate decision, period."

> "The president never asked me to pre-determine, commit, fix, or decide on any interest rate decision."

> "...nor would I agree to it if he had."

## Warsh on inflation and the Fed's record

The Fed's inability to return inflation to its 2% target is the result of what he called "policy errors" made in 2021 and 2022. He said the Fed "missed its mark" and the economy "is still dealing with the legacy of policy errors."

> "Inflation is a choice, and the Fed must take responsibility for it."

## Warsh on communications / "regime change"

Warsh did not commit to maintaining the current practice of holding a news conference after every FOMC meeting:

> "I think truth-seeking is more important than repetition."

He would abandon forward guidance — the Fed's signaling of where it wants rates to go — and is open to retiring the dot plot. He prefers decisions made during meetings rather than predetermined via forecasts, to "stop compounding errors."

## Warsh on the balance sheet

Warsh advocates reducing the Fed's $6.7 trillion balance sheet while cutting rates simultaneously. He believes the tools should work "in constant cross purposes." (Columbia professor Yiming Ma, quoted by Fortune, explained the logic: reducing the balance sheet decreases system liquidity, potentially allowing lower rates without additional inflation.)

## Sen. Elizabeth Warren (D-MA), Ranking Member

Warren opened with what reporters called a "blistering attack" of both Warsh and Trump. She called Warsh Trump's

> "sock puppet"

and said he is

> "uniquely ill-suited for the job as Fed Chair."

> "Trump's economic failures are causing him political problems and he wants the Fed to use monetary policy to artificially juice the economy in the short term and this is his last chance to do that before the November election."

> "The President has repeatedly and illegally attempted to take over the Fed."

In Q&A, Warren:
- Pressed Warsh on his finances and asked whether billionaire Stan Druckenmiller might donate funds post-confirmation. Warsh responded he had reached "full agreement with" ethics officials to divest assets — committing to divest roughly $100 million in assets within 90 days if confirmed.
- Asked whether Trump had won the 2020 election.
- Pressed the nominee to name any disagreement with Trump's economic policy.

Sen. John Kennedy (R-LA) followed up on the divestment commitment:

> "If you don't sell them, we'll know"

— and Warsh acknowledged he would be violating his ethics terms otherwise.

## Sen. Thom Tillis (R-NC)

Tillis did not ask Warsh substantive monetary-policy questions. Per CNN's takeaways: he "didn't ask Warsh any questions" and instead used his time to demand the DOJ end its criminal probe of Jerome Powell. He repeatedly called the probe "bogus."

> "Let's get rid of this investigation so I can support your confirmation."

> "We have got to get rid of this investigation."

Tillis told Warsh he otherwise supported him as the next chair. Republicans hold a 12-10 advantage on the Senate Banking Committee, meaning a single Republican dissent would block the nomination at the committee level — making Tillis the decisive vote.

(Three days later, on April 24, 2026, U.S. Attorney Jeanine Pirro announced DOJ was dropping the criminal probe of Powell. On April 26, Tillis said he would end his blockade and support Warsh's confirmation.)

## DOJ probe of Powell — was it raised at the hearing?

Yes — but only by Sen. Tillis, and only as a procedural objection rather than as a substantive question to Warsh. Available wire/live coverage (CNBC, CNN, Yahoo Finance, NPR, Deseret, Fortune, Axios) does not record any other senator pressing Warsh directly on his views about the DOJ criminal investigation of the sitting Fed Chair he is nominated to replace. Warsh himself does not appear to have been asked to give a view on the propriety of the probe.

## Committee dynamics

- Republicans 12, Democrats 10 on Senate Banking.
- Tillis the swing vote at committee.
- Hearing began 10 a.m. EDT, April 21, 2026.
