# Biden Justice Department Slow-Walked Key Decisions in Trump Legal Probes

MSNBC / MS.now, January 6, 2025. Companion reporting to the CNN "Lost Year" investigation. Documents the specific decision points at which Attorney General Merrick Garland's DOJ delayed action on the Trump January 6 and classified documents investigations.

## Core Claim

"The slow decision-making at the top of Attorney General Merrick Garland's Justice Department affected two major probes into Trump after he lost the White House in 2020: whether he illegally possessed and obstructed the retrieval of classified documents at his Mar-a-Lago residence, and whether he conspired illegally to overturn the 2020 election."

## Key Timeline Decisions

### Fall 2022 Freeze

Ahead of the November 2022 midterm elections, Garland opted to freeze both the classified documents investigation and the election interference investigation. This was based on what some DOJ officials described as his "overly cautious reading of a DOJ policy not to take any public action close to an election."

The freeze extended the investigation timeline by months. Critics inside DOJ argued the pre-election policy, which prohibits public investigative actions within 60 days of an election when such actions could affect voter decisions, does not prohibit ongoing behind-the-scenes investigative work.

### FBI Resistance to Opening a Trump Probe

Separately, Washington Post reporting (June 2023) documented that the FBI resisted opening a criminal investigation into Trump's personal role in January 6 for more than a year after the attack. Investigators reportedly feared political backlash and wanted to build bottom-up cases first.

### August 2023 Indictment

Special counsel Jack Smith did not file the January 6 indictment against Trump until August 2023 — **two and a half years after the Capitol attack**. Two former DOJ officials told CNN the charges included in the indictment could have been brought a year earlier.

### July 2024 Supreme Court Immunity Ruling

In July 2024, the Supreme Court held that Trump enjoyed "absolute" immunity from prosecution for actions taken within his core constitutional powers and more limited immunity for other official actions. This ruling effectively ended any realistic path to trial before the 2024 election.

### November 2024 and After

After Trump won the 2024 election, Smith's team dismissed the federal charges "without prejudice" before the inauguration — in theory allowing the case to be revived, in practice ending it.

## Critical Voices from Within DOJ

The article and companion CNN reporting document extensive internal DOJ criticism of Garland's timeline decisions:

> "They wasted time, they were not strategic. It was a whole year of nothing. And they waited so long they ended up helping Trump in the primary and dividing the country." — former DOJ official, CNN

> "I'm just not seeing the urgency from the attorney general. He's thinking more about protecting the institution of the Department of Justice. And I appreciate that, but he has to be thinking about protecting the institution of democracy." — Rep. Ruben Gallego (D-AZ), 2022

## DOJ Defense

Current and former DOJ officials defending the timeline told CNN:
- "The Justice Department does not make investigative steps public. Many times, when people assume nothing is going on, it's because as a general practice we don't make the investigative steps public."
- "It's not our job to influence elections."
