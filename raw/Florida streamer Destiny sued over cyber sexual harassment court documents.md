TAMPA, Fla. (WFLA) — The Florida-based livestreamer and YouTube personality known as “Destiny” was accused of sharing sexual content of a woman without her consent or knowledge.

A federal lawsuit filed in the Southern District of Florida on Tuesday alleged Destiny, whose real name is Steven K. Bonnell II, sent a sexual video to a “random fan,” who then spread them to various websites.

The plaintiff, a Jane Doe identified in the lawsuit only by her influencer username, Pxie, had a sexual encounter with Bonnell in 2020, according to the complaint. The lawsuit said that Pxie was a smaller content creator, with 15,000 followers on X and 10,000 followers on the livestreaming platform Twitch, compared to Bonnell’s 840,000 YouTube subscribers and the over 700,000 followers he accumulated on Twitch before his account was banned. Both Pxie and Destiny create politically-oriented online content.

Bonnell was accused of sending the sexual video to a fan identified as “Rose” in 2022. Two years later, the video appeared on the online message board Kiwi Farms and spread to other sites, whose captions listed the video’s subjects as Pxie and Destiny, and garnered tens of thousands of views, according to the complaint. Attorneys for Pxie claimed the leaked video “went viral” across social media.

The lawsuit alleged Bonnell admitted to sharing the video in a Discord message when confronted by Pxie in November 2024.

“Ughhhh there shouldn’t be no,” Bonnell said when asked if he shared additional videos of Pxie, according to the complaint. “I’m so sorry there’s literally no excuse, I’d had phone convos and stuff with this person they were fairly close to me, it’s worthless to say it at this point but  
I’m super sorry, there’s literally no excuse.”

The plaintiff’s attorneys alleged she received “hundreds of insulting and harassing messages” as a result of the leak and claimed Bonnell’s followers “discussing the leaks on the different accounts falsely claim that Plaintiff exchanged sex for professional growth and favors from Bonnell,” court documents said. The lawsuit claimed Bonnell also made disparaging comments about Pxie, including claims that she was “just after his money.”

The lawsuit laid out additional complaints against Bonnell, including allegations that “approximately fifteen women reached out to Plaintiff to tell her that they had received sexually explicit images from Bonnell of other women without the other women’s consent” in the wake of the video leak. Last month, Bonnell reportedly told his followers that his only crime was being “too much of” a slang term for “someone who is deeply engaged in excessive adult X-rated content consumption, sometimes in a compulsive or obsessive manner,” according to the complaint.

“The Videos disclosed by Bonnell were of a private nature and were never intended  
for public distribution. Defendant’s actions have caused Plaintiff severe emotional distress,  
humiliation, and reputational harm,” the lawsuit alleged. “Plaintiff’s career as a political streamer has been adversely affected and her aspirations of being elected for office have been greatly diminished.”

Attorneys for Pxie alleged Bonnell violated a federal “revenge porn” law and a Florida cyber sexual harassment law. They also claimed Bonnell intentionally inflicted emotional distress upon her and violated her privacy.

The lawsuit called for a jury trial to determine the monetary amount, if any, needed to compensate Pxie, as well as potential injunctive relief against Bonnell, compensation for attorney’s fees, “and further relief to which Plaintiff may be entitled.”

WFLA.com reached out to Bonnell for comment on the lawsuit, but did not immediately hear back.
