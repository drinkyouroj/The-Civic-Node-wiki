Samsung Electronics (005930.KS) narrowly avoided its worst-case scenario by reaching a tentative wage agreement with its union just before a general strike. But the bonus dispute that emerged during the process is now spreading across the auto, shipbuilding, and heavy industry sectors. Within Samsung Electronics' union itself, disparities in bonuses across business divisions have come to the fore, raising the possibility that the tentative agreement could be voted down.

The business community is concerned that the spreading practice of unions demanding a fixed share of operating profit as bonuses could intensify conflicts not only within companies but also between industries, between large and small businesses, and between prime contractors and subcontractors — exacerbating the socioeconomic problem of "K-shaped polarization."

According to labor circles on the 21st, the Kia (000270.KS) union mentioned the Samsung Electronics tentative bonus agreement in its newsletter distributed that day, ramping up pressure in wage negotiations by saying, "Management should learn from Samsung's decisiveness." The Kia union is demanding that 30% of last year's operating profit (9.0781 trillion won) be paid out as bonuses. Meeting the union's demand would require paying out approximately 2.72 trillion won in bonuses — a figure that exceeds Kia's first-quarter operating profit this year (2.2051 trillion won).

The union argued, "Samsung agreed to its wage negotiations without a strike and decided to pay 12% of business performance as a special bonus. In contrast, the Kia union has been waging a struggle for special bonuses, but the company has consistently ignored us."

Demands for higher bonuses are spreading across the industrial sector. While the HD Hyundai Heavy Industries (329180.KS) union has put forward a profit-sharing scheme of at least 30% of operating profit as its core agenda, the Doosan Enerbility (034020.KS) union also recently presented improvements to the bonus payment method (abolition of the cap) as a separate demand at this year's collective bargaining kickoff meeting. Doosan Enerbility pays bonuses based on the achievement rate of operating profit targets, with the current ceiling set at "530% of base salary."

The Doosan Enerbility union emphasized, "Even in years of outstanding performance over the past few years, the cap has remained fixed, accumulating frustration among union members." A union official explained, "This demand is not directly linked to the Samsung Electronics issue," but added, "There is an aspect where the social atmosphere has intersected with our cause, making members' demands stronger." In response, management countered with a proposal to abolish the bonus floor — which currently guarantees a base 100% even when the operating profit target achievement rate is below 50% — countering with the logic that "if we are to revise the cap, we should also eliminate the floor."

In addition, the Hyundai Motor (005380.KS) union is demanding a base salary increase along with bonuses equal to 30% of last year's net profit, while HD Hyundai Electric (267260.KS) and Hanwha Aerospace (012450.KS) are also facing demands to overhaul their bonus calculation methods and abolish the cap system.

As bonus demands spread across the industrial sector, the business community's concerns are deepening. The reasoning is that excessive bonus demands based solely on current market conditions not only constrain companies' capacity for future investment but could also intensify "K-shaped polarization."

The Korea Enterprises Federation stressed, "Since this Samsung Electronics agreement is the result of reflecting a special situation, excessive bonus demands should not be spread across the entire industry." Professor Suh Yong-gu of Sookmyung Women's University also pointed out that it is a mistake to try to apply the exceptional boom of the artificial intelligence (AI) semiconductor super-cycle as it is to other industries that experience significant ups and downs.

In particular, with the implementation of the Yellow Envelope Act, demands for bonus increases are spreading even to subcontractor unions, prompting numerous warnings that income gaps among workers, relative deprivation, and declining work motivation could escalate into serious social problems.
