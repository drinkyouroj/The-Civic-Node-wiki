POWER Magazine reporting on Microsoft's January 13, 2026 announcement of a four-point framework committing to ensure data center electricity costs do not increase residential utility rates. First major hyperscaler to make this comprehensive commitment.

Microsoft's four-point framework:

1. **Cost-Recovery Rate Design** — request utilities and state commissions implement rates covering electricity costs and dedicated infrastructure, preventing cost-shifting to residential customers. Wisconsin (We Energies) and Wyoming (Black Hills Energy) cited as models.
2. **Utility Coordination & Direct Investment** — collaborate transparently with utilities on generation, transmission, and substation capacity planning; directly fund grid upgrades driven by Microsoft projects.
3. **Efficiency Improvements** — Project Forge scheduler technology that boosts GPU utilization from 50-60% to 80-90%, "safely harvesting" approximately 800 MW of unused power from existing data centers since 2019.
4. **Policy Advocacy** — pursue federal and state policies accelerating project permitting, electricity interconnection, grid expansion, and large-user rate structures.

Capacity figures:
- Microsoft contracted for 7.9 GW of new electricity supply in the MISO footprint (more than double its current MISO consumption)
- Project Forge: 800 MW of unused power "safely harvested" since 2019

Regulatory context:
- FERC ordered PJM (December 18, 2025) to overhaul rules for co-located and behind-the-meter large loads
- January 15, 2026: All 13 PJM regional state governors signed a Statement of Principles directing data centers to "cover their share of the costs of any new resources"
- US electricity transmission infrastructure exceeds 40 years old in many regions; new transmission projects require 7-10 years due to permitting and siting delays

Adjacent data — Amazon-commissioned analysis (December 2025): data centers generating approximately $33,500/MW in surplus value in 2025 (~$3.4 million per typical 100-MW facility).

Direct quotes — Brad Smith, Microsoft Vice Chair and President:
- "This revives a longstanding question: how can our nation build transformative infrastructure?"
- "We believe the long-term success of AI infrastructure requires tech companies pay their own way."

Microsoft commitment language summarized:
- Request utilities establish rates "high enough to cover the electricity costs" for data centers
- Collaborate "early, closely, and transparently" with utilities for grid planning
