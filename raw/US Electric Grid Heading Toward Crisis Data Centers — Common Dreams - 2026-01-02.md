# US Electric Grid Heading Toward 'Crisis' Thanks to AI Data Centers

## Key figures

- **PJM Interconnection** (serves 65+ million across 13 states): projected 6-gigawatt shortage by 2027
- **Austin, Texas**: some proposed data centers seeking 5+ GW — exceeding the city's entire peak load
- **New York State**: estimated 1.6-gigawatt grid shortage by 2030, largely due to data centers
- **Maryland**: residents projected to pay $1.6 billion more in power bills due to out-of-state data centers

## Expert quotes

- Joe Bowring (Monitoring Analytics, PJM's independent market monitor): "It's at a crisis stage right now. PJM has never been this short."
- Rob Gramlich (Grid Strategies): Predicted intensifying political backlash as utility bills rise, with 2026 elections amplifying affordability messaging.

## Community concerns

Palm Beach County residents cited noise from cooling towers, water consumption, pollution risks, and higher utility costs when successfully postponing a 200-acre data center project.

Corey Kanterman (local opponent): "Put them in the location of least impact to the environment and people."

## Political framing

Opposition spans ideological lines — from Senator Bernie Sanders (left) to Governor Ron DeSantis (right) — reflecting bipartisan concern over grid reliability and ratepayer impact.
