Sen. Elizabeth Warren's opening remarks at Warsh's confirmation hearing, April 21, 2026.

Key points:
- Opened by saying "We should not be having this hearing today," citing Trump's economic record (consumer sentiment, inflation).
- Argued Trump has "repeatedly and illegally attempted to take over the Fed," and a Trump-controlled Fed could facilitate corruption and self-enrichment.
- On Warsh's record as Fed governor (2006-2011): noted he expressed "no regrets" about his actions during the financial crisis despite mass job and home losses.
- Documented what she characterized as Warsh's flip-flops on interest rates depending on Trump's political circumstances; he "sucked up to Trump to snag his dream job."
- Urged the committee to postpone the hearing and conduct oversight into Trump's alleged direction of criminal investigations into Fed officials.
- Called confirmation "aiding and abetting Donald Trump's illegal takeover of the Fed."
- Called Warsh "uniquely ill-suited for the job as Fed Chair."

Strategy implication: Warren is positioning for maximum public opposition rather than procedural blockade. Democrats lack the votes to stop Warsh; her approach is to make the vote politically costly and brand the Fed as captured.
