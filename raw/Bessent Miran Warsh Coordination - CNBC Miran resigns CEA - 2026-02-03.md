# Stephen Miran resigns from White House CEA, retains Fed seat

## What happened
On Feb 3, 2026, Stephen Miran resigned as Chair of the White House Council of Economic Advisers to focus full-time on his Federal Reserve Governor role. This followed through on a Senate commitment made during his September 2025 confirmation, when his term-limited initial Fed term was set to expire Jan 31, 2026 (he was reappointed to a full term).

## Background — the unpaid leave arrangement
- Miran was confirmed to the Fed in September 2025 while keeping his White House CEA role on unpaid leave — an unprecedented arrangement.
- During the unpaid leave: no White House email, no White House badge, "would not provide any advisory guidance as part of CEA in any way."
- Critics (Senator Warren and others) warned the unpaid-leave structure could give the president undue influence at the Fed.

## Voting record at the Fed
- Miran has dissented at every FOMC meeting he has attended — first three for a 50bp cut, last two for a 25bp cut. Five consecutive dissents through March 2026.
- Sole dissenter in the March 2026 11–1 vote.

## Why this matters for the coordination claim
The unpaid-leave structure (Sept 2025–Feb 2026) is the closest thing to documented institutional coordination among the three figures: a single person simultaneously holding a White House economic-policy role and a Fed Governor seat. The arrangement was eventually unwound under pressure, but its existence is direct, on-the-record evidence that the firewall between the Trump economic team and the Fed was deliberately bridged for at least five months.
