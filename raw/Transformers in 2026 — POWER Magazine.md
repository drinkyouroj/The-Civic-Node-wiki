POWER Magazine analysis of the US transformer supply chain crisis. Wood Mackenzie's August 2025 analysis projects a 30% supply deficit for power transformers and a 10% deficit for distribution units in 2025. Power-transformer demand has grown 119% since 2019; generator step-up (GSU) transformer demand has surged 274% over the same period.

Lead times (Q2 2025 survey):
- Large power transformers: averaging 128 weeks
- Generator step-up (GSU) units: 144 weeks
- Distribution transformers: improving but elevated
- Substation transformers: stretched from ~140 weeks (2023) to 160+ weeks (2026)

Unit prices: power transformers up 77% since 2019; GSUs up 45% over the same period.

Manufacturing buildout: ~$1.8 billion in announced North American transformer manufacturing expansions through 2028. Hitachi Energy leading at $1+ billion in continental investments; Siemens Energy ($150M Charlotte, NC plant); Eaton ($340M South Carolina facility); Prolec GE ($300M+ expansion). Other manufacturers cited: Virginia Transformer Corp., WEG, ERMCO, Central Moloney, MGM Transformer, HD Hyundai Electric.

Infrastructure aging: over 55% of US distribution transformers exceed 33 years old; approximately 40 million units beyond expected service life.

Competing demand drivers: data center construction; manufacturing facility buildout (96% increase over three years); renewable energy pipeline; aging infrastructure replacement.

Wood Mackenzie 9,000-units-by-2030 projection: annual transformer demand could exceed 9,000 units by 2030, up from roughly 1,500 today. Data centers projected as 40% of total US electrical equipment demand by 2030, up from a historically low single-digit share.

Direct quotes:
- Wood Mackenzie analysis summary: "Supply shortage persists as demand growth remains robust."
- Wood Mackenzie conclusion: "Extended lead times and elevated costs will become the new normal."
- Patrick Tarver, Owner, Bolt Electrical LLC, on standard substation transformers: "There is not a shortage."

Tarver's contrarian view: bottleneck lies beyond factory output and more in the structure of utility and EPC procurement — vendor lists, qualification rules, internal hierarchies. The article notes difficulty validating Tarver's claim without "broader engagement from manufacturers and buyers" given customization, regulation, and domestic-content complexity of large power transformers.

Trade policy caveat: tariffs up to 50% on copper; expanded Section 232 duties; mixed signals may inflate costs for both imported and domestically produced equipment, potentially offsetting manufacturing expansion benefits.
