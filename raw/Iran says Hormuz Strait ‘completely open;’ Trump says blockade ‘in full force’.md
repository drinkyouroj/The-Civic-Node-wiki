Iran announced that the critical global shipping lane known as the [Strait of Hormuz](https://thehill.com/policy/defense/5835612-strait-hormuz-trade-blockade/) is “completely” open after being mostly closed since the war [between the U.S. and Iran](https://thehill.com/policy/international/5833458-us-israel-iran-fragile-ceasefire/) broke out earlier this year.

“In line with the ceasefire in Lebanon, the passage for all commercial vessels through Strait of Hormuz is declared completely open for the remaining period of ceasefire, on the coordinated route as already announced by Ports and Maritime Organisation of the Islamic Rep. of Iran,” Iran’s Foreign Minister Abbas Arahgchi [wrote Friday](https://x.com/araghchi/status/2045121573124759713?s=46) in a post on social platform X.

[President Trump](https://thehill.com/people/donald-trump/) reacted enthusiastically to Iran’s announcement, but said the U.S. blockade remains in place.

“IRAN HAS JUST ANNOUNCED THAT THE STRAIT OF IRAN IS FULLY OPEN AND READY FOR FULL PASSAGE. THANK YOU,” Trump [posted](https://truthsocial.com/@realDonaldTrump/posts/116420194853200133) on Truth Social.

In a subsequent post, the president said the U.S. [blockade on the strait](https://thehill.com/policy/defense/5828980-trump-blockade-strait-hormuz/) will remain in “full force” until a long-term deal is reached between Tehran and Washington.

“THE STRAIT OF HORMUZ IS COMPLETELY OPEN AND READY FOR BUSINESS AND FULL PASSAGE, BUT THE NAVAL BLOCKADE WILL REMAIN IN FULL FORCE AND EFFECT AS IT PERTAINS TO IRAN, ONLY, UNTIL SUCH TIME AS OUR TRANSACTION WITH IRAN IS 100% COMPLETE. THIS PROCESS SHOULD GO VERY QUICKLY IN THAT MOST OF THE POINTS ARE ALREADY NEGOTIATED,” he wrote.

The news comes as the 10-day [Israel-Lebanon ceasefire](https://thehill.com/policy/international/5834520-israel-lebanon-10-day-ceasefire-trump-iran-conflict/) takes hold, potentially lifting a major hurdle to a peace deal with Iran.

Financial markets rallied in response to the news, with the Dow Jones Industrial Average, S&P 500, and the Nasdaq all showing gains.

The closure of the strait also spiked global fuel prices. On Friday, Brent crude, the global benchmark, decreased to $89, while the U.S benchmark West Texas Intermediate dropped to $81.

In another TruthSocial post, Trump claimed that Iran has agreed to hand over its “nuclear dust,” an apparent reference to its enriched uranium. Trump said that Israel would not be bombing Lebanon going forward.

“The U.S.A. will get all Nuclear “Dust,” created by our great B2 Bombers – No money will exchange hands in any way, shape, or form. This deal is in no way subject to Lebanon, either, but the USA will, separately, work with Lebanon, and deal with the Hezboolah situation in an appropriate manner. Israel will not be bombing Lebanon any longer,” Trump wrote.

The developments come after Trump announced Israel and Lebanon agreed to a 10-day ceasefire after meeting in Washington. The development could help clear a major hurdle in talks between the U.S. and Iran.

Israel has launched a barrage of attacks on Lebanon since the beginning of the U.S.-Israeli war in Iran, targeting the Iranian-backed Lebanese militia group Hezbollah. Iran and Pakistan said Lebanon was part of the initial ceasefire, but the U.S. and Israel argued it was not.

A first round of talks in Islamabad last week did not make headway toward a deal, according to Vice President Vance, who led the U.S. delegation.

The Pakistani Foreign Ministry said Thursday that Washington and Tehran are in touch through Pakistan about setting up a second round of talks, but said no date had been set.

Trump told reporters on Thursday that he may travel to Islamabad to seal a deal with Iran, if one is reached.

In Truth Social posts on Friday morning, Trump said he received a call from the North Atlantic Treaty Organization (NATO), asking if the U.S. needed help after Iran’s announcement the Hormuz Strait if open.

“I TOLD THEM TO STAY AWAY, UNLESS THEY JUST WANT TO LOAD UP THEIR SHIPS WITH OIL. They were useless when needed, a Paper Tiger!” Trump wrote.

The president praised the U.S. Gulf allies in another post, thanking Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates, and Qatar for their “bravery and help.”
