Jeff Bezos, speaking at Italian Tech Week in Turin on October 3, 2025, described the current AI investment cycle as "a kind of industrial bubble, as opposed to financial bubbles." The remark distinguished AI infrastructure spending from purely speculative financial instruments: the assets being built — data centers, power plants, transmission infrastructure — are real physical capital, even if the pace of investment has bubble-like characteristics.

Bezos said he remains bullish on AI despite the bubble framing, reasoning that industrial bubbles leave behind durable physical infrastructure even when speculative excess burns off. He pointed to the railroad bubble of the 19th century as a historical analogue: capital destruction at the investment level coexisted with lasting productive capacity.

Sam Altman, also present at Italian Tech Week, made parallel remarks defending continued AI capital commitment amid concerns about demand sustainability.

The "industrial bubble" framing is analytically significant because it reframes the constraint: financial bubbles deflate when confidence collapses; industrial bubbles stall when physical inputs run short. Bezos's own framing implies the binding variable is not investor sentiment but delivery capacity — transformers, substations, interconnection queue clearance.

## Key Quote

> "This is a kind of industrial bubble, as opposed to financial bubbles."

— Jeff Bezos, Italian Tech Week, Turin, October 3, 2025
