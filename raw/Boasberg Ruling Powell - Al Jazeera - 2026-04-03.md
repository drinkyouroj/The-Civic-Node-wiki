On April 3, 2026, Judge Boasberg rejected the Trump administration's motion to reconsider his March 13 ruling that invalidated the Powell subpoenas. Six-page opinion.

## Key quotes from the April 3 opinion
> "The Government's arguments do not come close to convincing the Court that a different outcome is warranted."

> "The subpoena power 'is not unlimited' and may not be abused."

> "what these 'subpoena[s'] dominant purpose' is: pressuring Powell to lower rates or resign, or pursuing a legitimate investigation."

> "The Government's fundamental problem is that it has presented no evidence whatsoever of fraud."

## Appeal posture
DOJ (Pirro) signaled intent to appeal to the D.C. Circuit. As of late April, the appeal had not been withdrawn — Tillis's April 26 statement specifically addressed this: "any appeal of Judge Boasberg's ruling will be with respect to legal principles and not for the purpose of reissuing subpoenas." This indicates the appeal is ongoing as a legal-principle matter (likely arguing about scope of judicial power to quash grand jury subpoenas) but not as a vehicle to reanimate the Powell probe.

## What's at stake doctrinally
Whether a district court can quash a grand jury subpoena based on a finding that the dominant purpose is harassment rather than legitimate investigation — Pirro framed this as judicial overreach into the grand jury process. The doctrine matters far beyond Powell.
