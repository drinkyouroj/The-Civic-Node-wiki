# More Than a Million Epstein-Related Documents Discovered; Release Delayed

Al Jazeera News, December 24, 2025.

## Lead

The US Department of Justice announced on December 24, 2025 — five days after the statutory deadline — that over one million additional documents potentially related to Jeffrey Epstein had been discovered. The DOJ stated it needed "a few more weeks" to process these files before releasing them under the Epstein Files Transparency Act, which Congress passed nearly unanimously in November.

## Source of the Discovery

The US Attorney for the Southern District of New York and the FBI informed the DOJ of the document discovery. The DOJ emphasized that lawyers were "working around the clock" to review materials and complete required redactions to protect victim identities.

The announcement came after the DOJ had already missed the December 19 statutory deadline for releasing all unclassified Epstein documents.

## DOJ Framing

Deputy Attorney General **Todd Blanche** told NBC's *Meet the Press* on Sunday (December 21, 2025) that the review continues "simply to protect victims," adding that critics demanding faster release "apparently don't want us to protect victims."

This framing — that delay is protective — became the DOJ's standard response to criticism of the missed deadline.

## Congressional Response

**Twelve senators — Republican Lisa Murkowski of Alaska and eleven Democrats** — sent a letter on Wednesday, December 24, 2025, to the DOJ Inspector General. The letter stated that victims "deserve full disclosure" and requested "an independent audit of DOJ compliance with the transparency law."

**Republican Representative Thomas Massie (R-KY)**, one of the chief Republican sponsors of the Epstein Files Transparency Act, posted publicly on X:

> "DOJ did break the law by making illegal redactions and by missing the deadline."

Massie's statement is the most significant public acknowledgment from a Republican lawmaker that the DOJ under Trump failed to comply with the law. The statement undercuts any framing of the non-compliance as partisan grievance: the law's Republican author publicly confirmed the violation.

## Release Timeline

- December 19: approximately 3,965 files / 3 GB released on the statutory deadline day.
- December 20–23: additional batches released over the weekend and Tuesday.
- December 24: announcement of 1 million+ newly discovered documents; "few more weeks" timeline.
- No hard timeline announced for further releases.
