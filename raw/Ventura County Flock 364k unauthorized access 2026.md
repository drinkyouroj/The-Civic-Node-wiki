# Flock License Plate Readers Shared Data with Out-of-State Agencies, Ventura County Audit Finds

**By Matthew Rodriguez, CBS Los Angeles**
**Published: February 27, 2026**

An audit of Flock Safety license plate readers revealed that the company inadvertently shared Ventura County's data with out-of-state law enforcement agencies, including federal agents, without departmental approval or knowledge.

## Key Audit Findings

Out-of-state agencies accessed Ventura County Sheriff's Office data more than **364,000 times** between February and March 2025 without departmental approval or knowledge.

Of those accesses, **299 justifications referenced immigration enforcement** — less than 1% of total searches.

## The National Lookup Reactivation

The Ventura County Sheriff's Office had disabled Flock's "National Lookup" feature in June 2023 to comply with California law prohibiting local agencies from sharing ALPR data with out-of-state and federal law enforcement.

In early February 2025, deputies discovered the feature had been reactivated without explanation.

**Flock Safety offered three possible explanations:**
1. A Sheriff's Office employee reactivated it
2. A Flock employee activated it
3. A system bug automatically activated it

The Sheriff's Office conducted an internal investigation and **"determined that no one from our agency activated the national lookup feature."**

Flock stated: "due to limitations in technical logging, it was impossible to determine the specific cause."

## Response and Remedies

**Sheriff's Office:**
- Implemented daily independent audits
- Began evaluating alternative vendors
- Emphasized ALPR "remains a powerful and critical tool in combating crime"

**Flock's subsequent actions:**
- Blocked federal agencies from data sharing with California law enforcement
- Verified no California agencies can share data with out-of-state law enforcement
- Initiated features automatically blocking immigration-related search justifications
- Maintained: "does not have any contracts with ICE or any sub-agency of the Department of Homeland Security"

**Oxnard Police Chief Jason Benites:** "Proper guardrails must not only be in place, but they must work reliably."

Nearly all Ventura County law enforcement agencies took similar suspension measures.

## Legal Action

An Oakland-based firm (Gibbs Mura) filed a class-action lawsuit against Flock, claiming violations of California law.
