Prominent crypto attorney James Murphy, known professionally as MetaLawMan, has filed a Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) lawsuit against the US Department of Homeland Security seeking documents related to Bitcoin's anonymous creator, Satoshi Nakamoto.

According to the lawsuit, a DHS official made a striking statement during a 2019 financial intelligence conference, claiming the agency had "discovered Nakamoto's identity and interviewed him face-to-face in California." The official reportedly noted that three other individuals involved in Bitcoin's development were present at this alleged meeting. The DHS special agent who made the claim was identified as Rana Saoud, speaking at the OffshoreAlert Conference in Miami.

Murphy's lawsuit demands access to internal DHS documents, emails, and notes that might confirm whether such an interview occurred. He argued that any genuine encounter would necessarily have generated official paperwork and records. Additionally, Murphy called on DHS Secretary Christy Noem to voluntarily release the information, warning he is prepared to continue litigation if the department refuses disclosure.

Murphy noted: "It is entirely possible that the DHS Agent was mistaken and DHS did not interview the real Satoshi." He believes that the identity of Satoshi should be made known because other cryptocurrencies are clear about who made them.

The article also notes that recent Satoshi speculation (prior to April 2026) had centered on developer Peter Todd (HBO, 2024) and Twitter founder Jack Dorsey, though both have denied involvement. Many in the cryptocurrency community believe maintaining Nakamoto's anonymity strengthens Bitcoin's decentralized nature.
