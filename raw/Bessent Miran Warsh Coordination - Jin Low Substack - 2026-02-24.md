# The Warsh Proposal: Fed-Treasury Coordination as Monetary Regime Shift

## Core framework
Warsh proposes two main commitments:
1. Explicit forward-looking Fed guidance on balance-sheet trajectory.
2. Treasury alignment of debt issuance timing with Federal Reserve liquidity operations.

## Context (as of Feb 2026)
- Fed balance sheet: $6.6 trillion
- Federal net interest costs Q1 FY2026: $270.3 billion (roughly $1.08T annualized on $38.56T total debt)
- Bessent has publicly stated the Fed will remain cautious on balance-sheet reduction — alignment with Warsh's frame.

## The 1951 contrast
The 1951 Treasury-Fed Accord "freed the Fed from yield targeting obligations, establishing the foundation for modern central bank independence." Warsh's proposal explicitly proposes coordination rather than separation — a partial rollback of 1951.

## Coordination question
Is this technical transparency, or "functional yield curve control operating under a different framework — essentially returning to Treasury-directed monetary policy through the back door"? The author leaves the question open.
