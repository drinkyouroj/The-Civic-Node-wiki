# Michigan Data Centers Could Hike Your Power Bill

*Planet Detroit — October 16, 2025 — Brian Allnutt*

Data center developments threaten significant electricity rate increases for Michigan residential customers unless regulators implement protections quickly, according to consumer advocates and energy experts.

## Projected rate impacts
- Energy consultant **Douglas Jester**: "a single 1-gigawatt data center could increase residential rates by **5%-10%** if protections aren't added." (1 GW ≈ twice Pittsburgh's annual energy consumption.)
- **Carnegie Mellon University** estimate: electricity bills could rise **8% by 2030** due to data centers, with some regions facing increases as high as **25%**.
- Mid-Atlantic region already saw **70% ($9.3 billion)** of last year's electricity price increase driven by data center demand (Monitoring Analytics).

## Michigan's data center pipeline
- **Consumers Energy:** considering **15 GW** of data center projects; signed first **1 GW** hyperscale facility deal (details undisclosed).
- **DTE Energy:** **7 GW** in pipeline.
- For comparison, Consumers' 2022 peak demand was **7.5 GW**; DTE's was **10.2 GW** (same day).

## Cost-allocation mechanism
Current utility frameworks divide new-generation costs **proportionally among all customer classes**. If a new power plant costs 30¢/kWh while existing generation costs 10¢/kWh, residential customers share the premium despite not benefiting from data center operations.

Jester: data centers "need to bring with them or pay for enough generating capacity to serve their additional demand to not drive up the cost for everybody else."

Intervenor filings: the "probable prospects" category alone (2.65 GW) could trigger **$8.1 billion** in generation costs.

## Consumers Energy's proposed protections (MPSC tariff case, filed February)
- 15-year minimum contract terms
- Exit fees for early service termination
- 80% minimum demand requirement (charging for at least 80% of forecasted needs regardless of actual usage)
- Stated purpose: "protect customers from potential stranded costs associated with data center loads."

## Advocacy objections
Michigan Environmental Council, NRDC, Sierra Club, Citizens Utility Board of Michigan: 15-year term is insufficient (utility infrastructure amortizes over 30+ years); service agreements should include benchmarks for Michigan's 100% clean-energy-by-2040 requirement.

Chris Gilmer-Hill (Michigan Environmental Justice Coalition): "You're raising rates all for a product that is not actually helping you."

## Grid and burden context
- 10-year queue for renewable projects connecting to the grid; ~7-year gas-turbine procurement waits (partly data-center-driven, per S&P Global).
- Insufficient generation could trigger "off-ramp" provisions keeping fossil plants running despite Michigan's clean-energy goals.
- Nearly half of Michigan census tracts spend 10% of income on energy; some Wayne County areas face 36% burdens (DOE energy-burdened threshold is 6%).
- The MPSC's decision on the Consumers tariff case could set statewide precedent for data center rate protections.
