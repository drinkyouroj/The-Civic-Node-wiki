[SK Hynix Inc](https://archive.ph/o/mfxoX/https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/000660:KS).’s labor union approved a landmark agreement that could see each employee on average receiving about $80,000 bonus for 2025 alone, averting a strike and potentially setting a precedent for South Korea’s tech industry.

The labor union on Thursday voted to accept the deal, which allocates 10% of the company’s annual operating profit to a bonus pool for its 33,625 employees — a key demand from the union. Based on analysts’ projections for SK Hynix’s profit this year, the agreement — which also includes a 6% wage increase — is expected to cost the memory maker about 3.8 trillion won ($2.7 billion). That’s about $80,000 per employee on average, based on Bloomberg’s calculations, though individual payouts will vary based on employee tenure and seniority.

SK Hynix is expected to post a 60% surge in operating income this year, turbocharged by booming demand for high-bandwidth memory used in artificial intelligence systems. This new compensation model — which will last for 10 years — sets a precedent at SK Hynix and could influence labor practices at other Korean companies.

It has also put pressure on rival [Samsung Electronics Co.](https://archive.ph/o/mfxoX/https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/005930:KS), which doesn’t tie bonuses to operating income. Soon after the SK Hynix deal was announced, unions from five Samsung affiliates urged Executive Chairman Jay Y. Lee to improve Samsung’s bonus system.

While Samsung workers typically receive yearly incentives and discretionary performance pay, their labor unions have demanded greater transparency over the formula for determining payouts.

The news of the large payout at SK Hynix has stirred up a range of emotions in the country. On anonymous employee forums like Blind, overjoyed SK Hynix workers are celebrating the outcome, praising SK Group Chairman Chey Tae-won for his bold decision and pledging their loyalty to the company. Meanwhile, workers outside of SK Hynix have reacted mostly with envy.

SK Hynix’s agreement abolished a previous cap that limited bonuses to 1,000% of an employee’s base salary, a source of past conflict. During negotiations, management had proposed raising the cap to 1,700%. Under the new agreement, 80% of the total 2025 bonus will be paid early next year, with the remaining 20% distributed over the subsequent two years.
