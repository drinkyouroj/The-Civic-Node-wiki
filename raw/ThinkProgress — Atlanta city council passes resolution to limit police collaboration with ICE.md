# Atlanta city council passes resolution to limit police collaboration with ICE

**Author:** Esther Yu Hsi Lee | **Published:** September 6, 2017 (Updated September 11, 2017) | **Source:** ThinkProgress

## Vote Date

Tuesday night (early September 2017 — text references vote happened "days after the Trump administration ended DACA protections" on September 5, 2017, and "one week following Illinois's similar Trust Act signing").

## Key Components

The resolution requires that "the Fulton County Sheriff's Office won't detain individuals for ICE unless the federal immigration agency presents a judicially-issued warrant."

Additional provisions:
- Preventing police from arresting, detaining, or transporting individuals based on immigration detainers
- Prohibiting detention beyond what criminal charges allow
- Requiring ICE notification to inmates' attorneys

## Context

Atlanta became the sixth Georgia locality adopting such a non-detainer policy.

## Quotes

> "Local police entanglement in immigration enforcement makes our communities less safe." — Azadeh Shahshahani, legal/advocacy director, Project South

## Notes

ThinkProgress was a credible secondary news outlet (defunct as of 2018; archives still online). This article is the clearest documentation of the 2017 Atlanta resolution language, including the specific "judicially-issued warrant" requirement that distinguishes Atlanta's policy from a broader sanctuary ordinance.
