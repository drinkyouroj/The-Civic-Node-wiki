# Fed chief nominee Warsh commits to central bank's independence, with limits

Kevin Warsh, Trump's nominee to lead the Federal Reserve, will testify before the Senate Banking Committee that he supports monetary policy independence with significant limitations.

## Key quotes from his prepared remarks

On monetary policy independence:

> "I am committed to ensuring that the conduct of monetary policy remains strictly independent."

On areas outside independence protection:

> "That degree of independence does not extend to the full range of its congressionally mandated functions."

Warsh specified that central bank policymakers should not receive "special deference" in their management of public funds, bank regulation and supervision, and international finance matters.

## Additional context

Warsh stated that Fed independence exists "at its peak in the operational conduct of monetary policy" but acknowledged the agency handles many congressionally assigned responsibilities beyond this core function. He emphasized that the central bank must "stay in its lane" and avoid straying into fiscal and social policy areas.

(Cross-confirmed via SRN News mirror of the same Reuters wire, which adds:)

> "I am equally committed to working with the Administration and Congress on non-monetary matters."

The Fed is not entitled to the same "special deference" in bank regulation, supervision, stewardship of public monies, or "areas affecting international finance, among other matters."

> "In a time that will rank among the most consequential in our nation's history, I believe a reform-oriented Federal Reserve can make a real difference."

> "Inflation is a choice, and the Fed must take responsibility for it."

His confirmation hearing was scheduled to begin at 10 a.m. EDT on Tuesday before the Senate Banking Committee.
