# Oracle's Project Jupiter Ditches Gas Turbines for Bloom Fuel Cells

## Project overview

Oracle's Project Jupiter AI data center campus in Doña Ana County, New Mexico, will span approximately 1,400 acres and include four hyperscale buildings.

- Long-term investment projected at $165 billion
- ~4,000 construction jobs
- ~1,500 permanent positions

## Power generation shift

The facility pivoted from its original design — gas turbines and diesel generators — to a fuel-cell-based microgrid. The revised architecture consolidates the campus into a single microgrid capable of supporting up to 2.45 GW of on-site capacity.

**Bloom Energy partnership:**
- Up to 2.8 GW of fuel cell capacity total
- 1.2 GW already contracted

## Environmental specifics

- Operates without combustion; minimal water use
- NOx emissions reduced ~92% versus conventional generation methods
- Paired with closed-loop, non-evaporative cooling systems

## Industry perspectives quoted

- Rob Gramlich (Grid Strategies, president): "Large data center operators still generally prefer grid power and use on-site generation primarily as backup." Emphasized that grid constraints drive adoption of microgrids for high-density workloads.
- Alex Cordovil (Dell'Oro Group, research director): observed rack density remains the primary factor influencing extreme architectural integration across data center systems.
- Prithpal Khajuria (Intel, segment leader for energy and utilities): "Fuel cells have very limited capacity to handle overloading" and operate optimally at steady power consumption rates.
