Latitude Media reporting on Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory (LBNL) interconnection-queue analysis. Article published April 11, 2024; updated March 6, 2025.

Headline finding: 2,600 GW of total capacity sits in US interconnection queues — more than double the 1,279 GW of installed US capacity (as of 2024).

Queue composition (from LBNL):
- Nearly 11,600 active projects seeking interconnection
- 1,570 GW generation capacity
- 1,030 GW storage capacity
- 95% of queuing generation: solar, storage, wind
- 3% of queuing generation: gas
- Solar and storage combined: >80% of the queue

Wait time evolution (renewable projects completed):
- 2008: under 2 years
- 2015: 3 years
- 2023: 5 years

Completion rates (projects requesting interconnection 2000-2018):
- Overall: only 20% operational by end of 2023
- Solar: 14% completion rate
- Battery: 11% completion rate

Project size growth (2015 → 2023):
- Mean solar plants: 250% larger
- Standalone batteries: 330% larger
- Wind: 66% larger

2023 withdrawals:
- Over 1,250 requests representing 200+ GW withdrew in 2023
- ~70% of all projects ultimately withdraw during the wait

Cost data: interconnection costs in 2019-2023 were 44% greater than during the preceding 5 years.

Quotes from the underlying LBNL report (per Latitude Media):
- "Nearly 11,600 projects representing 1,570 gigawatts of generator capacity"
- "Completion rates are even lower for solar (14%) and battery (11%)"
- "Interconnection costs ... were 44% greater than during the preceding 5 years"

Source/methodology: Lawrence Berkeley National Lab "Queued Up" annual report, interpreted for trade press. The LBNL "Queued Up: 2025 Edition" (Dec 2025) extends this dataset and is the primary citable document.
