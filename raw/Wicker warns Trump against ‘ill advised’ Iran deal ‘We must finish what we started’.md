Senate Armed Services Committee Chair [Roger Wicker](https://thehill.com/people/roger-wicker/) (R-Miss.) on Friday called for renewed strikes on Iran, pressing President Trump to “finish the job he started” and warning him of an “ill advised” deal with Tehran.

Wicker advocates for Trump to allow U.S. forces to fully destroy Tehran’s military capabilities and reopen the Strait of Hormuz, writing in a post on the [social platform X](https://x.com/SenatorWicker/status/2057865140402299375) that “it is past time for action.”

“We are at a moment that will define President Trump’s legacy. His instincts have been to finish the job he started in Iran, but he is being ill advised to pursue a deal that would not be worth the paper it is written on,” Wicker writes. “Our commander-in-chief needs to allow America’s skilled armed forces to finish the destruction of Iran’s conventional military capabilities and reopen the strait.”

He added that further pursuit of an agreement with Iran would risk “a perception of weakness.”

Trump has repeatedly threatened to resume attacks on Iran since a ceasefire was called in April, only to repeatedly call them off. In the latest such episode, the president said Monday he nixed a scheduled attack on Tehran that was set for Tuesday.

Trump also has repeatedly rejected Tehran’s peace proposals but on Wednesday said he’s willing to wait for a limited time for Iran’s latest response in negotiations between Washington and Tehran.

“If I can save war by waiting a couple of days, if I can save people being killed by waiting a couple of days, I think it’s a great thing to do,” the president told reporters.

Some on the right, however, believe Iran is simply stalling and that they’re not serious about making any concessions, including potentially giving up its enriched uranium and control over the Strait of Hormuz.

“Well, I think it’s a waste of oxygen to negotiate with the Iranians,” former White House national security adviser John Bolton told Bloomberg [on Thursday](https://thehill.com/policy/defense/5890675-bolton-calls-end-iran-ceasefire/). “I don’t think they’re ever going to come up with something that we should find satisfactory.”

He also claimed the ceasefire “has done nothing but benefit Iran” insofar as it has allowed the country to restart the production of drones and dig “out a lot of arsenals that had been buried in the first wave of attacks.”

The closure of the Strait of Hormuz, a vital shipping way, has caused inflation and energy prices to spike, and they will likely continue to rise if a deal is not reached.
