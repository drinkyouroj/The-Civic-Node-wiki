# Claims Denials and Appeals in ACA Marketplace Plans in 2024

Kaiser Family Foundation (KFF) analysis of claims denial and appeals data reported by HealthCare.gov insurers for plan year 2024. KFF's annual analysis of this data is the most comprehensive public view of private health insurance claim denial patterns in the United States.

## Headline Finding

HealthCare.gov insurers denied approximately **19% of in-network claims** and **37% of out-of-network claims** in 2024, for a combined average of **20% of all claims denied**. These rates were essentially stable compared to 2023.

In raw terms: of roughly 44 million claims filed with reporting insurers, approximately 8.8 million were denied.

## Variation by Insurer

Denial rates varied significantly across reporting insurers, ranging from **3% to 36%** in 2024.

- Among major parent companies receiving over 5 million claims annually, rates ranged from **8% (Elevance Health) to 25% (Oscar Health)**.
- In 2024, only 3% of reporting insurers had in-network denial rates of 30% or higher, down from 17% in 2023.
- State-level variation: Hawaii had the highest average state-level denial rate at 27%; South Dakota had the lowest at 7%.

## Reasons for In-Network Claim Denial

Insurers categorize denials in HealthCare.gov reporting. The 2024 breakdown:

- "Other" (unspecified): **36%**
- Administrative reasons: **25%**
- Excluded services: **13%**
- Lack of prior authorization/referral: **9%**
- Medical necessity: **5%**

The plurality of denials — 36% — falls in the "Other" category, meaning neither the consumer nor the public has visibility into the specific reason the claim was denied. This is itself a form of opacity that compounds the appeals problem: consumers cannot meaningfully contest a denial whose reason has not been specified.

## Appeals Data — The Critical Finding

- **Fewer than 1% of denied in-network claims were appealed** in 2024.
- Of appealed claims, insurers **upheld 66% of original denials** — meaning approximately 34% of appeals resulted in the original denial being overturned.
- At least 5,881 external appeals (to independent review organizations) were filed in 2024.
- Only 4% of internal appeals that were upheld proceeded to external review.

### 2023 Comparison

In 2023, also fewer than 1% of denied claims were appealed, but 44% of appealed denials were overturned. The overturn rate dropped from 44% (2023) to 34% (2024).

**The math of the denial machine**: ~44 million claims are filed annually with reporting HealthCare.gov insurers. ~8.8 million are denied. Under 88,000 of those denials are appealed (<1%). Of those, approximately 30,000 are overturned.

## Further Context

- Insurance data reporting requirements under the ACA apply only to HealthCare.gov plans; employer-sponsored, Medicaid, Medicare Advantage, and off-exchange individual plans are not included. The true national denial figure is almost certainly larger.
- The 0.2% appeal rate cited in some reporting conflates ACA marketplace data with broader industry estimates; the KFF figure (<1% for HealthCare.gov plans) is the primary source.
- External/independent review overturns the insurer's internal decision approximately 40–60% of the time when reviewed, per separate KFF tracking — further evidence that insurer decisions are frequently reversible when subjected to external scrutiny.
