By April 1, the informal toll system had hardened into a structured IRGC-run operation. Ships wanting to transit must contact an IRGC-linked intermediary, submit sensitive vessel data (ownership, flag, cargo manifest, destination, crew lists, AIS transponder data), pass a security screen ensuring no links to Israel or US, then pay in Chinese yuan or stablecoins. Five-tier classification by flag state; friendlier nations get better terms. Base rate ~$1 per barrel of oil carried — VLCC carrying 2 million barrels = ~$2 million per large tanker.

After payment, IRGC issues permit code and route instructions. Vessels must raise the flag of the nation that negotiated passage and in some cases re-register. As ships approach the strait, they broadcast passcodes over VHF radio. Patrol boats escort them through passages near island groups now called "the Iranian tollbooth" by industry insiders.

Pakistan proposed allowing 20 Pakistani-flagged vessels passage and reached out to commodity traders about temporarily re-flagging ships under Pakistani registry. Vessel traffic increased slightly by early April but remained far below pre-conflict levels. Legal exposure for ship operators: sanctions compliance, anti-money laundering rules, insurance voidance when dealing with IRGC (a designated entity).
