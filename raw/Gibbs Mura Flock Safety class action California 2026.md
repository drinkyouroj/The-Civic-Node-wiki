# Flock Safety License Plate Reader Cameras Lawsuit — Gibbs Mura Class Action

**Source:** Gibbs Mura, A Law Group (law firm case page)
**Amended complaint filed:** April 3, 2026
**Original filing:** February 26, 2026
**Court:** San Francisco Superior Court
**Status:** Active

## Case Overview

Gibbs Mura (Oakland, CA) with co-counsel Milberg PLLC filed an amended class action complaint against Flock Safety alleging unauthorized tracking of California residents' movements and illegal sharing of license plate and location data with out-of-state law enforcement agencies and federal immigration authorities.

## Key Allegations

- Flock Safety used ALPRs to track millions of Californians' daily movements
- Illegally shared license plate, location, and vehicle data with out-of-state law enforcement
- Violated California privacy protections while more than 200 California municipal police and sheriff departments used Flock cameras

## Statutory Violations Alleged

- **California Civil Code § 1798.90.55(b)** — Prohibits sharing ALPR data with non-California public agencies or federal entities
- **California Civil Code § 1798.90.51(a)** — Requires reasonable security procedures for ALPR information
- **California Civil Code §§ 1798.90.51(b) and 1798.90.52(b)** — Mandates privacy policies aligned with civil liberties protections
- **SB 54** — California sanctuary state law prohibiting immigration enforcement data sharing

## Documented Violations

- **San Francisco Police Department:** Federal agencies accessed ALPR data more than **1.6 million times**
- **Los Altos:** Out-of-state agencies accessed data exceeding **1 million instances**
- **Mountain View:** Shut down entire Flock network citing unauthorized federal access concerns
- **El Cajon:** California AG Rob Bonta filed separate lawsuit (October 2025) alleging systematic violations

## Geographic Scope

More than 200 California municipal police and sheriff departments use Flock cameras, including Oakland, San Francisco, Berkeley, Mountain View, Los Gatos, and El Cajon.

## Potential Damages

Under California Civil Code § 1798.90.54:
- Actual damages (minimum $2,500 per violation)
- Punitive damages for willful violations
- Attorney's fees and court-ordered injunctive relief
- Three-year statute of limitations from violation date

## Note on Scope

This lawsuit is grounded in California state law (ALPR privacy statutes, SB 54), not the Fourth Amendment. No federal constitutional claims are asserted. A separate California AG lawsuit against El Cajon (filed October 2025) raises similar state-law violations.
