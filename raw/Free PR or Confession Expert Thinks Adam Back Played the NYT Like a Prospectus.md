Adam Back, Blockstream CEO, was identified by the New York Times as the leading candidate for being Satoshi Nakamoto. Industry observers suggest he may have strategically cooperated with this investigation to generate publicity for Bitcoin Standard Treasury Company (BSTR), his Bitcoin treasury firm preparing for a public listing.

Investigative journalist John Carreyrou revealed that Back agreed to participate in a photo shoot for the NYT story weeks prior to publication. Carreyrou questioned this cooperation publicly: "If you're not Satoshi and you know The New York Times is going to publish a big story identifying you as Satoshi, do you agree to participate in a photo shoot for that story?"

ETF analyst James Seyffart commented on the timing: "If you're IPO'ing a company — it's pretty damn good PR. Particularly when the cost is roughly zero."

BSTR is completing a SPAC merger with Cantor Equity Partners I. The deal includes a $1.5 billion PIPE (largest for a Bitcoin treasury vehicle) and BSTR plans to launch with over 30,000 BTC. The merger was expected to close in Q1/Q2 2026. The Satoshi spotlight landed at the most commercially convenient moment possible, whether Back intended the headlines or simply welcomed them.
