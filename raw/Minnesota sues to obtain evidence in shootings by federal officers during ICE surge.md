# Minnesota's Lawsuit for Evidence in Federal Officer Shootings

**Date:** March 24, 2026

**Core Issue:**
Minnesota officials sued the Trump administration seeking access to evidence needed to independently investigate three shootings by federal officers during "Operation Metro Surge," an immigration enforcement crackdown in the Minneapolis-St. Paul area.

**The Victims:**
- Renee Good (killed)
- Alex Pretti (killed)
- Julio Cesar Sosa-Celis (wounded in right thigh, January)

**Primary Claims:**
The lawsuit alleges the federal government violated a prior cooperation agreement by "categorically withholding evidence." Minnesota contends the federal government cannot "withhold investigative evidence for the purpose of shielding law enforcement officers from scrutiny" when a state investigates serious criminal violations within its borders.

**Key Quotes:**

Hennepin County Attorney Mary Moriarty stated: "We are prepared to fight for transparency and accountability that the federal government is desperate to avoid."

She emphasized: "There has to be an investigation any time a federal agent or a state agent takes the life of a person in our community."

Regarding federal conduct, Moriarty described it as having "adopted a policy of categorically withholding evidence," calling the practice "unprecedented and alarming."

**Background Context:**
The Justice Department opened a civil rights investigation into Pretti's death but declined similar review of Good's case—a departure from past administrations' standard procedure.
