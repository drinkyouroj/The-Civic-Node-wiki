# The Untouchables — PBS Frontline (January 22, 2013)

PBS Frontline documentary investigating why no senior Wall Street executive was arrested or prosecuted for the systemic fraud leading to the 2008 financial crisis. Correspondent: Martin Smith. Producer: Nick Verbitsky.

## Opening Premise (Narrator)

More than four years since the financial crisis of 2008, there have been no arrests of any senior Wall Street executives.

## Lanny Breuer (Assistant Attorney General, DOJ Criminal Division)

> "I am personally offended by much of what I've seen. I think there was a level of greed, a level of excessive risk taking in this situation that I find abominable and I find very upsetting. But that is not what makes a criminal case. What makes a criminal case is that I can prove beyond a reasonable doubt every element of a crime."

On prosecutorial hesitation regarding large banks:

> "The jobs of tens of thousands of employees can literally be at stake."

## Eliot Spitzer (Former NY Attorney General)

> "The Justice Department failed. They have not done what needed to be done."

## Senator Ted Kaufman (D-Delaware)

> "The men and women who duped would-be home owners... need to be identified, prosecuted, convicted and thrown in jail."

## Two Former High-Level DOJ Prosecutors (anonymous, interviewed by Frontline)

Two former high-level Justice Department prosecutors who served in the Criminal Division under Lanny Breuer told Frontline that when it came to Wall Street, there were no investigations going on. There were no subpoenas, no document reviews, no wiretaps. The former prosecutors said Breuer was overly fearful of losing.

## Whistleblower Testimony

Frontline interviewed due diligence underwriters including Tom Leonard, Eileen Loiacono, and Chico Morton, who described systematic fraud in the mortgage underwriting process:

- Supervisors instructed staff to avoid using the word "fraud" in written communications
- Lending standards were deliberately loosened
- Loans were processed at speeds that made genuine review impossible

## Richard Bowen (Senior Vice President, Citigroup)

Bowen reported internally that approximately 60% of the loans Citi was purchasing did not meet Citi's own policy standards by 2006. By 2007, that figure had risen to in excess of 80%. He escalated his concerns to senior management, including Robert Rubin, without prosecutorial action resulting.

## Key Journalistic Finding

Frontline and independent filmmaker Nick Verbitsky, plus private law firms including Patterson Belknap, located whistleblowers willing to testify — work the DOJ had not pursued during its own investigations. The whistleblowers were not hiding; they were simply never contacted.

## Context for the Newsletter

The Breuer quote — "greed is not necessarily criminal" — combined with the reporting that there were "no subpoenas, no document reviews, no wiretaps" establishes that the Obama DOJ did not attempt to prosecute senior Wall Street executives after 2008. This is a foundational cross-partisan data point for any argument that institutional non-accountability is structural rather than partisan.

The documentary pairs with Eric Holder's March 6, 2013 Senate Judiciary Committee testimony (given two months after this documentary aired) in which he acknowledged that the size of some financial institutions made prosecution difficult — the "too big to jail" testimony that became the shorthand for the Obama DOJ's enforcement posture toward Wall Street.
