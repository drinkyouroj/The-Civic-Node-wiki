The point of a siege is not always to breach the walls. Sometimes the point is to starve the garrison. An attacker who knows they cannot take the castle by storm can still ruin whoever is inside by making the defense expensive enough. The walls stand. Holding them is what costs.

That is the shape of a civil lawsuit where the plaintiff never needed to win.

On February 18, 2025, Pxie filed [Doe v. Bonnell](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/69650487/doe-v-bonnell/) in the Southern District of Florida. By her own [account on Substack](https://web.archive.org/web/20250121011243/https://pxie412.substack.com/p/i-will-be-suing-steven-kenneth-bonnell) that January, she had spent the preceding month consulting with lawyers. A GiveSendGo fundraiser for legal fees was linked in the same post; it [raised more than twenty-eight thousand dollars](https://web.archive.org/web/20250124104808/https://www.givesendgo.com/suedestiny) in its initial wave. That was fourteen months ago. The trial is now [scheduled for May 18, 2026](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.flsd.684111/gov.uscourts.flsd.684111.23.0.pdf). The plaintiff has just filed to [delay it](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.flsd.684111/gov.uscourts.flsd.684111.227.0.pdf).

## The Definition: the lawsuit is the punishment, not the verdict

There is a class of civil filing whose primary function is not to win. It is to run. Discovery is expensive. Depositions are expensive. Being publicly accused of something for fourteen months is expensive in ways that no invoice captures. The verdict, if one arrives, arrives in a different news cycle than the accusation. The cost has already been imposed. The correction does not catch up.

This is not a secret. It is what every civil litigator learns in their first year, and what does not get written in the briefs.

A weak case is one that might lose on the merits. A meritless case is one the plaintiff knows the theory will not hold. Both can run the same playbook once the filing exists. The distinction matters analytically. It does not matter structurally.

Pxie has been [unusually candid](https://web.archive.org/web/20250121011243/https://pxie412.substack.com/p/i-will-be-suing-steven-kenneth-bonnell) about the resources in play. In her January 20, 2025 Substack post, published with a link to her GiveSendGo campaign, she wrote: *“I cannot imagine what he might try to do to me in this process... As if it’s an indirect flaunt/warning over his capability of out spending me.”* She understood, on day zero, that the case was a spending contest. She chose federal court anyway.

The question is not whether Destiny wins. It is what winning costs him — and the answer to that is always the same, regardless of outcome.

The walls hold. Holding them is what costs.

## The Mechanics: the meter is running, whether anyone wins or not

Federal civil discovery runs for months and bills by the hour. The [scheduling order in this case](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.flsd.684111/gov.uscourts.flsd.684111.23.0.pdf) gave the parties close to ten months from the complaint to the discovery cutoff. Attorney rates at the level of Destiny’s defense move in the several-hundred-dollars-per-hour range, plus court reporters, transcripts, and document review on every contested exchange. The defendant pays regardless of outcome. Wealth, for a defendant, is a liability: more documents to produce, more depositions to defend, more motions to answer.

That is the baseline. What happens when a plaintiff treats the baseline as a weapon is where the mechanics of the thing become visible.

Three items from the docket, in ascending order of what they tell you.

**One: the Abbymc subpoena.** On May 3, 2025, Pxie’s counsel filed a declaration from a third-party witness calling herself “Abbymc,” alleging that Bonnell had transmitted the Video to her. For the next six months, Pxie’s counsel, who also represented Abbymc, refused to accept service of a defense subpoena on her behalf. On September 8, Abbymc appeared on a streamer’s livestream and stated publicly that her lawyers had advised her to evade service and keep submitting evidence; that clip became [Exhibit H to the motion to dismiss](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.flsd.684111/gov.uscourts.flsd.684111.132.0.pdf), filed eleven days later. A *“last known address”* produced by plaintiff’s counsel turned out to be a location process servers found uninhabited; a [correct address was not provided until November 17, 2025](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.flsd.684111/gov.uscourts.flsd.684111.210.0.pdf), four weeks before the discovery cutoff. When the subpoena was finally served on November 21, Abbymc produced zero responsive documents. Bonnell’s own discovery production — a full log of every file he ever sent her — [shows the Video was never among them](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.flsd.684111/gov.uscourts.flsd.684111.210.0.pdf). An affidavit whose author cannot be cross-examined is not evidence. It is a document designed to sit in the record without being tested.

**Two: the footnote about Rose.** On October 3, 2025, Pxie’s opposition to the motion to dismiss [asserted in footnote 2](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.flsd.684111/gov.uscourts.flsd.684111.139.0.pdf) that Rose, the user Bonnell sent the Video to in 2022, was a minor at the time. Three weeks later, the real Rose was deposed and [testified under oath](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.flsd.684111/gov.uscourts.flsd.684111.204.1.pdf) that she had never communicated with Bonnell at all; the account had been run by a UK-based adult male using her identity. The footnote’s factual premise dissolved. Pxie’s counsel declined to retract during the Rule 11 safe-harbor period. Bonnell’s team filed a [motion for sanctions](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.flsd.684111/gov.uscourts.flsd.684111.183.0.pdf) on December 1, 2025. It is still before the court.

**Three: spoliation, alleged for a year, never moved on.** Since the April 4, 2025 TRO hearing, Pxie’s counsel has alleged in writing, in [filing after filing](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.flsd.684111/gov.uscourts.flsd.684111.235.0.pdf), that Bonnell is destroying evidence. They have never filed a spoliation motion. Judge Torres [noted the gap](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.flsd.684111/gov.uscourts.flsd.684111.143.0.pdf) in October: *“It is now October of 2025 and there is no such motion on the docket.”* As of April 20, 2026, there still isn’t one. The accusation is public and indefinite. The adjudication that would resolve it has never been requested. Her counsel’s explanation, [filed yesterday](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.flsd.684111/gov.uscourts.flsd.684111.238.0.pdf), is that the six-month stay on Bonnell’s deposition (entered at Bonnell’s own request) has prevented them from developing the required factual record. April 17 brought its own milestone: Plaintiff’s pretrial witness and exhibit lists were due. Bonnell filed his. [Plaintiff did not](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.flsd.684111/gov.uscourts.flsd.684111.236.0.pdf).

One of those three items is bad lawyering. Three of them, all pointing the same direction, is not. Bad lawyering is random. This is directional.

## The Applications: the coordination layer in her own words

A federal lawsuit is a public document. Once filed, the complaint generates legitimized public accusations — *she filed, therefore the accusation is at least credible enough to report.* Media coverage, YouTube commentary, and social pile-on follow automatically. If a party wanted to extend that attention across the full duration of discovery, the mechanism would need to be: more public discussion, more people talking on their own platforms about the case.

In a [now-deleted X post](https://x.com/laurendelaguna) captured at the time, Lauren DeLaguna, a conservative commentator and lawyer, described herself as a witness who did not actively want to be one, and denied that she had encouraged Pxie to file or given her legal advice. She had been subpoenaed by the defense. She was subsequently deposed.

Two weeks later, on [December 5](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/G7fpxmdWMAAC3It.jpg), DeLaguna posted again. This time she admitted she had “spoken about the case to my friends” and had “asked a friend to represent” Pxie, while maintaining that she was “ *still not relevant to the case* “ on the underlying issues.

Take her at her word. A non-party witness admits recruiting plaintiff’s counsel and discussing the case with friends, then argues she is nonetheless not a participant in the case. Both things are true inside the technical frame she is using. The first is the coordination layer describing itself; the second is the plausible-deniability frame laid on top of it. Readers do not need to resolve the tension. The tension is the evidence.

This is why creator-era litigation is the specific category the piece is about. In a creator economy, the attack surface is the relationship between a performer and an audience. An accusation against a corporate defendant lands on a legal department. An accusation against a political streamer lands on everyone who ever decided they were emotionally invested. That is a population that grows every year, on every platform, with every new solo operator who trades career stability for direct access to an audience.

The structural asymmetry does not change. The number of viable targets does. Every creator with a reputation-dependent income is one well-timed filing and one cooperative witness away from the mechanics section of this essay.

## The Human Element: what the verdict doesn’t cover

I have watched this happen. Not to me. Close enough to see the version that does not require fame or a public docket. The shape is the same at every scale. A filing lands. The clock starts. The accused begins spending money and time and attention on the defense. Nine months in, they have had the conversation about their reputation with family, with business partners, with employers, with insurance. Fifteen months in, a verdict arrives. If they win it, the cost of the fourteen months is not mentioned anywhere in the verdict. The verdict cannot cover what the fourteen months cost. It is not a category the verdict knows about.

Most cases like this settle before anyone has to say any of this out loud. The defendant pays something. The plaintiff accepts something. The thing is closed with a confidentiality clause that keeps the lesson in the room. The cases that reach public trials reach them because one of the parties refused to settle, usually the defendant, for reasons of principle or reputation or both.

Bonnell has not settled. He is going to trial, assuming the [plaintiff’s motion to continue](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.flsd.684111/gov.uscourts.flsd.684111.227.0.pdf) does not move the date again. A verdict will arrive by May, or by August, or by whenever the case resolves. Whether it lands in his favor will shape the narrative but not the cost structure. The cost is already locked in.

What the defendant has actually paid, by the time a case like this is over, is the thing the verdict cannot name.

The walls stand. They will probably stand through trial. If the verdict comes in Destiny’s favor, it will confirm what his [motion for summary judgment](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.flsd.684111/gov.uscourts.flsd.684111.210.0.pdf) already argued: the statute does not reach the conduct at issue, and the case should not have existed as a matter of law. That is what a civil-court win looks like.

The garrison held. The castle was not taken.

What will also be true, whoever wins, is that fourteen months of discovery cost the defendant the last half of 2025 and the first half of 2026 in legal attention; that a searchable category of accusations will attach to his name for the rest of his career; that an amplification layer of his own critics used the pendency of the suit to keep those accusations live longer than any editorial desk would have sustained them on merit alone.

The attacker never came to breach the walls.

The siege was the point.

The creator economy is going to spend the next decade learning a question nobody wants to price in: how much of a reputation can be burned by process alone, before the verdict arrives with the news that the process was never the case. The verdict says you won, but it doesn’t say what you lost getting there.
