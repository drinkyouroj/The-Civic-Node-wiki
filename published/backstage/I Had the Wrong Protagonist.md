Two scholars. One chart. No ships. The strait doesn't negotiate.

I wrote [one Fed piece in two acts](https://drinkyouroj.substack.com/p/independent-inside-of-government) treating the chair as the protagonist. That was the wrong protagonist.

The first act was about the Powell pressure campaign. The second was about the Warsh confirmation. Both organized around the same question: who holds the seat, and what their public record implies about direction.

My read changed when I started looking at what rate policy can actually reach.

Rate policy reaches demand-pull inflation. A wage-price spiral, domestic demand running hot. That’s the condition rate hikes address. Volcker’s record is real. But what ended the 1970s inflation was OPEC normalizing supply and demand destruction running its course. Volcker hiked into the shock. The shock resolved through different mechanisms. The Fed gets credited for a sequence it was only partly responsible for.

Using Volcker as the template for a supply-shock environment requires demand-pull inflation. That requirement isn’t in the data right now.

The Strait of Hormuz has carried roughly 20% of global oil supply. Through April, roughly 95% of pre-war traffic stopped transiting. The figure I kept returning to: 191 tracked crossings in April against a pre-war pace of roughly 3,000 per month. At a certain point the percentage becomes a tanker count and you stop reading it as a macroeconomic concept. When you see 191 where 3,000 should be, the supply shock stops being a model variable and becomes a logistics problem. Rate policy has no pathway to that chart.

![Ship traffic through the Strait of Hormuz, 2025 vs. 2026. The 2026 line collapses from ~150 vessels/day to near zero in early March and stays there. Source: Starboard Maritime Intelligence/AMTI.](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!-vnV!,w_1456,c_limit,f_webp,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F2ade797d-c632-4cb5-a7c3-aae491307a40_1300x1040.png)

3,000 crossings a month became 191. The Fed can't hike its way to this chart.

The tariff structure is the same category. The effective rate sits at 19.7%, highest since Smoot-Hawley. The FOMC didn’t set it. The FOMC can’t unset it.

We have two binding constraints, and neither is addressable by rate policy. That’s the environment before anyone at the Fed does anything.

The piece I was originally building was organized around Warsh: whether he’s independent from the White House, whether his prior record implies a directional shift. I was asking the wrong question. The mandate is what matters. The chair is downstream of that.

Warsh inherits those constraints. He holds one of twelve FOMC votes. His credibility arrives pre-loaded with everything the confirmation process required him to say. None of that changes what rate policy is built for.

June 16-17 is Warsh’s first meeting as chair. The statement that follows is the document to read. It will either name the two constraints the rate tool can’t reach, or it won’t. A statement that doesn’t name them is a political document regardless of who signed it. Read it for what’s missing.

The piece ended up being about the tool. When the private analysis produces a correction, the published work should say so.
