## The Argument
Pxie v. Bonnell (S.D. Fla.) is treated as a case study in a class of civil litigation whose primary function is not to win but to impose cost. Discovery, depositions, and the reputational overhang of being publicly accused for fourteen months are the actual punishment — the verdict is "a different news cycle" and does not catch up to the accusation. The title concept: the lawsuit is the punishment, not the verdict.

## The Structure
The piece builds its case from three docket exhibits already filed in the wiki:

1. **The Abbymc subpoena** — six months of service evasion, a false "last known address," zero responsive documents when the subpoena was finally served. The affidavit was designed to sit in the record without being tested. See [[Doe v Bonnell ECF132 — Motion to Dismiss (Abby Subpoena History)]].
2. **The footnote about Rose** — asserted in plaintiff's MTD opposition that a recipient was a minor; the real Rose testified under oath she had never communicated with Bonnell. Footnote's factual premise dissolved; Rule 11 safe-harbor expired without retraction. Motion for sanctions still pending. See [[Doe v Bonnell ECF183 — Rule 11 Sanctions Motion (False Minor Allegation)]] and [[Doe v Bonnell ECF139 — Plaintiff Opposition to MTD (False Minor Footnote)]].
3. **Spoliation alleged for a year, never moved on** — Pxie's counsel has alleged destruction of evidence in filings since April 2025, but never filed a spoliation motion. Judge Torres noted the gap in October 2025. Counsel's explanation: the six-month deposition stay prevented developing the factual record. See [[Doe v Bonnell ECF235 — Bonnell Opposition to Trial Continuance]].

The argument then closes by using Pxie's own Substack post (January 20, 2025, linked to a GiveSendGo that raised $28k) where she framed the litigation as "an indirect flaunt/warning over his capability of out spending me" — treating the fact that she chose federal court with full awareness of the asymmetric cost structure as the decisive evidence of intent.

## What the Piece Leaves Open
- No specific remedy is proposed. The concept is descriptive; the closing frame ("the walls hold; holding them is what costs") is analytical, not prescriptive.
- The wider class of cases that fit this pattern is gestured at ("every civil litigator learns in their first year") but not mapped. A follow-up piece could do the genre-mapping.
- Whether the May 18, 2026 trial will hold is a live question as of publication — the plaintiff's motion to continue is the immediate hook.
