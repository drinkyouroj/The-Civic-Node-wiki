## What It Is

A nonfiction essay subtitled "A Field Guide to Collective Dread in 2025." Argues that the phrase "the vibes are off" is a diagnostically precise expression of collective nervous system dysregulation — not slang, but an accurate somatic assessment of ambient threat conditions.

## The Argument

The essay makes a neuroscience-grounded case that widespread anxiety in 2025 is not individual pathology but appropriate threat detection in a legitimately destabilizing environment. Three main moves:

1. **Amygdala mismatch**: Paleolithic threat-detection hardware running against perpetual-crisis politics. Your amygdala can't distinguish "predator in the bushes" from "constitutional crisis in your phone" — both trigger fight-or-flight, but only one resolves through physical action.

2. **Collective nervous system dysregulation**: Mirror neurons mean we don't just observe other people's fear — we experience it. When 90% of your feed is people in alarm, your body absorbs the data and adjusts. The vibes aren't off because perception is distorted. The vibes are off because things are measurably off.

3. **Self-care as category error**: The yoga-meditation-green-juice approach fails because it asks you to self-soothe a threat that exists outside yourself. Strategic regulation requires categorizing threats into "action possible" vs. "witness only" — local organizing vs. federal chaos — to prevent the exhausted paralysis of caring about everything equally.

## Key Data Points
