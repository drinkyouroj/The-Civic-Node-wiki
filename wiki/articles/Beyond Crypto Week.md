## Argument

Crypto Week (July 14-18, 2025) is a beginning, not an ending. The GENIUS Act, CLARITY Act, and Anti-CBDC Surveillance State Act together reshape the US digital asset landscape — but they open more questions than they close. The piece walks through four tensions the legislation creates: (1) new Treasury demand vs. bank disintermediation, (2) regulatory clarity as the key unlock for institutional capital, (3) consumer protection tradeoffs within the package, and (4) the US approach as a global precedent vs. the EU's MiCA.

## Structure

1. Setup — Crypto Week as watershed, but not endpoint
2. Economic rebalancing — Treasury demand, bank disintermediation, financial stability
3. Innovation unlock — regulatory clarity as catalyst for institutional adoption
4. Social contract — consumer protection, privacy, and the CBDC prohibition
5. Road ahead — rulemaking timelines, global leadership framing, MiCA contrast

## Key Examples

- Stablecoin market projected to reach "nearly $2 trillion" — cited as a structural new source of Treasury demand
- American Bankers Association warning about bank disintermediation and higher lending rates
- FDIC clarifying process for bank crypto custody; Mastercard building stablecoin infrastructure
- Consumer Reports / Americans for Financial Reform criticism of CLARITY Act's shift of oversight from SEC to CFTC
- Anti-CBDC Act framed as preventing a "surveillance state" analog to China's e-CNY
- Agency rulemaking deadlines (180 days to 1 year for Treasury, Fed, SEC, CFTC joint rulemakings)
- MiCA vs. GENIUS Act contrast — single comprehensive framework vs. targeted multi-bill approach

## What It Leaves Open

- The piece is substantially pro-industry in framing — "dawn of a new, more defined landscape" language; "green light for institutional capital" framing. Consumer-protection criticism is acknowledged but not analytically developed
- The $2T stablecoin projection is cited without sourcing analysis — the wiki's own [[GENIUS Act]] page now treats such numbers as aspirational scenarios, not facts. This article does not hedge them
- The Trump family's USD1 / World Liberty Financial conflict of interest is not mentioned at all — a significant editorial omission given the piece was written on July 19, 2025, immediately after Trump signed the bill
- The Tether loophole, Fed master account access gap, bankruptcy priority bailout risk (per Adam Levitin), and CFPB exclusion are not flagged — the piece defers to the "robust consumer protection measures" framing from the White House fact sheet
- MiCA comparison is gestural; the actual substantive differences (e.g., algorithmic stablecoin treatment, non-USD stablecoin caps) are not explored
- Sourcing leans heavily on primary press releases (White House, House Financial Services Committee, Emmer) and industry-aligned publications — thin on independent policy analysis

## Newsletter Context

This is an early, relatively conventional explainer piece on Crypto Week. Its analytical frame is the "balanced policy tradeoffs" genre rather than the more adversarial institutional-capture framing the later crypto coverage develops ([[The Anti-CBDC Surveillance State Act]], [[The CLARITY Act]], [[The GENIUS Act Is Law]], [[Half Right About Bitcoin]]). It sits as the wide-angle overview of the package; the subsequent bill-specific pieces carry more editorial weight. The piece's main value now is as a time-capsule of the pro-clarity framing before the corruption dimension became central to the coverage.
