## Summary
Tech Policy Press research piece (Columbia SIPA Innovation Lab) walking through age-assurance methods — declaration, government ID, biometric, parental consent, vouching, credit card — and documenting the disproportionate harm to [[LGBTQ+ youth]]. Argues digital-literacy and harm-reduction approaches are superior to age gates, and highlights France's "digital intermediary" firewall model as a privacy-preserving alternative.

## Key Points
- Taxonomy of age-assurance methods, from least (declaration) to most invasive (biometric).
- Automated content moderation removes LGBTQ+ content at disproportionately high rates (cited study, Sage Journals 2022).
- [[GLSEN]] Out Online report: LGBTQ+ youth use internet at higher rates and display higher civic engagement online than offline.
- Parental consent (e.g., [[Florida HB 3]] under DeSantis) is dangerous for queer youth in unsupportive households.
- Biometric verification: Algorithmic Justice League research shows bias against women, dark skin — training data gap likely extends to queer youth.
- LGBTQ+ youth are more likely to experience online sexual exploitation (JAMA Network Open) — argues for agency-focused protection, not access restriction.
- France piloting a "digital intermediary" third-party verifier that acts as firewall between content provider and verifier.
- [[KOSA]] history: original version mandated study of device/OS-level age verification; later amended to focus on design features, dropping "grooming" language.

## Newsletter Angles
- The harm-reduction framing: applies public-health logic (which works for drugs, sex education) to online safety. This is an intellectually serious alternative to age-gating that gets almost zero political oxygen.
- The "sexual exploitation paradox": LGBTQ+ youth face higher online exploitation risk precisely because they need the online space for identity exploration. Age gates don't fix this; they worsen it.
- France as a test case: digital intermediary model deserves closer examination as a privacy-preserving path.
