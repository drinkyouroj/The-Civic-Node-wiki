## Summary

On February 23, 2026, U.S. District Judge [[Aileen Cannon]] permanently barred public release of Volume II of Special Counsel [[Jack Smith]]'s report on Trump's alleged unlawful retention of classified documents at Mar-a-Lago. Cannon rejected the [[Knight First Amendment Institute]]'s motion to compel release and granted motions by Trump and former co-defendants to permanently prohibit disclosure. The decision relies on Cannon's earlier (and controversial) holding that Smith was unlawfully appointed. The Institute's appeal to the Eleventh Circuit remains pending.

## Key Points

- Ruling date: February 23, 2026; venue: Fort Pierce, Florida (S.D. Fla.)
- Blocks release of the second volume of Smith's final report — the portion covering the classified documents case
- Rests on Cannon's earlier ruling that Smith's appointment as special counsel was unconstitutional
- Court denied co-defendants' request to order the report destroyed — the document physically exists but is sealed
- Knight Institute argued no constitutional or common-law basis for permanent suppression
- Legal team: Scott Wilkens, Jameel Jaffer, Alex Abdo, Noah Kim, David Buckner
- Jaffer: "The First Amendment protects the public's right of access to documents filed in connection with criminal trials."
- Eleventh Circuit appeal filed earlier in February 2026; outcome pending

## Newsletter Angles

- **Retroactive Executive Protection**: Cannon's prior ruling that Smith's appointment was unlawful is now being used to suppress Smith's findings — a single judicial determination cascading into both dismissal of charges and permanent document suppression. This is the [[Retroactive Executive Protection]] pattern in its purest form: the mechanism protecting the president runs through a single friendly district judge.
- **Institutional Capture at the Judicial Level**: A Trump-appointed judge uses a contested legal theory (special-counsel appointment defect) to permanently bury the findings of an investigation into the president who appointed her. The appeal is the institutional backstop — but if the 11th Circuit doesn't reverse, the record is lost.
- **Trump Is Covering Up**: Pair with the DOJ Epstein files handling, the ballroom construction secrecy, and the permanent sealing of the Smith report as a cluster of active suppression behaviors, not passive non-disclosure.
