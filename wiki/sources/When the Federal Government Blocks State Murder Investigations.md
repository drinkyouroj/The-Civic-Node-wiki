## Summary
A GovFacts legal analysis arguing that the federal response to the Renée Good shooting represents a novel doctrinal expansion: federal officials are claiming not merely immunity from prosecution but immunity from *state investigation itself*. The piece introduces the term "defensive immunity" to distinguish preemptive blocking of investigations from the traditional post-charge supremacy clause defense, and draws a sharp contrast with the 2020 George Floyd case — where state and federal authorities ran parallel investigations and neither claimed exclusive jurisdiction. The article documents how the Good family has been forced into private litigation (represented by Romanucci & Blandin, the same firm that represented George Floyd's family) because both the federal and state prosecutorial tracks have been closed off.

## Key Points
- **Novel doctrinal claim**: federal officials blocking state investigations *before* charges are filed — described as "not previously been attempted — or at least not on this scale"
- **Traditional vs. defensive immunity**: traditional immunity is litigated post-charge with federal courts determining whether conduct fell within federal authority; "defensive" immunity preempts state investigation entirely
- **The George Floyd contrast**: both state and federal authorities conducted parallel investigations of Derek Chauvin; neither side claimed exclusive jurisdiction. The article calls the contrast "stark"
- **Ruby Ridge reference**: Idaho initially charged FBI sniper, though charge was later dismissed by federal courts — but Idaho authorities were not blocked from investigating
- **Private litigation as fallback**: Good family retained Romanucci & Blandin; the same firm represented George Floyd's family. Private lawsuits can produce damages but not prosecution, conviction, or incarceration
- **Evidence asymmetry in private suits**: autopsy, forensics, personnel files — easily obtained in prosecution — require "protracted legal battles" in civil litigation
- **Systemic failure**: "Neither federal prosecutors nor state prosecutors are investigating a potential crime that may constitute murder under state law, federal law, or both"
- **Prosecutor resignations**: multiple senior Justice Department Civil Rights Division prosecutors resigned in protest
- **33 former federal prosecutors in Minnesota** — many Republican-era — issued an open letter arguing state-federal cooperation on sensitive investigations has been standard in Minnesota for decades
- **Minnesota counter-moves**: litigation; evidence portals collecting community witness accounts; AG investigations conducted independently

## Newsletter Angles
- **"Defensive immunity" is a useful editorial coinage.** The article names a pattern that most reporting has left unnamed. Using this term in a TCN piece creates a handle for a doctrinal move that deserves one.
- **The George Floyd parallel is the central editorial frame.** Same city, same prosecutorial apparatus, same general pattern (a killing during law enforcement activity), and one case had parallel federal investigation while the other has federal obstruction. That contrast is the argument. Pairs with [[George Floyd Parallel Tracks]] source.
- **"Immune from investigation, not just prosecution"** is the precise phrase that makes the threat legible. Immunity from prosecution is a legal outcome; immunity from investigation is an informational blackout. These are fundamentally different things.
- **Romanucci & Blandin retention** is a direct narrative callback to George Floyd — worth naming in a piece because it shows the Good family sees the parallel themselves.
- **"33 former federal prosecutors in Minnesota" open letter** is an underutilized institutional signal — this isn't partisan critique, it's career prosecutors from both administrations calling the current posture abnormal.
