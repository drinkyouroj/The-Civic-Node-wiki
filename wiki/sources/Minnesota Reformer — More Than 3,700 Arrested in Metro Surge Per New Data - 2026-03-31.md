## Summary
Working from the same [[Deportation Data Project]] FOIA release as the MPR analysis, the Minnesota Reformer reports more than 3,700 people were arrested during [[Operation Metro Surge]], with less than one-quarter convicted of any crime and 13% facing pending charges. Critically, this source defines what "crime" means in that denominator: ICE counts misdemeanors as small as unauthorized entry and traffic violations. It is the load-bearing source for understanding that even the ~24% "criminal" figure overstates serious criminality.

## Key Points
- **More than 3,700 people arrested** during Operation Metro Surge.
- Data obtained via **Freedom of Information Act** request, released through the **[[Deportation Data Project]]**; the dataset contains **no names** but includes **age, nationality, date, and location** of each arrest.
- **Less than one-quarter had been convicted of a crime; 13% had pending criminal charges.**
- **THE LOAD-BEARING DEFINITION:** ICE's "definition of a crime includes misdemeanors, such as unauthorized entry into the country or traffic violations." The "convicted of a crime" denominator therefore is NOT a "serious crime" denominator.
- **The data does not include arrests of U.S. citizens** — and the article notes a **2021 GAO audit** finding ICE does not systematically track its arrests of U.S. citizens.
- Majority of arrestees came from **Ecuador and Mexico**; people from **Somalia made up less than 3%** of arrests.
- **About 15% of records were missing the arrest state/location** — a data-completeness caveat.

## Newsletter Angles
- **The denominator trap.** This is the source that makes the demographics section airtight: when "criminal" includes a traffic ticket or the act of crossing the border, even the ~24% figure inflates the count of genuine public-safety threats. The honest reading is that the share with a *serious* criminal record is well below one-quarter.
- **The citizen blind spot.** ICE structurally cannot tell you how many U.S. citizens it grabbed (2021 GAO audit) — so the 3,700 is a floor that excludes wrongful citizen arrests entirely.
- **Data hygiene as accountability.** No names, ~15% missing location, deduplication required — the federal government released the messiest possible version of its own record, and journalists still extracted a damning topline.
