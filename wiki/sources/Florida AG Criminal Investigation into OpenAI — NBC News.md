## Summary
On April 21, 2026, Florida Attorney General James Uthmeier announced a criminal investigation into OpenAI — an escalation from his April 9, 2026 civil probe — and issued subpoenas demanding internal training materials, user-threat-handling policies, crime-reporting procedures, an organizational chart, and a complete list of ChatGPT employees. The investigation is anchored to the April 2025 Florida State University mass shooting by Phoenix Ikner, which killed Robert Morales and Tiru Chabba; court documents show Ikner messaged ChatGPT in the minutes before the attack. Uthmeier: "If this were a person on the other side of the screen, we would be charging them with murder."

## Key Points
- First state-level criminal investigation of a frontier AI lab in the U.S., per the wiki's tracking. Civil probes had been the prior ceiling.
- Subpoena scope (March 2024 through April 2026) covers the entire modern ChatGPT deployment era and demands employee-level attribution: "who knew what, designed what, or should have done what."
- OpenAI's defense reduces ChatGPT output to search-engine equivalence: "factual responses to questions with information that could be found broadly across public sources on the internet." This framing becomes precedential if accepted.
- DeSantis has a parallel state-level track: December 2025 "Artificial Intelligence Bill of Rights" proposal covering data privacy, parental controls, consumer protections, and name/image/likeness restrictions. Florida is staking out the Republican AI-skeptic position against national-champion framing from Vance and the White House.
- The Morales family is preparing a civil wrongful-death suit against OpenAI, alleging ChatGPT "may have advised the shooter how to commit these heinous crimes."
- Uthmeier's "limited government" disclaimer ("I believe government should only interfere in business activities when you have significant harm to our people") is the exact frame that would support an affirmative defense if OpenAI wins on a narrow theory — it also signals the political terrain.

## Notes
The subpoena is the leverage mechanism, not the charging document — Uthmeier has not named charges or individuals. The "who knew what, designed what, or should have done what" framing parallels personal-liability theories in corporate criminal law. If OpenAI's public-information defense holds, it establishes a powerful precedent; if Florida pierces it, the implications reach every frontier lab's training decisions.
