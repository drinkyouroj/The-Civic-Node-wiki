## Summary

Primary press release from the Office of Attorney General [[Keith Ellison]] announcing an amended federal complaint against [[Department of Homeland Security|DHS]] over [[Operation Metro Surge]], with updated economic-harm figures produced by the U.S. Immigration Policy Center (USIPC) at UC San Diego: more than **$240 million in lost wages** (Minneapolis $189.2M, Saint Paul $54.6M) and approximately **$610 million in lost business revenue** (Minneapolis $444.8M, Saint Paul $165.4M). The figures rest on USIPC surveys of 1,390 individuals and ~900 businesses conducted February 17–March 6, 2026. The filing calls the USIPC work "the most comprehensive economic assessment of the operation's impact to date." This is the PRIMARY source for the $240M/$610M figures the wiki currently holds only via the secondary "Frozen Accountability" piece — a metro-wide, longer-window update to the [[City of Minneapolis]]'s February 2026 one-month preliminary.

## Key Points

- **Lost wages over $240M (metro-wide)**: Minneapolis residents $189.2M + Saint Paul residents $54.6M.
- **Business revenue lost ~$610M**: Minneapolis businesses $444.8M + Saint Paul businesses $165.4M.
- **USIPC methodology**: U.S. Immigration Policy Center at UC San Diego surveyed Feb 17–Mar 6, 2026 — 1,390 individuals (728 Minneapolis / 662 Saint Paul; ~25% reported DHS interactions) and ~900 businesses (489 Minneapolis / 405 Saint Paul).
- **Filing's own framing**: "the most comprehensive economic assessment of the operation's impact to date."
- **Operational harm**: 60% of Minneapolis and 61% of Saint Paul businesses reported negative operational impact; 62% of Minneapolis respondents with medical appointments who encountered DHS agents missed care; 73.1% reported DHS agents did not show warrants.
- **Vehicle is an amended complaint**: figures are entered into the federal lawsuit against DHS as documented economic harm, not merely a press estimate.
