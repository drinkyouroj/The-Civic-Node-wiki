## Summary
Reviewing more than 1,100 habeas corpus petitions, the Minnesota Reformer identified 273 people with valid work permits arrested during [[Operation Metro Surge]] — asylum-seekers, refugees, and DACA recipients whom the federal government had explicitly authorized to live and work in the U.S. Because the FOIA arrest dataset has no legal-status field, this is the best available evidence on arrestees' immigration status, and the reporters call 273 "almost certainly an undercount." It establishes a documented floor of legally-present detainees.

## Key Points
- From a review of **more than 1,100 habeas corpus petitions**, the Reformer identified **273 individuals with valid work permits** arrested during the surge.
- The reporters describe 273 as **"almost certainly an undercount"** — a floor, not a ceiling.
- Work-permit holders included **asylum-seekers, refugees, and DACA recipients** — people the government had granted permission to live and work in the U.S.
- The March-31 FOIA arrest dataset contains **no legal-status field**, so habeas petitions are the best available proxy for arrestees' immigration status.

## Newsletter Angles
- **Legal status was no shield.** The "worst of the worst" / "criminal illegal aliens" framing ([[Department of Homeland Security|DHS]]) cannot accommodate 273 people the government itself authorized to be here. This is the status-side counterpart to the conviction-side data.
- **A floor, not a ceiling.** 273 confirmed legal-status detentions, drawn only from those who filed habeas petitions, means the true number of legally-present people swept up is higher — most detainees never file.
- **The category collapse.** Asylees, refugees, and DACA recipients arrested in a "criminal alien" operation shows the operation's net was indifferent to status, undercutting the premise that this targeted removable criminals.
