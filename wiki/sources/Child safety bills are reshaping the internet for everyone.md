## Summary
The Verge feature on the cascade of state and federal child-safety bills reshaping internet access for all users. Anchors on Texas's age-verification law and traces [[Louisiana]]'s template spreading to 17+ states, alongside federal [[KOSA]]. Frames the movement as two-track: sincere Big Tech concerns plus a culture-war project to age-gate LGBTQ content out of youth access.

## Key Points
- Louisiana was first state to successfully age-gate online porn (2022); leveraged [[LA Wallet]] digital ID. 17+ copycat bills followed.
- Dominic Ford (JustFor.Fans): only 25% of visitors click verify link, only 9% complete; $1.50 per verification. [[Pornhub]] responded by blocking entire states.
- A dozen+ states have passed resolutions declaring porn a "public health crisis" despite limited research support.
- Utah Social Media Regulation Acts require under-18 parental consent and full account access for guardians — queer kids in abusive households most at risk.
- [[KOSA]] voted out of committee July 2023. Loose "harmful" definition leaves interpretation to state AGs — Texas's Paxton has called gender-affirming care child abuse.
- [[Heritage Foundation]] publicly supports KOSA and is not shy about using it against trans content.
- [[NCOSE]] (formerly Morality in Media) lobbies for these bills; has history opposing LGBTQ rights and sex workers.
- Precedent: 1996 [[Communications Decency Act]] indecency provisions struck down in [[Reno v. ACLU]] (1997).
- Free Speech Coalition suing Utah/Texas on First Amendment grounds.

## Newsletter Angles
- The "easy villain" problem: Big Tech and porn industry both have legitimate critiques, which is exactly why bills targeting them face minimal political opposition — and why the collateral damage to speech is so politically cheap.
- Evan Greer's framing: "progressives had the idea that they wanted to regulate Big Tech without fully appreciating the degree to which they were playing with fire."
- Historical echo: the 1996 CDA was bipartisan; it lost in the Supreme Court. Today's bills likely face the same fate but their rapid passage creates a fait accompli that reshapes platform behavior before courts catch up.
