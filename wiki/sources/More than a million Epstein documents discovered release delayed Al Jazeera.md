## Summary

Al Jazeera reporting on the DOJ's December 24, 2025 announcement — five days after the statutory deadline under the Epstein Files Transparency Act — that more than one million additional potentially Epstein-related documents had been discovered. The DOJ stated it needed "a few more weeks" to process. The article is the primary source for two critical institutional-gaslighting data points: Rep. [[Thomas Massie]]'s public statement that "DOJ did break the law" and the 12-senator letter to the DOJ Inspector General requesting an audit.

## Key Points

- **The discovery**: On December 24, 2025, DOJ announced that the U.S. Attorney for the Southern District of New York and the FBI had identified over one million additional potentially responsive documents.
- **DOJ framing**: Deputy AG [[Todd Blanche]] told NBC's Meet the Press that the review continues "simply to protect victims," adding that critics demanding faster release "apparently don't want us to protect victims."
- **Massie's public rebuke**: Rep. Thomas Massie (R-KY), chief Republican sponsor of the Epstein Files Transparency Act, posted on X: "DOJ did break the law by making illegal redactions and by missing the deadline."
- **12-senator IG audit request**: Republican Sen. [[Lisa Murkowski]] and 11 Democrats sent a joint letter on December 24, 2025 to Acting IG Don Berthiaume requesting an independent audit of DOJ compliance. Victims "deserve full disclosure," the letter said.
- **No further timeline**: The department released additional batches over the weekend and Tuesday, with no hard schedule announced for subsequent releases.

## Newsletter Angles

- **The Massie quote is the article's most important single element** — the Republican co-author of the law saying on the record that the Trump DOJ violated it. Any argument that the Epstein non-release is a partisan grievance collapses under this quote.
