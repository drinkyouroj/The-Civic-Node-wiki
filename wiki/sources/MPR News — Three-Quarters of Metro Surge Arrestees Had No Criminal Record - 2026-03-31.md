## Summary
New FOIA data — obtained through the [[Deportation Data Project]] lawsuit and analyzed by MPR News and APM Reports — shows at least 3,789 people were arrested during [[Operation Metro Surge]], the winter 2025–26 federal immigration surge into Minnesota. Despite federal officials' repeated "worst of the worst" framing, fewer than one-quarter of arrestees had a criminal conviction, a little more than 13% had pending charges, and the vast majority were taken in for civil immigration violations. This is the cleanest available denominator source on the operation's arrest demographics.

## Key Points
- **At least 3,789 people arrested** during Operation Metro Surge; the data is described as likely an undercount (incomplete federal records, after MPR/APM de-duplicated arrests).
- Data released by the **[[Deportation Data Project]]** via a **Freedom of Information Act lawsuit**; includes arrest location, country of origin, and criminal-history status.
- **Fewer than one-quarter (≈24%) had a criminal conviction** on their record.
- **A little more than 13% had pending criminal charges.**
- **The vast majority were arrested for civil immigration violations** (not crimes); some with criminal histories/pending charges were taken directly from jails rather than via targeted enforcement.
- **About 35% of arrests were "collateral"** — not the targets of the operation — consistent with accounts of people swept up at bus stops and on sidewalks.
- Average of **~49 arrests per day**; **about 30 arrestees were 16 or younger**, including **one child who was just 2 or 3 years old**; a handful were over 70.
- **Just 106 people from Somalia** were arrested (<3% of total), despite Trump publicly calling Somali Americans "garbage"; most Somali Americans in Minnesota are U.S. citizens.
- Country-of-origin: majority from Latin America — **more than one-quarter from Ecuador**, plus many from Mexico, Guatemala, and Honduras.
- Arrests spiked after ICE agent Jonathan Ross killed [[Killing of Renée Good|Renee Macklin Good]] on Jan. 7; [[Kristi Noem]] vowed to surge hundreds more agents afterward, yet daily arrests declined through mid-February.

## Newsletter Angles
- **The rhetoric-vs-data gap, quantified.** Federal officials sold "worst of the worst"; the government's own FOIA'd data shows ~76% had no conviction. This is the empirical refutation of the [[Department of Homeland Security|DHS]] framing (see [[ICE Continues to Remove the Worst of the Worst — DHS]]).
- **[[Enforcement Theater]].** 35% collateral arrests + a 2-to-3-year-old child + a daily-arrest curve that fell as agents increased = the optics of a crackdown decoupled from any "criminal" targeting logic.
- **The Somali "garbage" claim collapses on contact with the data** — 106 Somali arrests out of 3,789 undercuts the president's public scapegoating.
- **Civil vs. criminal is the load-bearing distinction** the whole operation's PR depended on blurring; this source establishes that civil violations were the norm.
