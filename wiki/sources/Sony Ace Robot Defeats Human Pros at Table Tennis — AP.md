## Summary
Sony's robotic arm "Ace" has defeated professional human table tennis players in matches conducted at Olympic-sized courts in Tokyo, per a peer-reviewed paper published in Nature. Using reinforcement learning, nine cameras, and eight degrees of freedom, Ace executes shots at human-comparable speeds and won against three of four high-skill players in December 2025. Sony frames it as "the first time a robot has achieved human, expert-level play in a commonly played competitive sport in the physical world."

## Key Points
- Sony AI deliberately constrained Ace's speed, reach, and performance to roughly match "a skilled athlete who trains at least 20 hours a week" — the claim is about decision-making parity, not raw superhuman speed.
- Reinforcement learning was the core method; researchers emphasize the impossibility of hand-programming for open-environment physical tasks.
- Sony AI president Michael Spranger called the past year a "ChatGPT moment for robotics" — a framing repeated across frontier-AI robotics labs.
- Billingsley (retired mechatronics professor, pioneer of robot ping-pong contests since 1983) credits the achievement but notes the nine-camera advantage undercuts the "fair match" framing.
- Spranger volunteered that "it's also not hard to imagine how such high-speed and highly perceptive hardware could be used in war" — unusual candor from a corporate AI lab on the dual-use implications of physical-world RL.
