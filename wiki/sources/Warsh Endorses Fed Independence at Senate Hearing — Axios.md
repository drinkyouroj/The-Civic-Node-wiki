## Summary
At his Senate Banking Committee confirmation hearing on April 21, 2026, Kevin Warsh explicitly endorsed monetary-policy independence — "I am committed to ensuring that the conduct of monetary policy remains strictly independent" — while presenting a narrower "staying in its lane" vision that implicitly criticizes Powell-era mission creep. Trump, appearing on CNBC's Squawk Box two hours earlier the same morning, refused to de-escalate the DOJ investigation of Powell over Fed building renovation costs, which Sen. Thom Tillis (R-N.C.) is using to block Warsh's nomination from advancing through committee.

## Key Points
- Warsh's public position contradicts the "Trump-aligned family fight" model the wiki already documented from [[Trump Fed Pick Kevin Warsh Could Shake Up the Central Bank With His Family Fight Model]] — either a pivot or a tactical confirmation-hearing posture.
- Warsh explicitly bounded independence to monetary policy alone: "does not extend to the full range of its congressionally mandated functions" like bank regulation, stewardship of public money, international finance. This is a narrower independence claim than Powell's.
- Tillis's block is specifically conditional on the Powell DOJ investigation — a novel Republican-senator leverage play against a Republican White House to defend Fed independence.
- Powell's term as chair ends May 15. He has stated he will serve as chairman pro tempore if no successor is confirmed.
- Trump's Squawk Box comment — "I'm afraid Kevin will have to have an office next to me in the White House, because that building's not going to be done" — explicitly treats the DOJ investigation as a trap Warsh must wait out, not a good-faith probe.
- Elizabeth Warren (D-Mass.) called Warsh "uniquely ill-suited" citing Wall Street ties.
