## Summary

WOSU 89.7 NPR News reporting on a Public Utilities Commission of Ohio (PUCO) filing dated November 24, 2025, in which AEP Ohio and Meta jointly request approval for Meta's New Albany data center to draw power from the Green Chapel substation — a substation completed earlier in 2025 to serve Intel's $28B chip fabrication project, but unused since Intel's July 2025 announcement to slow construction. The proposed three-year arrangement (Jan 1, 2026 - Dec 31, 2028) would see AEP Ohio build four temporary power lines, with Meta's electricity charges covering the substation's full three-year cost.

## Key Points

- **PUCO filing date:** November 24, 2025 (joint AEP Ohio + Meta).
- **Arrangement window:** January 1, 2026 through December 31, 2028 (three years).
- **Substation history:** Green Chapel built to serve Intel's $28B chip fab; completed in 2025; unused after Intel's July 2025 slowdown.
- **Infrastructure plan:** AEP Ohio constructs four temporary power lines from substation to Meta's New Albany data center.
- **Cost recovery:** Meta's electricity charges cover the substation's full three-year cost. Without Meta, "the cost of Green Chapel substation would be recovered through rates paid by all AEP customers" (AEP Ohio framing).
- **Meta's New Albany footprint:** $1.5B in capital investments and 300 full-time jobs since the 2017 announcement.
- **Intel's original timeline:** Plant production start dates originally slated for 2030 and 2031.

## Newsletter Angles

- **The inheritance pattern is becoming a category, not a coincidence.** This is the second major case study (after [[Stargate Data Center Expansion Cancelled — Oracle and OpenAI]]) where a hyperscaler steps into infrastructure built for a different industry that delayed. Microsoft inherited Abilene grid position from OpenAI; Meta is inheriting Green Chapel substation from Intel. The contracts being inherited aren't for compute — they're for grid position.
- **Cost-shifting mechanism made explicit.** AEP Ohio's framing — "without Meta the cost would be recovered through rates paid by all AEP customers" — is the cleanest articulation of how stranded utility infrastructure gets re-allocated. This is the inverse of [[Microsoft Electricity Cost Recovery Commitment — POWER Magazine - 2026-01-22]]'s pledge to NOT shift costs to ratepayers.
- **Three years isn't permanent.** The Dec 31, 2028 end-date suggests AEP Ohio expects Intel to resume by then. What happens at expiration — does Meta extend, find another stranded substation, or build its own — is a forward question that lands in Q1 2029.
