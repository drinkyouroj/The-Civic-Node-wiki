## Summary
Atlanta City Council passed two anti-ICE resolutions on **April 20, 2026**. The first opposes large immigration detention facilities in Atlanta. The second requires APD to document federal immigration activity and preserve body camera footage during ICE interactions. Neither resolution mentions Flock Safety or vendor contracts. Both go to Mayor Andre Dickens. Authored by Alani Letang, Atlanta News First.

## Key Points
- Vote date: **April 20, 2026** (Monday) — URL contains /04/21/ but article published April 20
- Resolution 1: Opposition to large ICE detention facilities within Atlanta
- Resolution 2: APD must document federal immigration activity + preserve body camera footage during ICE interactions
- **Flock Safety is not mentioned anywhere** in the article or either resolution
- Neither resolution governs the vendor contract or procurement relationships
- Vote counts not provided
- Both resolutions go to Mayor Dickens's desk

## Newsletter Angles
- Flock's complete absence from both resolutions is the article's central argument: the city addressed ICE through policy documents that don't reach the infrastructure layer
- The body camera preservation requirement (Resolution 2) governs officers during ICE interactions — it does not govern what the camera network logs automatically and shares via the vendor relationship
- Kelsea Bond's framing ("documentation," "accountability," "transparency") stops at officer behavior; vendor data flows are outside the resolution's scope
