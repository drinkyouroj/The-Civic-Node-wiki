## Summary
On April 19, 2026, the US Navy intercepted and seized the Iranian-flagged cargo ship Touska (~900 feet, under US Treasury sanctions) in the Gulf as part of the ongoing blockade. US forces disabled the ship with a direct hit to the engine room after it ignored stop warnings. Iran's Khatam al-Anbiya command called the boarding "armed piracy" and promised retaliation; Tehran said it would not attend the second round of US talks in Pakistan while the blockade remains. The announcement came as VP JD Vance prepared to lead the delegation with Witkoff and Kushner.

## Key Points
- First US seizure of an Iranian-flagged vessel during the conflict. Prior US actions were interception/turnaround.
- The Touska was already on the Treasury sanctions list — the legal predicate was pre-established, so the seizure extends an existing sanctions regime rather than creating new authority.
- Trump's framing — "it did not go well for them" — matches the Truth Social announcement pattern of policy-by-post.
- Iran retaliated three days later (April 22) by firing on and seizing three cargo ships in Hormuz. This source documents the escalation that preceded that retaliation — timing matters for the causal read.
- French CMA CGM vessel confirmed attacked on April 18; UK vessel claim unconfirmed.
- Strait was closed on April 19; 20% of world's oil and LNG normally transits the strait.
- Trump threatened to "knock out every bridge and power plant in Iran" if Tehran did not accept a deal.
