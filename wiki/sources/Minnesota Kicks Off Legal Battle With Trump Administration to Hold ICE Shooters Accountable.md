## Summary
ProPublica reports on the federal lawsuit filed in late March 2026 by Hennepin County and the State of Minnesota against DHS and DOJ, seeking evidence related to three shootings by federal agents during [[Operation Metro Surge]] — the killings of [[Renée Good]] and Alex Pretti and the wounding of Julio Cesar Sosa-Celis. Hennepin County Attorney Mary Moriarty calls the federal evidence-withholding "unprecedented in American history." The piece lays out the Supremacy Clause immunity doctrine as the primary legal obstacle, details Moriarty's 14 parallel investigations into federal-agent conduct during the surge, and flags procedural complications: leadership transition, unknown agent whereabouts, extradition uncertainty, and the possibility of federal defiance even on a winning ruling.

## Key Points
- Plaintiffs: Hennepin County + State of Minnesota; defendants: DHS + DOJ
- Moriarty calls the lawsuit "unprecedented in American history"
- Three covered incidents: Renée Good (killed Jan. 7), Julio Cesar Sosa-Celis (wounded Jan. 14), Alex Pretti (killed Jan. 24)
- Prosecutors sought weapons, investigative reports, agent GPS data, and witness statements via formal "Touhy letters" with a Feb. 17 deadline
- Feb. 13: FBI notified state investigators it would not share materials in the Pretti case; no materials provided more than a month past deadline
- Federal government initially agreed to joint investigation with Minnesota BCA after Good's shooting, then reversed after public statements by Trump administration officials
- Primary legal obstacle: the Constitution's Supremacy Clause immunity doctrine protecting federal officers acting lawfully within their duties. Jill Hasday (U Minn constitutional law) quoted: "probably the odds are stacked against it."
- Supreme Court has not addressed supremacy clause immunity cases in over 100 years
- Moriarty's office has opened 14 additional criminal investigations into federal-agent conduct during the surge, including a probe into Border Patrol commander Gregory Bovino (videotaped dispersing chemical agents at Minneapolis protests)
- Procedural hurdles flagged: Moriarty not seeking reelection; agent locations unknown; extradition "sort of iffy" per Ilya Somin; compliance with any ruling is uncertain (Timothy Johnson: "probably no")

## Newsletter Angles
- **The direct through-line to Justin's "Trump Is Covering Up the Minneapolis ICE Shooting (Just Like He's Covering Up Epstein)."** Withheld weapons, withheld GPS data, withheld witness statements — the federal government is not arguing the shootings were lawful; it is arguing states don't get to find out. That's a cover-up framing the source material fully supports.
- **The "unprecedented" claim is load-bearing.** Moriarty is a sitting county prosecutor; she is not rhetorically loose. The fact that the Supreme Court has not addressed supremacy clause immunity in 100+ years means the doctrinal ground is old and the federal claim here is stretching it.
- **14 parallel investigations is the real story.** The media frame is one lawsuit about three shootings; Moriarty is actually running a 17-incident docket against federal agents. That's the scale of documented misconduct during a two-month operation.
- **Extradition and compliance uncertainty** is the sleeper issue — even if Minnesota wins, the federal government may simply refuse to produce agents or evidence, and there is no straightforward state remedy for that.
