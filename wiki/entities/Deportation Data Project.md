## Overview
The Deportation Data Project (deportationdata.org) is an academic-led effort that obtains, cleans, and publishes the federal government's own immigration-enforcement data through **Freedom of Information Act litigation**. It is the reason the public knows what [[Operation Metro Surge]] actually did, as opposed to what DHS said it did: the project forced release of ICE's record-level arrest data, which newsrooms then analyzed to produce the population-level demographics DHS never volunteered.

## Key Facts
- Academic-led; co-director **Graeme Blair** (UCLA political scientist). Obtains ICE/CBP data via FOIA requests and lawsuits, then posts machine-readable datasets for journalists and researchers.
- The Metro Surge dataset it released carried per-arrest fields (age, nationality, date, location, ICE's conviction/pending-charge flags, and an ICE "collateral" flag) but **no names** and **no legal-status field**, and **excludes U.S. citizens** entirely (per a 2021 GAO finding, ICE does not systematically track citizen arrests).
- Its data is the common source under at least four independent Metro Surge analyses: [[MPR News — Three-Quarters of Metro Surge Arrestees Had No Criminal Record - 2026-03-31]] (APM Reports), [[Minnesota Reformer — More Than 3,700 Arrested in Metro Surge Per New Data - 2026-03-31]], The Intercept, and CBS Minnesota.
- **ICE's rebuttal**: ICE called the project "not accurate," arguing its releases are "information releases that have not been reviewed, audited or given context" — while offering no alternative dataset of its own.

## Newsletter Relevance
The project is the structural counter to [[Enforcement Theater]]: where DHS controls the narrative by controlling disclosure (~11% of arrests publicized; see [[I Mapped Every Confirmed ICE Arrest in Minnesota Heres What I Found]]), FOIA litigation pries loose the full population record. It is also a clean illustration that the accountability infrastructure for federal enforcement increasingly lives *outside* government — in universities, newsrooms, and civil-society litigation — precisely because the internal mechanisms ([[Touhy Doctrine]], DHS oversight offices) have been disabled or weaponized.
