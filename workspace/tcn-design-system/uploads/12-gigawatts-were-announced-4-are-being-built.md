# 12 Gigawatts Were Announced. 4 Are Being Built.

## Not chips. Transformers, batteries, circuit breakers — under 10% of the cost, 100% of the gating. The AI capex story is being told from the wrong layer.

By every model the AI capex story has been priced against, the binding constraint is supposed to be compute. Chips, contracts, GPUs, memory. That's what the earnings calls discuss. That's what the cap tables price. That's what crashed the market in March, when a 6x compression paper temporarily made it look like the whole pyramid was rebalancing.

It isn't. The constraint binds two layers lower, in a document class the financial press doesn't read.

[A month ago I named five forces](https://drinkyouroj.substack.com/p/the-bluff-is-over-the-price-isnt) holding DRAM prices at roughly double where the models say they should be. Force 4 was the data-center delay; the delay mechanism was electrical, not memory. Transformers. Switchgear. Batteries. I treated it as one of five. It isn't. It's the slowest one, the most concentrated one, and the one whose calendar can't be shortened by anything you can buy in 2028.

Hyperscalers are already pricing this. They're not buying compute. They're buying grid position. They're financing on-site generation because the queue for utility-scale interconnection runs longer than the asset's economic life. The chip layer is where the press release lives. The substation is where the contract dies.

The grid is the binding constraint. Six signals say so out loud, all already public, none yet connected.

---

## Signal 1: Microsoft Inherited a Grid Position, Not a Data Center

The Stargate Abilene expansion died on [March 6, 2026](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-06/oracle-and-openai-end-plans-to-expand-flagship-data-center). The 1.2 GW campus had been slated to grow to 2.0 GW; the 800 MW expansion got killed when Oracle and OpenAI's financing negotiations collapsed and OpenAI's demand forecasts shifted. Press read it two ways: a financing failure, a demand failure. Both were true on their face. Both were wrong by one layer.

Microsoft took over the construction project. Crusoe Energy is building two AI factory buildings on the same campus and a power plant on-site. [Meta is reportedly in talks](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-06/oracle-and-openai-end-plans-to-expand-flagship-data-center) to acquire the excess capacity, with NVIDIA brokering. A demand failure leaves the site sitting. This site didn't sit. It got handed off three times in ninety days, each handoff an upgrade rather than a write-down. The asset was more valuable than the demand picture suggested. What made it valuable wasn't the building. It was the existing 1.2 GW of grid position.

Crusoe building its own power plant on the Abilene site is the second tell. Hyperscalers don't usually finance their own generation. They contract for it. When they start writing the checks themselves, it isn't strategy. It's a workaround for a queue that doesn't move. They're not buying compute. They're buying grid position. Same site, three different operators in a season. The contracts being inherited aren't for chips or buildings. They're for proximity to electricity.

The original announcement was [a Trump-era $500B Stargate Project commitment](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-06/oracle-and-openai-end-plans-to-expand-flagship-data-center) in January 2025. Fourteen months in, the flagship expansion is dead and the operator has changed. The headline writes the announcement. The substation writes the cancellation.

## Signal 2: Less Than 10% of the Cost. 100% of the Gating.

Twelve gigawatts of US data center capacity were planned for 2026. [Only four are under construction](https://futurism.com/science-energy/data-centers-construction-supply). One-third delivery rate. 2027 is worse: 6.3 GW being built against 21.5 GW announced. 2028 through 2032: 4.5 GW broken ground against 37 GW planned. The gap widens every year. It is not a one-year anomaly recovering. It's a trend.

The bottleneck is named explicitly in the data: batteries, transformers, circuit breakers. Sourced from Canada, Mexico, South Korea, and China. Less than 10% of construction costs. 100% of the gating factor. That ratio is the article. [Andrew Likens](https://futurism.com/science-energy/data-centers-construction-supply), the energy and infrastructure lead at Crusoe Energy, says it cleanly: "If one piece of your supply chain is delayed, then your whole project can't deliver."

The press release is denominated in dollars. The delivery is denominated in megawatts. Different units. Different timelines. Only one of them gates anything. The capex story is being told from the chip layer because the chip layer is what fits in an earnings call. The grid layer doesn't fit in an earnings call. It fits in a transformer manufacturer's order book.

## Signal 3: The Document Class the Market Doesn't Price In

PJM, ERCOT, MISO, SPP, ISO-NE, and NYISO each maintain their own interconnection queue. The queues are public. They're updated quarterly. They're dispositive. A project with PPAs but no queue position is a press release. A project with queue position is real. The financial press AI capex coverage doesn't reference the queue. The queue is the first place the binding constraint shows up and the last place financial press looks.

Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory tracks the queues annually in the "Queued Up" series. The [2025 edition counted 10,303 active projects representing 2,290 GW of proposed capacity in queues](https://eta-publications.lbl.gov/sites/default/files/2025-12/queued_up_2025_edition_12.15.2025.pdf) against 1,322 GW of installed US capacity — nearly twice the entire grid. [Wait times have nearly tripled since 2008](https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/the-us-interconnection-queue-is-twice-its-installed-capacity/): under two years then, [55 months](https://eta-publications.lbl.gov/sites/default/files/2025-12/queued_up_2025_edition_12.15.2025.pdf) now (four and a half years) on the US median for 2024 completions — [seven in Northern Virginia, nine-plus in California](https://introl.com/blog/google-intersect-power-acquisition-energy-vertical-integration-january-2026).

[Roughly 77% of capacity](https://eta-publications.lbl.gov/sites/default/files/2025-12/queued_up_2025_edition_12.15.2025.pdf) from the 2000–2019 cohort withdrew before energization. Projects reach the front at price points or timelines that no longer support the economics that justified them. The withdrawal rate is the structural failure mode. It isn't bureaucracy. It's the wait outliving the financing.

The asymmetric edge for any analyst willing to read the queue is this: the document is public, the document is updated, the document decides who gets power, in what order, on what timeline. The chip layer is where the press release lives. The substation is where the contract dies. The queue is where the contract gets read.

## Signal 4: The Grid Stack Has Three Calendars. None of Them Are Short.

The grid stack has three nested calendars. First: the interconnection queue. Four and a half years on the US median for projects completing in 2024, per the [LBNL 2025 edition](https://eta-publications.lbl.gov/sites/default/files/2025-12/queued_up_2025_edition_12.15.2025.pdf); seven in Northern Virginia; nine-plus in California. Second: transformer procurement. Large power transformers averaging [128 weeks](https://www.powermag.com/transformers-in-2026-shortage-scramble-or-self-inflicted-crisis/) in the Q2 2025 industry survey; generator step-up units 144 weeks; substation transformers stretched from 140 weeks in 2023 to 160+ weeks in 2026. Wood Mackenzie projects a 30% supply deficit. Demand is up 119% for power transformers and 274% for generator step-up units since 2019. Third: substation and transmission energization. [Seven to ten years](https://www.powermag.com/microsoft-commits-to-full-electricity-cost-recovery-in-data-center-communities/) for permitting and siting, per Microsoft's own framing in its January 2026 commitment.

Four and a half years. Two and a half years. Seven to ten. They don't sequence. They run in parallel and the longest one wins.

The stack made operational: in November 2025, [Meta and AEP Ohio jointly filed](https://www.wosu.org/2025-11-26/meta-wants-to-draw-power-for-unused-new-albany-electrical-substation-built-for-intel-project) with the Public Utilities Commission of Ohio for a three-year arrangement. Meta would draw power from the Green Chapel substation, which AEP Ohio had built to serve [Intel's $28 billion chip fab](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/meta-seeks-to-use-idle-aep-ohio-substation-slated-to-power-delayed-intel-fab-for-new-albany-data-center-report/) but which had sat unused since Intel slowed construction in July 2025. The terms: 250 MW for three years, Meta initially taking 120 MW and ramping to 250 MW in April 2026. Intel's full 500 MW allotment is restored at the start of 2029.

What Meta is paying for in that contract isn't capacity. The capacity already exists. What Meta is paying for is the calendar Intel already cleared. Same shape as Microsoft inheriting Stargate Abilene. Two cases of hyperscalers acquiring already-elapsed time.

The chip layer can be unconcentrated by Chinese fab capacity by 2028. [CXMT's Shanghai mega-fab and YMTC's third Wuhan facility](https://drinkyouroj.substack.com/p/the-bluff-is-over-the-price-isnt) both target 2027 production; competitive pricing pressure lands 2028 at earliest. The grid stack cannot be unconcentrated on a comparable timeline. The queue can't be reformed into compliance with five-year financing horizons. The transformer manufacturers don't reach 9,000 units a year by 2030 starting from 1,500 today without the supply gap widening before it narrows. Transmission permitting takes the time it takes. Different calendars. Different decongestion dates. The chip layer reverts in eighteen months. The grid layer reverts in seven years if everything breaks right.

## Signal 5: The Variable the Fed Has Been Gaming Around

[Jason Furman's calculation](https://fortune.com/2025/10/07/data-centers-gdp-growth-zero-first-half-2025-jason-furman-harvard-economist/), published in Fortune in October 2025, is that US GDP growth in the first half of 2025 was 0.1% annualized excluding AI and data-center investment. AI data centers were 4% of GDP and 92% of GDP growth in that period. By August 2025, [per Renaissance Macro](https://fortune.com/2025/10/07/data-centers-gdp-growth-zero-first-half-2025-jason-furman-harvard-economist/), the dollar value of the AI data-center buildout had surpassed US consumer spending's contribution to GDP for the first time in the series.

Hyperscaler capex is approaching [$400 billion annually](https://fortune.com/2025/10/07/data-centers-gdp-growth-zero-first-half-2025-jason-furman-harvard-economist/). The top ten hyperscalers account for roughly a third of that figure. Jeff Bezos has called the buildout an ["industrial bubble"](https://fortune.com/2025/10/04/jeff-bezos-amazon-openai-sam-altman-ai-bubble-tech-stocks-investing/) rather than a financial bubble. He's not wrong. Financial bubbles pop. Industrial bubbles don't pop. They just don't deliver. The constraint isn't a price the market can mark down. It's a substation that hasn't been built yet.

If 30-50% of 2026 data-center capacity slips because of electrical infrastructure — and on the current trajectory, it will — that isn't a sector slowdown. It's a top-line GDP problem. The variable the Fed gamed the dual mandate around in 2025 is now downstream of substation-procurement timelines. Two of the three biggest binding instruments on US monetary policy in 2026 are not the Fed's to move. One of them is a shipping lane. The other one is a transformer manufacturer in South Korea. Neither moves with an interest rate.

## Signal 6: Three Predictions That Cost Something If They're Wrong

Three predictions for the rest of 2026, framed so being wrong is informative. First: the data-center delivery rate closes at or below the 4-of-12 trajectory. The slip will be reported as a financing or demand story; the underlying constraint will be electrical. Second: at least one major hyperscaler discloses a multi-year project delay attributable to interconnection or transformer procurement, in language that breaks from the chip-layer framing. Third: on-site generation deals — the Crusoe pattern, the [$4.75B Intersect Power acquisition pattern](https://introl.com/blog/google-intersect-power-acquisition-energy-vertical-integration-january-2026) — accelerate. Hyperscalers building or buying their own generators are pricing the grid queue as longer than the asset's economic life. They aren't betting on grid expansion. They're routing around it.

The information sits on state utility commission websites. Read the PUC filings. Track the queue depth. The chip-layer story is the document the press has bookmarked. The grid-layer story is the document nobody on the AI desk has bookmarked yet. The edge isn't that the data is hidden. The edge is that the data is in a different document class.

## Personal Reflection: What I Read Wrong First

I read the Stargate cancellation in March as a demand failure. I had two pieces in the can on DRAM concentration and the OpenAI LOI panic. The cancellation fit the existing narrative cleanly: Force 4 of five forces, one of the locks holding DRAM prices in place. I wrote it as a confirmation, not a discovery.

The thing that broke the read was Microsoft moving in. A demand failure leaves the site sitting. This site didn't sit. The sequence was Oracle out, OpenAI out, Microsoft in, Crusoe building on-site generation, Meta in talks for excess capacity. That's not what a stranded asset looks like. That's what an asset whose underlying scarcity has been mispriced looks like. The contract Microsoft inherited wasn't for compute. It was for grid position. Once I saw that, the rest of the analysis reorganized itself.

The chip layer is concentrated and slow. The grid layer is more concentrated and slower. The chip layer can be unconcentrated by Chinese fab capacity in 2028. The grid layer can't be unconcentrated by anything on a comparable timeline. I'm spending more reading time on PUC filings now. The newsletter will spend more analytical bandwidth on the grid layer in Q3. I run hardware. I know what it costs to run a node off a residential meter. The math at hyperscaler scale is the same shape, six or seven orders of magnitude up. Substation arithmetic is operator arithmetic. I just hadn't been applying it at the macro layer until Microsoft showed me where to look.
