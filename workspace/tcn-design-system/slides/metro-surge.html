[01] CONTEXT · MAY 1, 2026

A federal judge is doing the investigation nobody else will.

Judge Jeffrey M. Bryan ordered DHS to produce one ICE agent's personnel file, body-cam footage, witness statements, medical records, and cell-phone data. It is the first piece of the operation's original record any part of the federal government has put on the public record.

The retcon needs the record gone first.

[01]

Archive off.

DHS turned off automatic text-message archiving in April 2025. NARA ordered an investigation; DHS had not responded as of October 30.

[02]

Watchdog gutted.

Office of Civil Rights & Civil Liberties: 147 → fewer than 40 staff. Detention oversight: 118 → 5. One of 32 in-custody deaths investigated.

[03]

Data merged.

IRS-DHS data-sharing agreement signed; thousands of taxpayer records subsequently found wrongly disclosed. Three actions, one direction.

[02] FRAME · TWO RESPONSES

One agency walks back. Another files first.

FEDERAL · WALK-BACK
"We never do."

DOJ Civil Rights Division declined the Renée Good investigation, then opened one for Alex Pretti — same city, same operation, two weeks apart. Six federal prosecutors resigned in protest.

STATE · COUNTER-ARCHITECTURE
"Who files the second?"

Hennepin DA Moriarty filed first-in-modern-history state assault charges against a federal ICE agent. 14 open investigations. AG Ellison filed Minnesota v. DOJ/DHS on the anti-commandeering gap.
