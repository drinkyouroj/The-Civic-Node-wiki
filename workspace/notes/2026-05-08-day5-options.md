### Option A — Framework Hand: "The one question"

When you see a Fed rate decision story, ask one question before you read past the headline: what is actually moving the inflation number?

If the answer is a supply shock with a physical or political source, the rate decision is downstream of the constraint. The committee describes what it can do about a problem it can't reach.

The Strait of Hormuz is the live example. Tonight I'm writing about the bind.

*~70 words*

---

### Option B — Compressed Frame: "Read for the omission"

The FOMC meets next week. Wall Street is debating whether Warsh cuts.

The Strait of Hormuz is moving US inflation more than the federal funds rate is right now. Rate policy doesn't reach a closed shipping lane. The committee's statement will either acknowledge that or it won't.

Read for the omission.

*~54 words*

---

### Option C — Framework Hand: "Count the actors"

When you see coverage of who controls US inflation, count the actors.

The executive controls tariff policy: 19.7% effective rate, highest since 1933. Iran controls the Strait. Two levers the FOMC has no vote on.

Analysis that names only the Fed is describing one-third of the constraint.

*~50 words*

---
