### Option A -- Framework Hand -- contract vehicle test [Frame-forward, no trigger needed]

When a government passes a rule that vendors have to follow, ask one question before you move on: does that rule cover the kind of contract those vendors are using? Vendors hired through a contract type the law doesn't name aren't bound by the restriction. It can die in the fine print without anyone changing their vote.

*53 words, ~315 chars. Frame-forward: structural mechanism, durable across any news outcome.*

*Voice check: no em dashes; no negative parallelisms; "kind of contract" replaces "contract vehicle"; "the law" replaces "statute"; "fine print" is vivid and accessible; closing names the mechanism (dies in the fine print) with a specific condition (without anyone changing their vote). Passes §3J.*

### Option B -- Framework Hand -- procurement category test [Frame-forward, no trigger needed]

When you see a new law limiting what government vendors can do, ask one question: what kind of contract are those vendors actually operating under? If vendors work through a contract structure the law never names, the restriction doesn't reach them. Legislators can vote yes unanimously. The vendors just need the right contract on file.

*53 words, ~325 chars. Frame-forward: structural mechanism.*

*"Kind of contract" replaces "procurement category"; "the law never names" replaces "the statute doesn't cover"; "the right contract on file" is accessible and slightly ominous without being jargon. Closes on a concrete actor (vendors) doing a concrete thing. Passes §3J.*

### Option C -- Framework Hand -- contract-vs-statute split [Frame-forward, no trigger needed]

When you see a government rule that's supposed to limit vendors, ask what kind of contract those vendors are using. The law names what it covers. The contract determines whether the law actually applies. Legislators can pass the rule unanimously. Vendors can follow the contract and ignore the law. The documents are usually public.

*55 words, ~318 chars. Frame-forward: structural mechanism.*

*"Kind of contract" replaces "contract vehicle"; "the law" replaces "statute" throughout; "actually applies" replaces "vendor falls inside that name." Final parallel sentences: "Legislators can pass the rule unanimously. Vendors can follow the contract and ignore the law." -- two named actors, two active verbs, no rule of three, no §3F negation. Closes on "The documents are usually public" -- concrete fact, named subject. May 29 seed intact. Passes §3J.*
