US AI data center buildout: 12 GW announced for 2026. 4 GW under construction.

The construction filings name the constraint directly: transformers, switchgear, batteries,
circuit breakers. These components represent less than 10% of total construction costs
and 100% of the gating factor.

Three data points that frame the bottleneck:

The US interconnection queue (Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, 2025): roughly 2,290 GW
of proposed generating and storage capacity waiting to connect to the grid, against total
installed US capacity of approximately 1,322 GW. Median queue clearance time in 2024 was
55 months nationally. In California: 75–94 months.

Wood Mackenzie projects a 30% supply deficit for large power transformers against demand
that has risen 274% for generator step-up units since 2019. Lead times: 128 weeks average
for large power transformers, stretching to 160 weeks for substation units.

Global pipeline: 16 GW of AI data center capacity announced. 5 GW under construction.
30–50% schedule slip is documented once hardware queues are factored in.
(Source: Bricks & Bytes / Sightline Climate.)

The operational response from hyperscalers is instructive. Microsoft's Stargate Abilene
acquisition was specifically structured around the site's existing 1.2 GW grid connection.
Crusoe Energy Systems built on-site generation at the same campus to bypass the utility
queue entirely. Both moves represent the same underlying trade: acquiring time someone else
already cleared.

AI data centers represent approximately 4% of US GDP and accounted for roughly 92% of
GDP growth in the first half of 2025 (Furman/Fortune analysis). $400 billion in announced
capex is running through a transformer order book and a 55-month interconnection queue.
