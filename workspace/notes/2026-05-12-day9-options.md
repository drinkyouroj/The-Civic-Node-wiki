## NOTE 1: Framework Hand (3–5 PM)

*Give the reader one specific analytical move they can use immediately.*
*No image needed.*

---

When a new Fed chair publishes their first FOMC statement, the useful read is the
gap inventory: what binding constraints on inflation does the statement not name?

The June 16-17 meeting is Warsh's first as chair. The two binding constraints entering
that meeting are already on record: Hormuz throughput and the tariff structure. A
statement that holds rates without naming either one is a document. Read it that way.

*~75 words*

## NOTE 2: Contested Claim (6–7 PM)

*One committed position, no hedging.*
*No image needed.*

---

The instrument problem is bigger than the independence problem.

Rate policy reaches demand-pull inflation. Hormuz is a supply shock. Tariffs are a
supply shock. A chair who holds rates and gives an honest explanation has to name two
constraints the rate tool doesn't reach. A chair who holds rates and gives a political
one avoids naming them.

Whether Warsh is independent from Trump is a real question. Whether independence changes
what rate policy can do about either constraint is a different one. The discourse is
spending its attention on the first.

*~90 words*
