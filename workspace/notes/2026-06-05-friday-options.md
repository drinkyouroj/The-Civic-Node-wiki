## LINKEDIN REPOST — post ~8:00 PM ET, after the flagship is live

*Professional / policy-watcher register. No first-person, no newsletter voice, no CTA. Every figure cited with its source. Ends on the link. ~365 words. Asymmetric weld kept honest (shared cause named, divergent positions named); "leverage" avoided; 0 em dashes.*

On May 27, Samsung's semiconductor union ratified a profit-sharing agreement that ties chip-division bonuses to 10.5% of the division's operating profit, paid annually for ten years (Korea Herald; Tom's Hardware). The average payout this year approaches $400,000 per chip worker. Employees in Samsung's phone and appliance divisions, working under the same corporate roof, are in line for roughly $4,000.

The gap reflects where each group sits in the supply chain. The high-bandwidth memory Samsung's chip workers produce (the stacked memory every AI chip requires) is sold out, and Samsung accounts for 22.8% of South Korea's exports and 26% of its stock market. A work stoppage in Pyeongtaek does not dent a single quarter; it slows the global AI buildout. Samsung's chip-division profit rose 755% year over year (Bloomberg). SK Hynix's union had already secured 10% of operating profit the previous autumn, setting the benchmark before Samsung's workers negotiated.

The settlement split Samsung's workforce. The phone and appliance union approved the deal with just 21% support; a breakaway union grew from roughly 2,600 to 13,000 members and is preparing a legal challenge (Korea Herald). Because the profit share is indexed to the chip division alone, only chip workers' compensation scales with AI demand.

A parallel constraint sits on the other side of the same buildout, in the power grid. Lead times for high-capacity transformers have reached four years (pv magazine USA). The grain-oriented electrical steel that transformer cores require is produced domestically at a single mill: Cleveland-Cliffs in Butler, Pennsylvania. In PJM, the grid operator serving 13 eastern states, data center demand added $13.77 billion to customer bills across two capacity auctions, with wholesale power prices up 75.5% year over year (PJM Independent Market Monitor).

The same demand surge produces opposite outcomes at each constraint. What differs is position. The workers at the memory bottleneck could halt production and negotiate. The households near the grid bottleneck have no production to halt and cannot leave the grid, so they absorb the cost on bills they did not vote on.

Full analysis: https://drinkyouroj.substack.com/p/samsungs-400000-bonus-and-the-4000-one
