## LINKEDIN REPOST (post 6:30 PM ET, ~15 min after flagship publishes)

A governance vote authorizing one company to set commercial pricing for a network of 385,000 user-owned devices renewed itself by default in April. No replacement vote was filed.

The Helium DePIN network operates approximately 385,000 hotspots deployed by independent operators who paid between $249 (basic indoor IoT) and $949 (pro outdoor) per unit (AMBCrypto, August 2025). After the network's August 2025 halving cut new HNT emissions from 15 million to 7.5 million per year (Helium Foundation, August 2025), a well-placed urban hotspot earned $4–$8 per month (same source). The pro outdoor unit's payback period at the high end of that range is ten years.

The pricing those hotspots receive is set by Nova Labs, the company that operates Helium Mobile and negotiates carrier offload agreements with AT&T and other partners. That authority was granted by HIP-143, a Helium Improvement Proposal passed on April 3, 2025 (Helium Vote, April 2025). The proposal carried a one-year initial term and an auto-extension clause: the authority would extend by one additional year if no superseding HIP passed. In April 2026, the initial year ended without a superseding HIP, and the authority extended to April 2027 (Messari, State of Helium Q4 2025).

HIP-143 passed with 90.53% of 763 million veHNT votes cast. The Nova Labs proxy held 26.00%; the ferebee proxy, listed as a co-author of the proposal, held 24.00% (Helium Vote, HIP-143 results). Six months later, HIP-148 passed with 96.72% of 902 million veHNT cast. The ferebee proxy held 31.00% and Nova Labs held 26.00%, combining for 57% of the total vote (Helium Vote, HIP-148 results). HIP-148 redirected the Service Provider Pool from 10% to 24% of Mobile network emissions, directing the freed allocation "to the single Service Provider Nova Labs" (HIP-148 proposal text, github.com/helium/HIP).

Between the two votes, ferebee's veHNT holdings grew 55.5% in six months. Total network participation grew 18.2% in the same period (Helium Vote, comparison of vote pages).

The piece published today in The Civic Node walks the architecture, names four operator disclosures that would have caught this in 2021, and addresses a DePIN project The Civic Node previously cited as the legitimate counter-example.

https://drinkyouroj.substack.com/p/you-own-the-hotspot-nova-labs-owns-what-it-earns

## NOTE 3 — Framework Hand (post 9:30 PM ET)

### Option A — Framework Hand — single litmus question (re-vote vs. auto-extend) [Frame-forward, no trigger needed]

When you see a DePIN project pitch a governance vote that grants commercial authority to the parent company, ask one question before you read the proposal text. Does the authority end and require an active re-vote, or does it extend automatically if no one overrides? If "extends by default" is the answer, you're buying a franchise with no Franchise Disclosure Document. Live example: HIP-143 in April 2026.

*68 words / ~411 chars. Single-question litmus is the sharpest and most portable of the three. The Franchise Disclosure Document reference names a specific federal-law mechanism (FTC Franchise Rule, 16 CFR Part 436) — not a dismissal label, a structural comparison. Closer names a concrete event with date, passing §3J.*

### Option B — Framework Hand — two-question litmus (re-vote + exit right) [Frame-forward, no trigger needed]

When you see a DePIN project sell hardware that "earns" tokens, ask two questions before paying for the box. One: what is the exit right when the reward structure changes materially? Two: does the parent company's commercial authority extend automatically, or does it require an active re-vote? If the answers are "nothing" and "extends," the marketing sold a brochure. Live example: HIP-143 in April 2026.

*68 words / ~413 chars. Two-question version covers the two strongest moves from the article's four-part audit. Borderline: "the marketing sold a brochure" is a category-label closer — defensible because "brochure" is the specific document-type contrast the piece sets up, not a verdict-label like "press release."*

### Option C — Framework Hand — documents-written-after-the-hardware pattern [Frame-forward, no trigger needed]

When you see a DePIN project ship hardware before its governance documents are written, ask one question: which document explains what the hardware actually earns, and was it written before or after the box shipped? If "after," the operator agreed to whatever the governance documents say later. McDonald's franchisees read the disclosure before signing. Federal law requires it. DePIN operators do not get that.

*65 words / ~411 chars. The most cross-domain version — leads with the McDonald's analogy as the structural test. Closer names a specific actor class ("DePIN operators") with an active verb ("do not get that"), passing §3J.*
