## OPENER APPROACH MAP

**Technique:** Uncomfortable Truth — state what the primary document proves, dry, no editorializing. Let the contradiction land on the reader before naming it.

**Broad entry point:** Atlanta's formal pro-immigrant commitments — the policy layer the reader already knows exists. This is the setup the reader accepts before the floor drops.

**Paragraph structure:**

¶1 — Establish the policy layer with specificity (not generality): three concrete data points (2013, 2017, 2025) and the APD standing policy. End on the denial — verbatim.
- 2013: Atlanta declared itself a Welcoming City (joined national initiative)
- 2017: SAFE Cities Network membership
- 2025: City Council approved $4M in migrant case-management funding; accepted $156K deportation defense grant
- APD standing policy: officers do not request immigration documents; do not honor civil ICE detainers without a judicially-issued warrant
- APD denial (verbatim): "The Atlanta Police Department has not assisted any federal law enforcement agencies with immigration enforcement activities this year."
  Source: [[Atlanta PD used Flock cameras to track migrants]] (ACPC, Nov 13 2025)

¶2 — The same response that contained the denial also contained the audit. Numbers, tags, scale. Thesis lands in the final two sentences.
- 15 immigration-tagged searches, March 20–24, 2025 (four days)
- Search reasons logged: "locate alien" and "ERO assist" (ERO = Enforcement and Removal Operations, ICE's operational arm)
- U.S. Border Patrol: 3,254 searches against APD's camera network in 2025
- External agencies flagging immigration keywords: 3,383 searches
  Source: [[Atlanta PD used Flock cameras to track migrants]] (ACPC, Nov 13 2025)
- Thesis landing sentence (what it must say, not what it says): the camera network operated independently of the resolution — and that independence is structural, not incidental.

**Opener contract:** "They don't attend those meetings." The cameras were not briefed on the Welcoming City resolution. The close returns to what it would take for them to be: a procurement contract that reflects the policy. The sign becomes true when the vendor contract says so.

**Marcus 30-second test:** The denial + the audit in the same open-records response is the hook. Marcus reads the first sentence of ¶2 ("The same open-records response that contained that statement also contained the audit.") and he's committed. He's already checking sources in his head.

**What to avoid:**
- Don't editorialize the contradiction in the opener — the numbers make the argument; the writer's opinion is not needed here and weakens the effect
- Don't explain what ERO stands for in a way that reads as a lecture — define it in a parenthetical and move on
- The opener should feel like the journalist who found the document, not like the person who already knows what it means
