## Section 1: Problem — The Capture Already Happened (~450 words)
*Tone: dry, declarative; lay out the timeline as evidence*

- Lead with the eight-day arc as a single compressed unit: Apr 21 hearing → Apr 24 DOJ drops probe → Apr 26 Tillis flips → **Apr 29 committee vote at 10am, FOMC decision the same morning**. The most serious assault on Fed independence in living memory closes inside one workweek and gets ratified at 10am while the outgoing chair is in another room voting on rates.
- Quote Warsh's opening: "I am committed to ensuring that the conduct of monetary policy remains strictly independent." Place beside the same-testimony narrowing: independence "does not extend to the full range of its congressionally mandated functions" — bank regulation/supervision, stewardship of public monies, international finance. The carve-outs were in the same statement as the commitment.
- Add Warsh's own gloss, delivered in Q&A: the Fed is "independent inside of government, not independent of government." This is the precision-gift line — the redefinition that makes respectability capture work.
- Tillis Apr 26: "the U.S. Attorney's Office criminal investigation into Chair Powell was a serious threat to the Fed's independence, and it needed to end before I could support Kevin Warsh's confirmation." What this concedes: a sitting Fed chair was under criminal investigation as leverage over his successor's confirmation. Tillis treats it as a problem solved; it is a problem confessed.
- Trump on CNBC Apr 21 morning, two hours before Warsh testified: "Kevin will have to have an office next to me in the White House." Warsh did not address it. Neither did the committee.
- Federal judge James Boasberg, March 13, quashing the DOJ subpoenas of Powell: "no evidence whatsoever of fraud"; the probe's dominant purpose was "to harass and pressure Powell." That ruling is the judicial finding the Senate is voting past.
- Sources: [[Fed nominee Warsh endorses monetary policy independence as Trump declines off-ramps]], [[Tillis Backs Warsh as Powell Probe Ends — USA Today]], Reuters Warsh testimony preview, Boasberg ruling (Democracy Docket / Al Jazeera), [[Kevin Warsh]]

## Section 2: Analysis — Why "Respectability" Is the Capture Mechanism (~750 words)
*Tone: analytical, building; intellectual core*

### 2a. Why Warsh, not Miran, not a Cook firing
- Miran's unpaid-leave FOMC cameo too visible; Cook's attempted firing litigated and lost. Warsh works because resistance pattern-matches to "qualified, market-friendly former governor with a Hoover fellowship," not "regime change."
- Morgan Stanley M&A → 2006 Bush appointment (youngest Fed governor ever) → 2008 GFC Wall Street intermediary → Hoover post-Fed. The CV is the camouflage.
- The Kennedy "sock puppet" exchange shows the mechanism in action. Sen. Kennedy presses Warsh on whether he'll be Trump's instrument; Warsh: "the president never once asked me to commit to any particular interest rate decision." Technically responsive, structurally evasive — the question wasn't whether Trump asked for a specific number; the question was whether the entire selection process screened for accommodation. Warsh answers a smaller question and the Senate accepts it.
- Sources: [[Kevin Warsh]], [[CFR — Kevin Warsh Won't Revolutionize the Fed]], CNBC/CNN/Fortune hearing coverage

### 2b. The hawk-to-dove pivot is auditioning behavior
- 2010 Bernanke dissents (balance-sheet expansion); 15 years of public balance-sheet hawkishness; "hesitancy to cut rates is quite a mark against them" (CNBC, Summer 2025); now claims tariffs won't drive inflation — clearing intellectual lane for cuts Trump demands.
- David Wessel (Brookings): "in recent years, particularly as he's been campaigning, auditioning, if you will, to be Fed Chair, he's become more in sync with President Trump." That's a former Fed reporter saying it on PBS.
- The economy didn't change. The audience did.
- Sources: [[Kevin Warsh]], [[PBS NewsHour — What Trump's nomination of inflation hawk Kevin Warsh means for the Federal Reserve]], [[Commonfund — Fed Watching under Warsh]]

### 2c. The narrowing clauses are the actual policy
- The three carve-outs Warsh testified to:
  1. Bank regulation/supervision — independence "less warranted"
  2. Stewardship of public monies — Fed-Treasury coordination "not subordinate but more coordinated" (quietly erodes the 1951 Treasury-Fed Accord)
  3. International finance — narrower mandate
- Bloomberg (Apr 22) reads this as the Fed leg of a Bessent-Rubio "Statecraft" agenda for the dollar. Warsh's "independent inside of government, not independent of government" line is the formal version of that posture.
- A Warsh-chaired Fed could accommodate the White House on regulation, supervision, and fiscal coordination *while claiming the independence norm is intact*, because monetary-policy-narrowly-defined remained "independent."
- Precision gift: the trick is not to attack independence; the trick is to define it down to "the rate decision" and accommodate everywhere else.
- Sources: [[Fed nominee Warsh endorses monetary policy independence as Trump declines off-ramps]], Bloomberg "Statecraft" framing, Capital Flows Research

### 2d. The Burns parallel is wrong; Martin is right; Warsh changes both
- Burns faced exogenous supply shock (OPEC). Powell faces endogenous supply shock — tariffs manufactured by the same actor demanding accommodation. The president is both arsonist and fire marshal.
- Martin (1965) raised rates against LBJ at the Texas ranch. Powell ran a Martin defense for two years.
- Warsh changes the framing entirely: this is no longer "will Powell hold?" It is "will the institutional norm survive a successor who narrates it while narrowing it?"
- Sources: [[Fed Independence Under Endogenous Supply Shock]], [[Arthur Burns]], [[William McChesney Martin]]

## Section 3: Implications — What the Senate Just Ratified (~500 words)
*Tone: building to a single brutal reframe*

- The leverage architecture: probe opened Jan 2026 → Boasberg quashes subpoenas Mar 13 ("no evidence whatsoever of fraud") → DOJ closes probe Apr 24, routing renovation costs to Fed IG → Tillis flips Apr 26 → committee vote Apr 29. The probe wasn't dropped because it was unfounded; a federal judge had already said it was unfounded six weeks earlier. It was dropped because it had served its purpose.
- IG referral residual: only condition that reopens Powell's criminal exposure is a referral from the Fed inspector general on cost overruns. DOJ→IG handoff is procedurally available but conspicuous — the criminal probe was opened *without* an IG predicate, then closed under Boasberg's pressure, with the IG installed as the reopen mechanism. Powell finishes his pro-tempore term as a chair re-prosecutable at administrative discretion. Not exoneration; a pinned grenade.
- The silence is the story. Across three months of an active criminal probe of a sitting Fed chair, Warsh said nothing — no Hoover paper, no op-ed, no interview. On the day DOJ dropped the probe, Reuters called for comment; he did not respond. At his hearing he used the "pending matter" dodge on the Lisa Cook parallel: "inappropriate for me to weigh in." The probe simply *closed* on its own pretext, exactly when one GOP senator needed cover.
- Tillis at the hearing asked no substantive questions. He used his time to instruct DOJ — "Let's get rid of this investigation so I can support your confirmation." That is the entire Republican due-diligence record. Three days later DOJ obliged, and three days after that the committee voted. That's how respectability capture works: nobody has to say anything embarrassing because the embarrassing thing is processed administratively.
- Warren made the probe a central theme of Democratic opposition — $100M+ undisclosed holdings, refusal to confirm Trump lost in 2020, FBI background gaps, called the DOJ closure "an attempt to clear the path." But the votes weren't there. Democratic opposition is now a public-record exercise, not a procedural blockade.
- The norm test: Fed independence as a norm survives only so long as nominees refuse to define it down. Warsh is the test case for what happens when the nominee *is* the narrowing — and on Apr 29 at 10am, the Senate Banking Committee records it as compliance.
- Sources: [[Tillis Backs Warsh as Powell Probe Ends — USA Today]], JURIST DOJ-to-IG, Boasberg ruling, NOTUS Tillis statement, Senate Banking minority (Warren), [[Fed Independence]]

- Powell's last FOMC as chair-in-fact happens hours after the man replacing him clears committee on the strength of a closed criminal probe. The choreography is too clean to be accidental, but it doesn't have to be coordinated to be revealing. It just has to be allowed.
