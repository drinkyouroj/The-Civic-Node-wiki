## Detailed Outline: The Cypherpunk Who Filed an S-1

**Trigger:** Unexpected Connection
**Template:** Triple Connection
**Timeliness:** Adam Back is mid-process on a public SEC-registered offering via a Cantor Fitzgerald shell. Every securities lawyer in that process is about to ask a question Satoshi spent 17 years avoiding.
**Target length:** 1,500–1,600 words
**Headline:** The Cypherpunk Who Filed an S-1
**Subheadline:** Adam Back is taking a Bitcoin company public. If he's Satoshi Nakamoto, the SEC's disclosure rules are about to answer the question seventeen years of speculation couldn't.

### OPENER APPROACH MAP

Analogy to use: The patent filing. When you file a patent, you trade anonymity for credit — the invention leaves your notebook and enters the public record, but your name is now attached. The clerk's desk is also an identity desk. Satoshi filed a whitepaper (October 2008) but never filed the patent — the identity desk sat empty for seventeen years. Bitcoin has operated as the most successful anonymous patent in the history of money. The SEC is a different kind of clerk's desk: it doesn't care about credit, it cares about undisclosed assets. Adam Back is about to find out how different.

Paragraph structure:
- ¶1 — The broad hook: patent filing as the trade between anonymity and credit. The clerk's desk is an identity desk. Establishes the universal human-scale version of the argument.
- ¶2 — The narrowing: Satoshi filed a whitepaper, not a patent. The identity desk sat empty. Seventeen years. Pivots from broad to specific.
- ¶3 — The SEC pivot: a different kind of clerk's desk. Doesn't care about credit, cares about $118B in undisclosed assets. Thesis setup.
- ¶4 — Landing sentence: short, forward-pointing, creates momentum into Section 1. The reader now knows the argument.

Marcus 30-second test: The patent analogy is broad enough that a non-crypto reader follows. The narrowing to Satoshi is fast enough that crypto-native readers aren't waiting. The $118B number is the stop-scrolling fact. By end of ¶4, Marcus knows exactly what the piece argues.

What to avoid: Don't explain the patent analogy — if it needs explaining, it's the wrong analogy. Don't add context about who Satoshi is in the opener (Section 1 handles that). Don't use the word "irony" — let the irony speak. Don't reach for em dashes. Don't soften the landing sentence.
