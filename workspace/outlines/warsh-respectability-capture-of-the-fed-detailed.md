### OPENER APPROACH MAP

**Technique:** Uncomfortable Truth Stated Plainly (System Audit default)

**Hook to use:** The Senate isn't refusing to defend the Fed; it's ratifying the redefinition that makes a defense unnecessary. That's the broken thing. Open with it.

**Paragraph structure:**

¶1 — Establish the timing collision in the most physical possible terms: the committee vote and Powell's last FOMC happen on the same morning, in the same building complex, hours apart. No hostile takeover, no firing, no loyalty oath — a dark suit, a Hoover fellowship, and a unanimous-Republican committee voice vote. End on a short declarative: the Senate will record it as compliance.

¶2 — Land the precision-gift line. Warsh said it out loud at the hearing: "independent inside of government, not independent of government." Position it beside his "strictly independent" commitment from the same opening statement, and beside the carve-outs (bank regulation, stewardship of public money, international finance) from the same paragraph. Final sentence by end of ¶2: this is the Senate Banking Committee accepting the redefinition as the price of confirmation.

**Marcus 30-second test:** ¶1 names the news peg in concrete terms (date, time, simultaneity). ¶2 delivers the precision-gift phrase Marcus will forward. He stays.

**What to avoid:**
- Do not start with "On April 21" or any date-led sentence. The collision in ¶1 is the hook, not the chronology.
- Do not editorialize "respectability capture" in ¶1. The phrase belongs in the body. Let the facts produce the indictment.
- Do not use the phrase "Fed independence" before the Warsh quote lands. The quote is what makes the phrase contestable.

### Section 2 — Analysis: Why "Respectability" Is the Capture Mechanism

Word target: ~750 words | Tone: analytical, building, with one sardonic landing
Argument job: Explain how a capture this consequential can be ratified without anyone having to admit to it. The mechanism has four parts: the camouflage CV, the auditioning behavior, the redefinition of the word "independence," and the structural difference from Burns/Martin precedents.

#### 2a. Why Warsh, not Miran, not a Cook firing (~180 words)

**¶6 — The two prior attempts and why they failed.**

Facts to deploy:
- Stephen Miran's "unpaid leave" arrangement (Sept 2025–Feb 2026): same person held White House CEA chair and Fed Governor seat simultaneously. Resigned CEA Feb 3, 2026. Source: [[Stephen Miran]], `raw/Bessent Miran Warsh Coordination - CNBC Miran resigns CEA - 2026-02-03.md`
- Lisa Cook firing attempt: litigated; pending before the Supreme Court at time of Warsh hearing. Source: gap-fill hearing composite (Warsh "pending matter" dodge).
- Both were too visible — Miran's dual-role headline, Cook's Supreme Court litigation. The resistance landed on the form, not the substance.

Argument move: Establish the contrast frame for why Warsh works. The capture had to learn what doesn't work before arriving at what does.

Key sentence to land: A sentence that names what Miran and Cook had in common — visibility — and what Warsh offers that neither had: respectability that pre-empts the visibility problem.

**¶7 — The CV as camouflage.**

Facts to deploy:
- Morgan Stanley M&A group → 2006 Bush appointment to Federal Reserve Board (youngest-ever Fed governor at 35) → 2008 GFC primary intermediary between Bernanke's Fed and Wall Street → Hoover Institution Visiting Fellow + Stanford GSB lecturer post-2011. Source: [[Kevin Warsh]] entity, [[Commonfund — Fed Watching under Warsh]]
- Trump's nomination announcement (Jan 30, 2026): "He will go down as one of the great Fed chairmen, maybe the best. On top of everything else, he is central casting and will never let you down." Source: [[PBS NewsHour — What Trump's nomination of inflation hawk Kevin Warsh means for the Federal Reserve]]

Argument move: Show that the resistance to Warsh has nowhere to land. The CV pre-empts every standard objection. Trump's "central casting" line is the unintentionally honest one.

Key sentence to land: The sentence that takes "central casting" seriously as a description of the selection criterion, not a Trump verbal tic.

#### 2b. The hawk-to-dove pivot is auditioning behavior (~180 words)

**¶8 — Fifteen years of hawkishness, fourteen months of pivot.**

Facts to deploy:

Argument move: Build the contrast between the documented track record and the recent posture. The economic conditions did not pivot; the audience did.

Key sentence to land: David Wessel's PBS line, paraphrased or quoted: "auditioning, if you will, to be Fed Chair, he's become more in sync with President Trump." The Brookings senior fellow saying it on PBS is the credibility.

#### 2c. The narrowing clauses are the actual policy (~210 words)

**¶9 — Walk the three carve-outs in order.**

Facts to deploy:
- Warsh testimony, April 21: "the operational independence of monetary policy is properly viewed as paramount." But independence "does not extend to the full range of its congressionally mandated functions." The three carve-outs: (a) bank regulation/supervision; (b) stewardship of public monies; (c) international finance. Source: `raw/Warsh Confirmation Hearing - Reuters via The Standard - 2026-04-20.md`, [[Fed nominee Warsh endorses monetary policy independence as Trump declines off-ramps]]
- Warsh on Treasury coordination: not a return to pre-1951 Accord era but a "more coordinated (though not subordinate) relationship." Source: [[Janus Henderson — Quick View — Warsh's nomination and the next era of monetary policy]]
- Bloomberg framing (Apr 22): Warsh wants the Fed to back a Bessent-Rubio "Statecraft" agenda for the dollar. Source: `raw/Bessent Miran Warsh Coordination - Bloomberg - 2026-04-22.md`

Argument move: The body of the section. Each carve-out is a policy domain — name what the White House would gain in each. The carve-outs are the policy; the rate decision is the alibi.

Key sentence to land: The sentence that converts the abstract carve-outs into concrete administration wins — bank merger approvals, stablecoin oversight, Treasury debt-management coordination, dollar policy coordination with State.

**¶10 — The 1951 Accord, briefly.**

Facts to deploy:
- 1951 Treasury-Fed Accord ended the Fed's wartime peg of Treasury yields and established the operational firewall that defines modern Fed independence. Source: [[1951 Treasury-Fed Accord]]
- Warsh's "more coordinated, not subordinate" language is the precise vocabulary that erodes the firewall without breaching it. Source: [[Janus Henderson — Quick View — Warsh's nomination and the next era of monetary policy]]

Argument move: Give Marcus the historical anchor for why "coordination" is the operative word. He may know the Accord by name; many readers will not. Brief, factual, no melodrama.

Key sentence to land: A sentence that names what the Accord was *for* — preventing exactly the dynamic Warsh's "coordination" language permits.

**¶11 — The Kennedy "sock puppet" exchange as the mechanism in action.**

Facts to deploy:
- Sen. John Kennedy (R-LA) at the hearing pressed Warsh on whether he would be Trump's instrument. Warsh: "the president never once asked me to commit to any particular interest rate decision." Source: gap-fill hearing composite.
- The question Kennedy asked was structurally smaller than the question that mattered. Warsh answered the smaller question with technical precision and the Senate accepted it.

Argument move: Show the mechanism operating in real time. Respectability capture works because every individual question can be answered narrowly without addressing the systemic structure.

Key sentence to land: A sentence that names the move — answering the question asked rather than the question implied — and connects it to the carve-out structure of the opening statement.

#### 2d. The Burns parallel is wrong; Martin is right; Warsh changes both (~180 words)

**¶12 — Why every Fed-coverage hot take is using the wrong analogy.**

Facts to deploy:
- Burns faced exogenous supply shock (1973 OPEC embargo) plus political pressure. Standard reading: he capitulated. Revisionist reading (Meserve 2022, Democracy Journal): rate policy had limits against supply-side inflation regardless of independence. Source: [[Arthur Burns]], [[Rethinking Arthur Burns the Worst Fed Chair in History]]
- William McChesney Martin (Fed Chair 1951–1970) raised rates against LBJ's pressure in December 1965 at the Texas ranch. Held. Preserved institutional independence at political cost. Source: [[William McChesney Martin]]
- Trump's tariff-driven inflation is *endogenous* — manufactured by the same actor demanding rate cuts, reversible by the same actor at will. Source: [[Fed Independence Under Endogenous Supply Shock]]

Argument move: Reframe the conventional analogy. Burns ≠ Powell; Martin = Powell. But Warsh changes the question entirely: it is no longer "will Powell hold?" but "will the institution survive a successor who narrates the principle while narrowing it?"

Key sentence to land: The sentence that pivots from Powell-as-Martin to Warsh-as-the-test-case. Powell's defense is over; the next test is whether the norm survives someone who passed the confirmation hearing by redefining it.
