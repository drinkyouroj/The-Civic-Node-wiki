### Section 4: My Debug — What Happens When the Retcon Meets the Record (~200–250 words)
*Tone: first person, less analytical, more human; the argument's emotional anchor; mostly unchanged from v1*

- **Personal angle**: what it means to watch an institution try to rewrite its own paper trail in real time — and fail. Not because of journalists or activists, but because contemporaneous documents, judicial orders, and city accountants exist.
- **The anecdote should land on the distinction between spin and retcon**. Spin works on the future. Retcon tries to alter the past. The difference matters: a retcon fails the moment a primary document contradicts it. The DHS playbook this year has been to attempt the retcon before the documents exist, and to manage the documents into not existing where possible. **The April 2025 disabling of the text-archiving system is the unmistakable signature of an institution preparing for a retcon to come.**
- **Personal stake**: the constitutional questions in this piece extend beyond immigration. If a state cannot hold a federal agent accountable for on-duty conduct, and the federal mechanism declines to activate, there is a zone of unaccountability in American governance. The specific policy filling that zone today is immigration; the structural problem is not.
- **Connect back to the opener**: we opened with a court order and a deadline. The close: "In a few days we'll find out whether federal institutions still comply with federal courts. The documentation already exists either way."
