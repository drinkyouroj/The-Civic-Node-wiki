## Item 1: The Cancellation Wasn't a Demand Story (~275 words)
*Tone: analytical, building — taking the consensus reading apart*

- The Stargate Abilene expansion was killed in March 2026. The press read it as a demand failure (OpenAI's shifting forecasts) and a financing failure (Oracle / OpenAI couldn't agree on who pays). Both readings are partly right.
- The third reading is the one that matters: Microsoft inherited the project; Crusoe Energy is building an on-site power plant. The capacity is still being built. The owner just changed.
- The musical chairs are a tell. Inheritance is what happens when the slowest input — power — is more scarce than the demand for the building. If demand had collapsed, the site would sit. It didn't sit. Microsoft moved in.
- Crusoe building on-site generation is the second tell: hyperscalers are now financing private generation because they can't wait for utility-scale interconnection. That's not procurement — that's a workaround for a queue that doesn't move.
- The original announcement (January 2025) was a $500B Trump-era infrastructure commitment. Fourteen months later the flagship expansion is gone and the operator changed. The headline writes the announcement; the substation writes the cancellation.
- Sources: [[Stargate Data Center Expansion Cancelled — Oracle and OpenAI]] (Bloomberg, Mar 6 2026), [[Crusoe Energy]]

## Item 2: Four Gigawatts of Twelve (~275 words)
*Tone: numbers-forward, structural — let the ratios do the work*

- Sightline Climate analysis via Bloomberg, reported by Futurism (Apr 2 2026): of 12 GW of US data center capacity planned for 2026, **only 4 GW is actually under construction.** One-third delivery rate.
- The trajectory worsens. 2027 projections: 6.3 GW under construction vs. 21.5 GW announced. 2028–2032: 4.5 GW broken ground vs. 37 GW planned. The gap widens every year.
- The bottleneck named explicitly: batteries, transformers, circuit breakers — sourced from Canada, Mexico, South Korea, and China. **Less than 10% of construction costs. 100% of the gating factor.** That ratio is the whole thesis.
- One short Likens quote (Crusoe Energy), under 15 words: "If one piece of your supply chain is delayed, then your whole project can't deliver."
- The cost-vs-gating asymmetry is what's being missed. The AI capex story is told in dollars; the AI delivery story is told in transformer lead times. Different units. Different timelines. Only one of them is binding.
- Sources: [[Almost Half of US Data Centers Slated to Be Canceled or Delayed — Futurism]] (Bloomberg + Sightline Climate), [[Crusoe Energy]]

## Item 3: The Interconnection Queue (~275 words)
*Tone: precise, document-forward — the load-bearing item*

- Public utility commission filings are how the grid layer becomes legible. PJM, ERCOT, and MISO publish interconnection queue snapshots quarterly. The numbers are public. The financial press doesn't read them.
- The scale: ~2,290 GW of proposed generation and storage sits in US interconnection queues as of end-2024 — nearly twice the ~1,322 GW the grid currently delivers. 10,303 active projects. The queue is not a niche database; it is the largest electricity-infrastructure document in existence.
- The queue is not a procurement document — it is the document that decides who actually gets power, in what order, on what timeline. A project with PPAs but no queue position is a press release. A project with queue position is real.
- Frame the queue as the document the market doesn't price in: it is public, it is updated, it is dispositive, and it sits on a state-utility-commission website nobody on the AI desk has bookmarked.
- Note: this is an asymmetric edge. The PUC filing is the first place the constraint shows up and the last place financial press looks. Saying so is half the analytical content of the piece.
- Sources: [[Queued Up 2025 Edition — LBNL - 2025-12-15]], [[US Interconnection Queue Twice Installed Capacity — Latitude Media - 2024-04-11]]

## Item 4: The Grid Stack Has Three Calendars. None of Them Are Short. (~275 words)
*Tone: document-forward, structural — three calendars stacked, each independently multi-year*

- The grid stack has three nested calendars — **interconnection queue clearance**, **transformer procurement**, **substation / transmission energization** — each running multi-year independently, each compounding the others.
- **Calendar 1 — interconnection queue.** US-wide median 55 months (4.6 years) for projects completing in 2024 (LBNL 2025 edition); Northern Virginia 7 years; California 9+ years. ~2,290 GW in queues vs. ~1,322 GW installed — "nearly twice" installed capacity (LBNL 2025). ~77% of capacity from the 2000–2019 cohort withdrew before completion.
- **Calendar 2 — transformer procurement.** Large power transformers averaging 128 weeks (~2.5 yr); generator step-up units 144 weeks (~2.75 yr); substation transformers stretched from 140 weeks (2023) to 160+ weeks (2026). Wood Mackenzie projects 30% supply deficit; demand growth +119% (power) / +274% (GSU) since 2019. Lead-time trajectory widens year-over-year.
- **Calendar 3 — substation / transmission energization.** US transmission projects require 7-10 years for permitting and siting (Microsoft's own framing in its January 2026 commitment). >55% of US distribution transformers exceed 33 years old — aging-infrastructure load compounds the new-build calendar.
- **Worked case — Meta + AEP Ohio Green Chapel:** November 24, 2025 PUCO filing for 250 MW for three years (Meta initially 120 MW, ramping to full 250 MW in April 2026); full 500 MW Intel allotment restored at start of 2029. Substation completed 2025 for Intel's $28B fab; sat idle after Intel's July 2025 slowdown. **Same inheritance pattern as Microsoft taking over Stargate Abilene.** What Meta's contract pays for isn't megawatts — it's *already-elapsed calendar time*, the years of Calendar 3 that Intel paid through. Parallel infrastructure: Meta's separate 200 MW Socrates South behind-the-meter gas plant (June 2025 PUCO) at same site = inheritance + on-site generation as combined strategy.
- **Structural duration asymmetry.** Chip layer can be unconcentrated by Chinese fab capacity by 2028 ([[CXMT]] / [[YMTC]] 2027 production targets, 2028 market pressure). Grid stack cannot be unconcentrated by anything on a comparable timeline. **Different units, different decongestion dates, different binding properties.**
- Sources: [[Queued Up 2025 Edition — LBNL - 2025-12-15]] (queue size + median wait time), [[US Interconnection Queue Twice Installed Capacity — Latitude Media - 2024-04-11]] (2024 edition figures), [[Google Intersect Power Acquisition — Introl - 2026-01-20]] (Northern Virginia 7 years / California 9+ years), [[Transformers in 2026 — POWER Magazine - 2026-01-02]], [[Power Transformer Shortage]], [[Microsoft Electricity Cost Recovery Commitment — POWER Magazine - 2026-01-22]], [[Meta New Albany Substation Inherits Intel Project — WOSU - 2025-11-26]], [[Meta AEP Ohio Power Swap for Intel Delay — DCD - 2026-05-06]], [[The Bluff Is Over. The Price Isn't.]] (chip-layer 2028 timeline)

## Item 5: The Macro Layer (~275 words)
*Tone: systems-level — connecting the constraint upward, not just sideways*

- Furman / Fortune (Oct 2025): excluding AI/data-center investment, US GDP growth in H1 2025 would have been **0.1% annualized.** AI data-center investment was 4% of GDP and 92% of GDP growth.
- Renaissance Macro: by August 2025 the dollar value of AI data-center buildout had surpassed US consumer spending contribution to GDP for the first time. Hyperscaler capex nearing **$400B annually**; the top 10 hyperscalers account for roughly one-third of that total.
- The implication the macro coverage is missing: if 50% of 2026 data-center capacity slips because of electrical infrastructure (per Item 2), the slip is not a sector slowdown — it is a top-line GDP problem. The variable the Fed gamed the dual mandate around in 2025 is now downstream of substation-procurement timelines.
- This bridges to the Hormuz piece (May 8): two of the three biggest binding instruments on US monetary policy in 2026 are not the Fed's to move — one is a shipping lane, the other is a transformer manufacturer in South Korea.
- Bezos: "industrial bubble" rather than financial bubble. Brief Bezos quote, under 15 words. The bubble framing is wrong because the constraint isn't financial — it's physical, and physical constraints don't pop, they just don't deliver.
- Sources: [[Without data centers, GDP growth was 0.1% in H1 2025]] (Fortune / Furman), [[Bezos Italian Tech Week AI Industrial Bubble — Fortune 2025-10-04]] (Bezos "industrial bubble" quote), [[The Strait Is the Mandate]] (May 8 — companion piece)

## Item 6: What This Implies for the Rest of 2026 (~250 words)
*Tone: forward-looking, structural — the asymmetric reads available to a reader who is now armed*

- Three predictions the article commits to, each falsifiable:
  1. The 2026 data-center delivery rate will close at or below the 4-of-12 trajectory. The slip will be reported as a financing or demand story; the underlying constraint will be electrical.
  2. At least one major hyperscaler will publicly disclose a multi-year delay attributable to interconnection or transformer procurement, in language that breaks from the chip-layer framing.

- The asymmetric edge for the operator-class reader: read the PUC filings; track the queue depth; ignore the press release. The information arbitrage is sitting on state-utility-commission websites.
