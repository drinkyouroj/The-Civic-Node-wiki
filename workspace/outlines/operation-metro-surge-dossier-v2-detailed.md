### OPENER APPROACH MAP

**Analogy to use:**
The broad concept is **the institutional paper trail**. Every chain of command exists by producing documentation that runs upward — a structural, almost mechanical, design feature of bureaucracies. The bridging property: a paper trail is a load-bearing component of accountability the same way a foundation is a load-bearing component of a building. The narrowing move goes: paper trails as a general institutional design → the specific Federal Records Act / FOIA / discovery architecture → the May 1 court order as a single forced disclosure of one part of one operation's record → the larger operation, of which that single forced disclosure is a fraction.

The reframe the analogy must enable: **the interesting question isn't what DHS wants to hide; it's what — on its own published numbers — never came into public view in the first place.**

**Paragraph structure:**
- **¶1** — Open on the paper-trail principle as institutional design, then narrow to the specific May 1 court order. Establish what's being compelled (Ross files, body-cam, fitness records), who's doing the compelling (Judge Bryan), what's at stake (a federal records confrontation in either direction). The opening image: a federal judge as the only mechanism actively producing this record.
- **¶2** — Land the thesis. The specific sentence that must exist by end of ¶2: the structural claim that DHS publishes a number (3,000+ arrests) it cannot back with public names (only 335 disclosed; agents not publicly identified; 89% gap). The piece is about the gap between the published version and the verifiable version of one federal operation.

**Marcus 30-second test:**
Marcus is scanning. The opener earns the next 30 seconds via three signals: (1) a specific date and a specific judge — operational credibility that this piece has done the work; (2) a counter-intuitive frame ("the interesting thing isn't what they're hiding, it's what was never visible to begin with") that gives him a thought he doesn't already have; (3) a concrete number (89%) that is falsifiable and that he can immediately decide whether he believes. If he believes the number, he reads on. If he doesn't, he checks the source — Davidson — which is also a credibility signal.

**What to avoid:**
- Do not open with the killing of Renée Good. That belongs in ¶3, not ¶1. Opening on the killing makes the piece feel like a Renée Good piece; it's a structural opacity piece for which Renée Good is the irreducible human core.
- Do not open with "the Trump administration" or any partisan framing. The argument is structural; partisan framing narrows the audience.
- Do not let the May 1 framing become "the deadline is approaching" suspense — Marcus reads that as content marketing. It must be "this is what's actually being compelled and why."
- No throat-clearing. The first sentence has to be an image or a specific fact, not a setup.
