**The Central Tension**

The most compelling frame isn't "crypto project fails" -- that's a dog-bites-man story. The interesting frame is: _what does it look like when a VC-backed project is deliberately structured so that the public-facing leader has no actual power?_ Jason was the face, the community trusted him, he believed he was running something -- and the whole time he couldn't move a token, couldn't implement his own plans, couldn't stop William from announcing random features. That's not incompetence. That's architecture.

---

**Potential Article Structure**

_Part 1: The Pitch_ -- How Datagram recruited community members and why people believed in it. The node sale, the tokenomics promises, the energy in the Discord. This is where you establish what people thought they were buying into. You're a primary source here, which is a strength.

_Part 2: The Gap Between the Map and the Territory_ -- The difference between what was publicly announced and what was actually happening internally. The VPN debacle is a perfect case study: Kyle's Jan 2 meeting shows the mods trying to work with a product spec they half-understood, while William and Andy were fighting behind the scenes over whether to cut corners. The community was told to hype something nobody on the mod team fully understood.

_Part 3: The Structure of Control_ -- This is the meatiest piece. Who actually held power: the multisig wallets, the board, Richard and Tiffany. Jason's confirmation that he had theoretical tokenomics authority but zero operational control. The pattern of him writing plans that William would then ignore. This is where Jason's quote about ultimatums goes, if you can get it.

_Part 4: The Unraveling_ -- The payment breakdown, December collapse, the mod exodus, Jason's departure announcement. Chris's December timeline and Kyle's front-row seat to all of it. This is the most document-rich section given the Discord logs.

_Part 5: What It Means_ -- The VC angle. The broader pattern of crypto projects where the "decentralized" framing is cover for concentrated control. This is where your Civic Node thesis lives -- how power actually works versus how it's presented.

**The Angles That Are Strongest Right Now**

The mod payment story is probably your most airtight narrative because it's documented. You have the Google Sheets, the Discord logs, Chris confirming the chain of approval ran through Richard -- and you have a timeline. That's publishable without needing anyone to go on record with accusations.

The "Jason as fall guy" theory is explosive but requires more sourcing. Jason believes it, and hinted Carth shares the suspicion, but neither can prove it and Jason is scared. You'd need to handle it carefully -- probably presenting it as a theory held by insiders rather than an established fact.

The Richard/Tiffany thread is underdeveloped but potentially important. Chris named Richard as controlling payments and making direct hiring decisions. That's a structural claim that points away from William and toward whoever Richard actually is. Worth figuring out who he is before you write the VC angle.

William hasn't responded to anything, and without his voice the series has a hole in the middle. The "bad VC" framing might also be too convenient -- it lets Jason off the hook for the community experience even if he was structurally powerless. The strongest version of this series holds that complexity rather than resolving it too cleanly.
