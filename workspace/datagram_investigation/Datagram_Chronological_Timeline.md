# Datagram Network: Chronological Timeline
**Jun 2024 – Apr 2026 | Compiled from Discord Export Data**

---

> **How to read this document:** This is a plain-language narrative reconstruction of key events in Datagram's public history, built from 434,364 Discord messages across 46 channel parts, plus AMA transcripts, admin logs, and mod-discussion records. Dates are exact where sourced from message timestamps. Quotes are verbatim. Persons: BitBender = Jason Brink (CEO), drinkyouroj = Justin Hearn (community mod, researcher/author), kyleczech = Kyle (Senior Mod), nephilimhoss = Lead Discord Admin/Mod, enron_financial_intern = Chris, devikinbop = Tommy, jj3ss = Jess, aetrna1405 = Aety, yusha.nft = Yusha. Dr. William Nguyen = board-level founder/controlling shareholder.

## Phase 1: Pre-Launch Quiet Period
### Jun 2024 – Jan 2025

The Discord server was created in mid-2024 but showed almost no activity. The earliest logged messages in general-chat date to December 11, 2024 (1 message), December 12 (1 message), and a small cluster in late January 2025. This suggests the server was standing by while internal team work was underway.

By January 31, 2025, activity ticked up to 7 messages from 5 unique authors — likely early access members or team accounts testing channels. The public community had not yet been invited in any meaningful way.

**Quantitative baseline:** 0–7 messages/day, 1–5 unique authors, zero negative keyword signals.

---

## Phase 2: Server Launch and Early Community Building
### Feb 3 – Apr 2025

**February 3, 2025** is the server's true public launch date. Message volume jumped from near-zero to 66 messages across 24 unique authors in a single day, then 90 the next, indicating a public open or invite wave.

The mod-discussion channel shows that nephilimhoss (the Discord admin who built and owned the server) set up the moderation structure on this date:

> *"There is a fixed number of shifts per day, so covering Discord/Telegram is a simple matter of allowing y'all to pick up when you're able to work. I'm a believer in folks self-regulating, so I won't be micromanaging the schedule."* — nephilimhoss, Feb 3, 2025

This is the formal start of the volunteer/paid mod system. Mod pay was promised but details were deferred to Jason (BitBender).

Through February and March 2025, general-chat messages grew modestly: 23–266 messages per day, with between 8 and 37 unique authors daily. Negative keyword hits remained low (0–4 per day), indicating a phase of genuine community building rather than frustration.

Key facts established in this period:
- Datagram was presented as a DePIN (Decentralized Physical Infrastructure Network) built on Avalanche L1.
- The core product was a node network for routing UDP/VPN traffic, with node operators earning DGRAM tokens.
- Testnet nodes were free to run. Mainnet participation would require purchasing a Full Core Node License (FCNL).
- The tokenomics centered on a "burn-and-mint equilibrium" and a capsule NFT system that locked operator rewards on a vesting schedule.

**Quantitative baseline:** 4–266 messages/day, 3–37 unique authors/day, 0–4 negative hits/day.

## Phase 3: Testnet Goes Live and Community Accelerates
### May – Jun 11, 2025

Through April and May 2025, traffic grew steadily. By May 6–13, general-chat was seeing 263–488 messages per day with up to 45 unique authors, which represented a real engaged community.

The node software was in alpha testnet. Mods like jj3ss, kyleczech, devikinbop, and yusha.nft were handling a high volume of technical support questions: node installation, connectivity, point accumulation, wallet setup.

**Justin (drinkyouroj) joined the mod team in July 2025**, after testnet was already active. He was told he would be paid for his shifts; specifics of the payment structure were nominally set by nephilimhoss but ultimately controlled by Jason.

In mod-discussion on **June 16, 2025**, kyleczech made a notable comment revealing the team's strategic awareness:

> *"I want to help them get the narrative straight."* — kyleczech, Jun 16, 2025

And shortly after:

> *"I've been spamming the Copy Message Link function for specific announcements all day."* — kyleczech, Jun 16, 2025

These were not sinister statements — they reflected the genuine work of coordinating community communication during a technically chaotic testnet launch. But they document that "narrative management" was an explicit part of the mod team's job.

On the same day, the mod team was dealing with a serious incident: a mod named jl.lopezvelarde (Jose) was apparently hacked and lost more than $170,000 in SPX6900 tokens. The team privately provided support, with enron_financial_intern and nephilimhoss helping him investigate and secure his accounts:

> *"If they targeted you for being a mod, there's always someone who will target you to gain access."* — nephilimhoss, Jun 16, 2025

This illustrates that the mod role carried real personal risk, with no formal compensation structure in place to match that risk.

## Phase 4: TGE Week Explosion — The Server's Biggest Moment
### Jun 11–30, 2025

The data shows a sudden and massive spike on **June 11–12, 2025**:

| Date | Messages | Unique Authors | Neg Keyword Hits |
|------|---------|---------------|------------------|
| Jun 11 | 2,579 | 515 | 36 |
| Jun 12 | 22,656 | 5,490 | 272 |
| Jun 13 | 11,807 | 2,977 | 238 |
| Jun 14 | 12,289 | 3,139 | 194 |
| Jun 15 | 9,684 | 2,240 | 131 |

This is the TGE (Token Generation Event). DGRAM listed on exchanges. The server went from a few hundred daily participants to 5,490 unique authors in a single day. It was the largest single-day event in Datagram's entire Discord history by an order of magnitude.

The immediate post-TGE period showed a pattern characteristic of token launches: the "neg keyword hits" spiked dramatically proportional to the traffic spike, indicating a mix of hype, confusion, and early dissatisfaction arriving simultaneously with the new users.

Notable TGE-week events, sourced from the general-chat and node-owner-chat extracts:

- DGRAM listed on at least one CEX. Exact listing price was not sourced in these channels (exchange controlled the announcement).
- Multiple users expressed immediate confusion about the capsule system — specifically, that testnet node runners could not directly claim DGRAM but instead received capsule NFTs that only Full Core Node owners could "open."
- Users who had run free testnet nodes for months discovered that their reward path required selling their capsule to a node owner or buying a node themselves.
- Several users immediately characterized this as unfair: *"I ran my laptop for 60 days 24/7, I am getting 5-6 USD but 1 year vesting. Is there any biggest scam like this?"* — rejoan09, Nov 21, 2025

The mod team held the line with calm explanations. Kyle's "fruit analogy" for how capsules work became a stock response. But the community's trust in the capsule mechanics was clearly strained from day one.

On **June 23, 2025**, Kyle made a candid admission in mod-discussion:

> *"Those promises came from Jason."* — kyleczech, Jun 23, 2025

This was in response to a question about whether certain commitments had been made. It confirms that the mod team understood Jason as the source of the project's community-facing commitments, not the technical team or the board.

**Quantitative summary (Jun 11 – Jun 30):** 2,579–22,656 messages/day, 515–5,490 unique authors/day, 36–272 negative hits/day.

## Phase 5: Post-TGE Decay — Volume Collapse and Node Sale
### Jul – Sep 2025

After the TGE spike, traffic declined steeply and leveled off at a new plateau:

| Period | Avg Messages/Day | Avg Unique Authors |
|--------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Jul 1–15 | ~4,200 | ~900 |
| Jul 16–31 | ~2,800 | ~550 |
| Aug 1–15 | ~2,000 | ~370 |
| Aug 16–31 | ~1,200 | ~240 |
| Sep 1–30 | ~750 | ~185 |

This is textbook post-TGE audience bleed. The community was losing roughly half its active participants every few weeks.

**August 10, 2025: The Full Core Node sale opened to the public.** This was a major commercial event — the point at which real money changed hands at scale. Tier pricing started at lower tiers (accessible) and rose through multiple tranches. The node sale was conducted on Ethereum mainnet (USDT payment), which caused gas fee friction for some buyers:

> *"I lost 70$ in gas sir. I tried in rabby, base and metamask for hours."* — krish_genie, Aug 12, 2025

BitBender acknowledged the technical issues:

> *"The problem seems to have been isolated to the USDT contract on some RPCs used by Metamask."* — bitbender, Aug 12, 2025

Mainnet was publicly projected for **"late September – early October 2025"** during this period:

> *"Mainnet is scheduled for late Sept early Oct at this stage."* — devikinbop, Aug 12, 2025

> *"Our team are eyeing to have it on Late Sept - Early Oct. So stay tuned until then!"* — yusha.nft, Aug 12, 2025

These would turn out to be the first of several mainnet timeline misses.

Community concern about the sustainability of the tokenomics was already surfacing. On August 12, a community member asked:

> *"What happens to the remaining unsold Nodes?"* — tatoyuyu, Aug 12, 2025

No clear public answer to this question was found in the data.

## Phase 6: Pre-Mainnet Stress — October–November 2025

Through September and October 2025, the community stabilized at a quiet plateau (500–1,200 messages/day), but that stability masked growing anxiety. Negative keyword hits ran at 5–25/day — low absolute numbers, but representing 2–4% of all daily messages, a notably elevated ratio.

October saw two localized spikes (Oct 25–28 and Oct 30–31: 858–1,232 messages/day) that corresponded to specific events or promotions, but no documented mainnet launch. Late September mainnet date had already quietly passed.

In mod-discussion, the team was discussing practical problems: reward reconciliation, user verification issues, Engage bot costs (nephilimhoss was personally paying ~$50/month for Engage), and the challenge of a community that had been promised specific outcomes at TGE that were now delayed.

Justin (drinkyouroj) noted a growing pattern in mod-discussion on **September 27, 2025**:

> *"Between the kind of sentiment in the 'pay to get paid' message and the fact that so many people have been (apparently) willing to run testnet nodes without understanding why…"* — drinkyouroj, Sep 27, 2025

This was an honest observation: a large portion of the community had joined without fully understanding the capsule/vesting mechanics, and this misalignment was a ticking clock.

## Phase 7: Mainnet Launch and Immediate Crisis
### Nov 14–20, 2025

**November 14, 2025: The TGE and mainnet launch week finally arrived.** The data shows an explosion:

| Date | Messages | Unique Authors | Neg Hits |
|------|---------|---------------|---------|
| Nov 14 | 4,168 | 569 | 147 |
| Nov 15 | 4,426 | 751 | 122 |
| Nov 16 | 4,556 | 864 | 135 |
| Nov 17 | 8,564 | 1,498 | 281 |
| Nov 18 | 7,271 | 1,433 | 321 |

The testnet explorer switched to mainnet on November 14 — team members in general-chat cheered this in real time. All testnet nodes went offline simultaneously, which was expected but caused a wave of confused users.

The snapshot for testnet reward capsule airdrops was announced as having been taken the previous week. Capsule claiming was set for November 25, with a conversion rate "better than 100:1" (DG Points to DGRAM).

**The negative keyword spike on Nov 17–18 (281–321 hits/day) is the highest absolute count in the entire dataset.** This reflects thousands of new users arriving who did not understand the capsule system, combined with existing community members who were frustrated by:

1. Not being able to claim DGRAM immediately (capsules still locked until Nov 25)
2. Discovering that opening a capsule early would forfeit ~90% of unvested tokens
3. The DGRAM price dropping significantly in the days following listing
4. Confusion about the difference between testnet capsules, bonus capsules, and mainnet node rewards

Representative quotes from this period:

> *"Why rugged after listing even though there's no claiming the airdrop. You guys avoiding dump on listing but when I check the coin now it's rugged."* — melzxc\_, Nov 21, 2025

> *"Ser, a rugpull is when our team pulled out the liquidation and sold massive amounts. We didn't perform any rugpull. We are still building."* — yusha.nft, Nov 21, 2025

> *"I have taken many nodes for myself. I have built a huge team. I have sold many nodes. But you are not giving commission. You are not giving rewards. You are just harassing me. My trust in you is decreasing."* — tuhin200, Nov 21, 2025

From kyleczech, trying to manage expectations:

> *"Today's rewards are not normal. Our dev team is looking into it. I'll be able to tell you more once they update me."* — kyleczech, Dec 11, 2025

Token price was already in steep decline by the week after TGE. Community members in node-owner-chat noted:

> *"Price looks like it was artificially kept stable as long as nodes were selling and after boom dump."* — alex97xs, Dec 11, 2025

> *"And price -60% in 3 days. Going for pennies per node."* — alex97xs, Dec 11, 2025

## Phase 8: Post-Capsule Launch — Gradual Disillusionment
### Nov 25 – Dec 31, 2025

On **November 25, 2025**, capsule claims opened. The conversion rate was revealed. Many testnet participants who had accumulated significant DG points found their DGRAM allocation disappointing given the token price at that time.

One candid calculation from the community:

> *"Think: I have 50,000 points and the conversation rate is 50:1, I get 1,000 DGRAM, price now 0.006$ — I get 6$ only."* — simantoout, Nov 21, 2025

**November 26, 2025** saw another major spike (4,236 messages, 730 unique authors, 243 negative hits) — the highest negative hit count for a single day outside the TGE week itself. The ratio of negative keywords to total messages (~5.7%) was the worst single-day reading in the entire dataset for any moderately trafficked day. This date aligned with capsule distributions and the continuing price decline.

Traffic remained elevated through late November and into early December but was clearly driven by frustration rather than engagement:

| Date | Messages | Neg Hits | Neg % |
|------|---------|---------|-------|
| Nov 26 | 4,236 | 243 | 5.7% |
| Nov 28 | 3,478 | 166 | 4.8% |
| Nov 29 | 4,430 | 149 | 3.4% |
| Nov 30 | 2,887 | 102 | 3.5% |
| Dec 1 | 2,529 | 93 | 3.7% |

By December, the community was smaller but angrier. Node operators began reporting:
- Reward distribution anomalies (Dec 11: ~40–50% reward shortfall affecting many nodes)
- The uptime percentage display had been removed from the dashboard with no explanation
- Token price continuing to fall

Kyle's posture in December was to absorb frustration while relaying dev team responses that often amounted to "we are investigating":

> *"Whatever your rewards were today, this is likely not the trend. Our devs are investigating."* — kyleczech, Dec 11, 2025

Toward the end of December, traffic dropped sharply — a combination of holidays and community attrition. The server went from ~2,500+ messages/day to under 400 by late December 2025.

Justin (drinkyouroj) was still active as a mod through this entire period, cheerfully helping users and building scripts:

> *"Products are cookin'!"* — drinkyouroj, Jan 2, 2026

> *"Stay tuned, I heard a rumor we might hear something sooner than later."* — drinkyouroj, Jan 2, 2026

## Phase 9: The Collapse — Jason Resigns and William Disappears
### Jan 4–31, 2026

The first sign that something major was happening appeared in the stats:

| Date | Messages | Neg Hits |
|------|---------|---------|
| Jan 4 | 338 | 28 |
| Jan 7 | 528 | 26 |
| Jan 8 | 595 | 28 |
| Jan 17 | 323 | 39 |
| Jan 18 | 259 | 31 |

The negative hit rate in January was extremely elevated relative to traffic volume, suggesting a small but very agitated community.

**January 17, 2026: Jason (BitBender) announced his resignation from Datagram.** Before making the public announcement, he sent a private message to the mod team in mod-discussion:

> *"I am getting ready to drop a community announcement. I want to give you some clear guidance following the public announcement. If you are asked about the situation, please respond with: 'The announcement speaks for itself, and we can't really comment on it.' Please do not speculate, elaborate, or engage in back and forth beyond that within the Datagram server. I also want to be explicit that I will make sure all moderators are paid their USDT through…"* — bitbender, Jan 17, 2026

[Note: the message was cut off in the extract, but the commitment to pay USDT was made.]

The mod team's reactions were immediate and revealing. Kyle's was gracious:

> *"Understood. Thank you, Jason, for everything you've poured into this project... the seen and the unseen."* — kyleczech, Jan 17, 2026

Chris (enron_financial_intern) was more candid:

> *"Just like Jason I'm frankly done with the madness bestowed upon us by the leadership. That being said I value each and everyone of you guys."* — enron_financial_intern, Jan 17, 2026

In Jason's public announcement, he stated: *"I do not have the ability to access or move tokens, and I do not control treasury or on chain resources."*

This was a bombshell. The CEO of Datagram was saying publicly that he had no control over the project's treasury or blockchain resources. This confirmed that Dr. William Nguyen held the controlling position.

The same evening, Justin asked the question the entire mod team must have been thinking:

> *"Hey Jason, was DGRAM already set aside for mods that you just don't have access to right now? Or should we expect the USDT to be our final payment? Sad ending, but thanks for all the fish."* — drinkyouroj, Jan 17, 2026

Five days later, on **January 22, 2026**, after no response from Jason or William, Justin followed up:

> *"I'm not going to expect any token payment (it probably wouldn't be worth very much anyway…) but I was hoping to hear that tokens had at least been set aside at TGE to pay mods and you're just unable to access them directly. I'd still like to receive my DGRAM allotment, even if it's only worth a fraction of a percent of what it 'should' be. For me, it's about follow-through more than money."* — drinkyouroj, Jan 22, 2026

Jason did respond to this one:

> *"William has told me that tokens are set aside. He has told me that he has allocated some tokens to give to mods as team members, etc, but as of yet I have not received them. He has told both me and Kyle that he has set them aside though, so as soon as I have access to them, I will get them out to you guys."* — bitbender, Jan 22, 2026

The tokens never arrived.

## Phase 11: Terminal Decay — March–April 2026

By March 2026, the server had collapsed to single-digit message counts most days:

| Date | Messages | Neg Hits |
|------|---------|---------|
| Mar 1 | 9 | 1 |
| Mar 5 | 24 | 5 |
| Mar 11 | 7 | 1 |
| Mar 22 | 12 | 2 |
| Apr 3 | 3 | 1 |
| Apr 9 | 22 | 6 |
| Apr 12 | 5 | 0 |

Kyle remained in the server, still answering occasional questions. In mid-March, he offered a frank assessment:

> *"I think he [William] either believes that nothing is wrong or that something is wrong and he can save it... Based off of his behavior and lack of community involvement, I have zero clue what he intends to do to right this ship that he has steered directly onto the rocks."* — kyleczech, Mar 11, 2026

Nephilimhoss seriously considered converting the server:

> *"If no one comes in here and captains the ship, we can take a vote and turn it into a support group, game server, place to warn against scummy projects, etc."* — nephilimhoss, Mar 4, 2026

> *"I'll need to talk with a lawyer anyway to make sure I CYA. Anyone scummy enough to take money and never deliver a product should not be trusted to not make predatory lawsuits."* — nephilimhoss, Mar 4, 2026

Node infrastructure began failing. Community member vhhggy reported that CapsuleHub — the third-party marketplace for trading capsules — would be decommissioned on March 15, 2026. Users who had not claimed or sold their capsules before that date would lose their primary exit route.

> *"I officially shutdown all my nodes."* — cydereyesx, Mar 4, 2026

By April 2026, the server was functionally empty. The project showed no public leadership, no updates, and no evidence that Dr. William Nguyen had any intention of continuing operations.
