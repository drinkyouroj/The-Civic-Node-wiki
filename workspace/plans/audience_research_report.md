# Audience Research Report

## 1. WHO THEY ARE
They do not identify as “political junkies.” They see themselves as system interpreters or signal decoders.

- Age: 24–45 primary, 45–65 secondary
- Income: $80k–$250k core, $250k+ upper tier
- Fields: finance, tech, law, consulting
- Identity labels:
  - macro tourist
  - policy watcher
  - rates guy
  - liquidity analyst

Common self-descriptions:
- “I track flows, not opinions”
- “I map incentives behind decisions”
- “I care about plumbing, not headlines”

---

## 2. WHAT THEY WANT
They want predictive clarity about power.

Goals:
- Understand who actually controls outcomes
- Anticipate policy before announcements
- Build a mental model of how systems work

Success in 6–12 months:
- Explain events before reading analysis
- Replace news with frameworks
- Stop reacting late

---

## 3. WHAT’S STOPPING THEM
Main issue: noise disguised as analysis.

Pain points:
- Media lagging reality
- Too many signals, no hierarchy
- Lack of synthesis
- Distrust of incentives

Quotes:
- “Everyone’s narrating after the fact”
- “You read 10 threads and still don’t know what matters”
- “It’s all vibes until it breaks”

---

## 4. THE LANGUAGE THEY USE
Tone: cynical, compressed, signal-focused

Common phrases:
- “Flows > narratives”
- “Watch liquidity”
- “Everything is downstream of rates”
- “Incentives explain everything”

In-jokes:
- priced in
- pivot soon
- soft landing cope
- Fed put

---

## 5. WHAT THEY SPEND MONEY ON
They pay for information density.

Typical spend:
- $10–$50: newsletters
- $100–$500: research tools
- $1k+: institutional data

They avoid:
- basic explainers
- recycled news
- influencer finance content

---

## 6. HOW THEY CONSUME CONTENT
They build custom stacks.

Platforms:
- X (Twitter)
- Reddit
- Substack
- Podcasts

Behavior:
- Scan constantly
- Trust selectively
- Save long-form
- Cross-check sources

---

## 7. COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE
Most explain events, not systems.

Gaps:
- No unified power map
- Weak incentive analysis
- Little cross-domain synthesis
- Poor explanation of decision mechanics

---

## 8. WHAT SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE
They want clarity and control.

External:
- Anticipate moves early
- Speak with confidence

Internal:
- Less confusion
- More conviction

---

## DAY IN THE LIFE
Morning: scan X, discard most  
Midday: read Reddit threads  
Afternoon: listen to macro podcast  
Evening: read deep analysis  
Night: simulate scenarios  

---

## KEY INSIGHTS
1. They buy better models, not information  
2. Trust comes from accuracy over time  
3. Synthesis beats access  
4. Incentives matter more than statements  
5. Clarity is the real payoff  

---

## LANGUAGE STRATEGY

Use:
- Follow the incentives
- Watch this, not headlines
- Second-order effects

Avoid:
- Here’s what you need to know
- Game-changing insight
- Experts say
