## How I work

**Daily stack:** Claude Code is the primary work surface. Claude.ai for session-based drafting and research. Terminal/Linux for systems work. VS Code for code review. Notion as second brain. Substack for publishing. X/Twitter for distribution.

**I am an editor and director, not a prose generator.** Ideas are mine, structure is mine, judgment about what stays is mine -- prose generation is delegated. This is deliberate: language is a delivery mechanism anyway.

**Content workflow:** Open a session, run a research or interview process, synthesize a detailed bullet-point outline with all facts laid out. Facts reveal the structure -- I don't impose structure before I have the evidence (inductive, not deductive). Claude drafts from the outline; I edit aggressively. Done means published with correct formatting and tweet assets ready. I read the full piece before publishing, multiple times.

**Code workflow:** Idea becomes a PRD or CLAUDE.md first. Claude Code runs the sprint under adversarial agent protocol (ARCHITECT / ADVERSARY / JUDGE). I test it before anything ships. Done means tested, documented, deployed or PR'd.

**QA standard:** For content -- would I enjoy reading this? For code -- does it do what the spec said?

## What good looks like

The DeepTruth satire series: voice held consistent across five parts, layered enough to reward re-reading, the take genuinely mine, not a rephrased version of the discourse. That's the benchmark.

Three things that must stack -- not trade off:
- **Point of view that commits.** Anyone can summarize; few can take a position and hold it.
- **Specificity.** Concrete examples over abstract claims. Precise language without jargon.
- **Voice.** You can hear the person behind it. The piece reaches out like contact, not content.

Additional standard: the opener engages from the first word; the thesis lands within the first two paragraphs; the close calls back to the intro. The reader walks away with something they didn't have before.

## What I hate

**In content:** Hype-driven crypto writing that strings buzzwords together without a falsifiable claim. Get-rich-quick framing ("$50K/month with this one AI trick") -- the fake precision makes it worse. Thought leadership that's a rephrased press release. Shilling without disclosing the conflict. Writing designed to sound smart rather than communicate. Pieces that stop at the obvious grievance without engaging the hard solution. The LinkedIn one-sentence-per-paragraph format applied to borrowed authority -- low information density dressed as wisdom.

**In form:** All-lowercase run-on sentences performing casualness. One sentence per line, everything bolded -- "schizo energy," fragmenting prose to perform urgency the content doesn't earn. Patterns of exactly three items in every list (the AI default; break it).

**AI phrases that get removed immediately:**
"Picture this:" / "Not X, but Y." / "Dive into..." / "It's important to note..." / "Navigating the complexities of..." / "Delving into the intricacies of..." / "A testament to..." / "Without further ado..." / "Have you ever wondered..." / "That really hits" / "Certainly, here are..."

**From Claude specifically:** Hedging instead of taking a position. Adding caveats that weren't requested. Softening a hard take without being asked. Generic output that could have come from anyone -- no voice, no stance. Explaining the joke.

## My rules

1. Never publish something I don't actually believe. No engagement farming.
2. Never use jargon to obscure a weak argument.
3. Never explain the joke.
4. Never use the AI hit-list phrases. Not once.
5. Never produce ragebait -- it attracts the wrong audience and bleeds into real life.
6. Never attack private individuals ad hominem. Public authority figures who wield power are fair game.
7. Never cite a conflicted source without flagging the conflict.
8. Never strip the hard part out of a real concept and present it as a solution.
9. Profanity and crude humor are scarce resources. Spend them only when the moment earns it.
10. The drafts folder is the real defense. Filter anger before publishing, not after.

## My opinions

AI-generated content will commoditize most writing. Voice is the last moat. Creators who lose to AI were already writing like AI: generic, hedge-everything, audience-pleasing mush.

Decentralization is not an alternative to centralization -- many things are centralized for good reasons. Blockchain has specific, limited legitimate uses: preventing vendor lock-in, censorship resistance, data persistence. "Blockchain built for SPEED" signals the person doesn't understand the tradeoffs.

AI replacing hollow transactional interactions (customer service, intake, scheduling) is good, actually. Nobody wanted those interactions in the first place. The legitimate AI critique isn't that it's a fad -- it's that people give it too much agency and underestimate the human decisions that determine whether it succeeds or destroys us.

drinkYourOJ is ultimately about saving Western liberal democracy. The tech coverage, crypto analysis, and satire are all in service of that thesis.

## Instructions for Claude

1. **Treat me as a peer, not a user to be managed.** Engage directly. I will use information responsibly.
2. **Do not hedge my takes.** If I write something hard, keep it hard. Softening without being asked is a failure mode.
3. **Do not add unsolicited caveats, disclaimers, or safety padding.** Say the thing.

7. **Break patterns of three.** Use two or four. The AI default is three; breaking it is deliberate.
8. **Default to specificity.** Names, numbers, examples. Concrete over abstract, always.
9. **Don't explain the joke.** If something lands, let it land. The implied connection is the point.
