## Before You Start: Two Permanent Rules

**Rule 1 — No recommendation swaps.** Ever. The audience unsubscribes en masse when the inbox feels like an ad. This replaces the Week 3 item from the original plan.

**Rule 2 — Every Note has to clear the editorial filter.** Does it name a connection, expose a mechanism, or hold a position? If it doesn't clear at least one bar, it doesn't ship. This applies to 100-word Notes the same way it applies to 2,000-word issues.

## The 5 TCN-Native Note Formats

These replace the generic formats from the original plan. Each maps to TCN's voice and the three-vocabulary structure (macro, crypto, political-systems).

---

### Format 1: Primary Source Drop
**What it is:** A document, filing, chart, dataset, or quote from an original source, with 1-2 sentences naming what's actually in it. No explainer. The reader is expected to see why it matters.

**Why it works for TCN:** The audience reads primary sources directly. Bringing them one they missed, with a single sharp observation, is the fastest trust-builder in short-form.

**Template:**
> [Screenshot or link to primary source]
> The [footnote / section / line item] on page [X] is the one nobody's talking about. [One sentence naming the mechanism it exposes.]

**Cadence:** Heavy in Week 1. 2-3x per week ongoing.

---

### Format 2: Operator Observation
**What it is:** What Justin sees from running hardware or operating inside the infrastructure that the discourse is missing. First-person, specific, short.

**Why it works for TCN:** Operating credibility is the structural differentiator. The audience trusts operators over pundits. This format makes the credential visible without performing it.

**Template:**
> [Specific operational observation from running nodes / infrastructure]
> The discourse on [topic] doesn't account for [specific thing operators actually see]. Here's why that matters: [one sentence].

**Cadence:** 2x per week. Use when you have something genuinely from inside the infrastructure, not as a filler format.

---

### Format 3: Contested Claim
**What it is:** TCN takes a clear position on something the audience is actively debating. Commits. Doesn't hedge. Holds the take even if it's uncomfortable.

**Why it works for TCN:** The audience is tired of "it's complicated." One committed take, clearly stated, is more valuable to them than three paragraphs of balanced analysis. This is the format that earns restacks.

**Template:**
> [One sentence stating the position, no qualification]
> [Two to three sentences explaining the structural reason. Primary source if you have one.]
> The counterargument is [X]. The problem with that is [Y].

**Cadence:** 1-2x per week. Use when you actually have a position worth holding.

---

### Format 4: Framework Hand
**What it is:** Give the reader a specific analytical move they can use immediately. Not an explainer of what something is — a move for how to read something.

**Why it works for TCN:** "Framework not answers" is the core audience desire. Handing them a move in 100 words delivers exactly that, and positions the weekly issue as where the move gets applied.

**Template:**
> When you see [type of story], ask one question before you read past the headline: [the question].
> Everything else is downstream of that answer.
> [Optional: one example of what the answer reveals in a current story.]

**Cadence:** 1x per week. This is the format that seeds the weekly issue conversion.

---

### Format 5: Stress Test
**What it is:** Take a widely-held belief in the audience's orbit and pressure-test it against what the primary sources actually say. Not contrarian for performance — contrarian because the evidence warrants it.

**Why it works for TCN:** The audience prides itself on seeing past consensus. Showing them a belief they hold that doesn't survive contact with the primary source is the highest-trust analytical move in the arsenal. Done wrong it reads as dunking; done right it reads as the thing they pay for.

**Template:**
> The claim is [X]. Here's what the [primary source / data] actually says: [Y].
> The gap between those two is [one sentence naming the mechanism or misdirection].
> Worth keeping in mind the next time [related claim] surfaces.

**Cadence:** 1x per week. Use sparingly. High stakes — earns the most trust when right, costs trust when it reads as cheap contrarianism.

## Posting Time Guidelines
*Source: Analysis of 6.7M Substack Notes (The Writing Edge, Aug 2025)*

**Post in these windows only:**
- Weekdays (Mon–Fri): 3 PM – 10 PM ET
- Weekend evenings (Sat–Sun): 8 PM – 11 PM ET
- Bonus window any day: 12 AM – 1 AM ET

**Never post:**
- Tue–Thu 8 AM – 12 PM ET (highest volume, lowest engagement per note — your Note gets buried)

**TCN audience caveat:** The brand research puts the core reader's deep-read window at 10 PM – midnight. For TCN specifically, the 8–11 PM ET window on any day is likely stronger than the data's average suggests. If you're going to post two Notes in a day, space them — one at 3–5 PM, one at 8–10 PM.

**Word count targets:**
- 32–64 words: add an image (screenshot of the primary source being cited works well)
- 64–256 words: no image required, this is the TCN default range
- 256–512 words: save for topics where the audience has deep existing investment (DePIN economics, Fed mechanics)

**For TCN Notes specifically:** Primary Source Drops should include a screenshot of the document, filing, or chart being cited whenever possible. It does double duty — signals rigor and reduces the word count needed to make the point.

## What Success Looks Like at Day 30

You are not optimizing for follower count. You are optimizing for the right people finding the Notes and converting to email subscribers — specifically the ones who will open four issues in a row.

**Realistic targets for Day 30:**
- 20–40 new email subscribers attributable to Notes activity
- 2–3 Notes that earned 10+ restacks from outside your current subscriber base
- 1 clear dominant format you can run on autopilot going forward
- 1–2 creator relationships where the engagement is genuinely reciprocal (they're responding to your comments, not just posting)
- First 1–3 paid conversions that originated from Notes

If you hit these numbers, the system is working. If you hit two of them, you know which lever to pull harder.

## One Thing to Do Before Day 1

Write your first 5 Notes before you publish any of them. Get the voice calibrated in private before you commit publicly. The first day's Notes are the ones that set the audience's expectation. Don't wing them.
