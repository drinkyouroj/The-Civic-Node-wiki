# Opener: You Own the Hotspot. Nova Labs Owns What It Earns.

**Template:** System Audit | **Trigger:** Named Hypocrisy | **Technique:** Counterintuitive Fact

---

## Selected Opener — Variant B (LOCKED)

A pro outdoor Helium hotspot costs $949. As of last August, after the network's halving, a well-placed urban unit earned between $4 and $8 a month. At $8 a month, the hardware pays itself off in ten years. At $4 a month, in twenty.

The pricing that determines what those hotspots earn is set by Nova Labs, the company that operates Helium Mobile and negotiates carrier offload deals with T-Mobile and AT&T. That authority was granted by a governance vote in April 2025. The vote had a 1-year sunset. The sunset arrived in April 2026. No replacement was filed.

Helium hotspot operators bought a franchise. They were sold a business. The hardware they paid for is theirs. The pricing authority that determines what their hardware earns is not.

---

**Contract:** "Ten years. Twenty." — The payback math from ¶1 is what the close returns to. The proposed closing line ("does the math before the hardware ships, not after") echoes the opener's math directly — the math that should have been done first is the governance check, not the earnings estimate. The opener creates the math; the close reframes which math matters.

## Rejected Variant — A (Uncomfortable Truth)

> A vote in April 2025 handed one company unilateral pricing authority over a 385,000-hotspot wireless network. The vote had a 1-year sunset. A year passed. >
> The operators of those 385,000 hotspots paid for the hardware out of their own pockets. A basic indoor box costs $249. A pro outdoor setup runs $949. >
> Helium hotspot operators bought a franchise. They were sold a business. The hardware they paid for is theirs. The pricing authority that determines what their hardware earns is not.

**Contract (corrected):** "Renewed itself by silence." — The opener introduces silence as the mechanism of extension: pricing authority that persists through the absence of a replacement vote, not through any affirmative act. The close must return to this image, transformed — naming the operator who does not stay silent.

**Reason rejected:** Variant B leads with personal stakes (the payback math) before the governance finding. Closer callback is structurally cleaner — "does the math before the hardware ships, not after" echoes the ten-years/twenty number directly.
