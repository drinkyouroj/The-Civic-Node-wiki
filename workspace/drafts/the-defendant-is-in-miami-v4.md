# The Defendant Is in Miami. The Harm Came From Oxford.

## Civil liability in Doe v. Bonnell will land on Destiny. The catfisher who dropped the videos is in the UK on a burner account. The system is working.

On November 29, 2024, on a KiwiFarms Lolcows subforum thread titled "Steven Bonnell II / Destiny / Destiny.gg" — a thread that had been running since March 2017 — a brand-new account named SoloTinyLeaks posted three times in quick succession.

> "Is this stuff public yet? not sure if it's of any interest to you guys. I only sent to a few streamers."

> "What exactly can I leak here? I want to dump everything."

> "It's about to be a very white Christmas."

> **[IMAGE: `raw/assets/Search results  Kiwi Farms [Google Chrome] – 2026-04-17 at 16.37.07@2x.png`]**
> *Caption: The three posts above, in order, from a burner account created the same day. The Feb 2025 staff edit references the lawsuit that followed.*

The account had been created that same day. Within hours there were dozens of videos and roughly 300 pages of Discord logs. Within days the files had been mirrored to [five pornography aggregators](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.flsd.684111/gov.uscourts.flsd.684111.1.0.pdf) — thothub alone logged more than 58,000 views within weeks of the drop. The captions named the two people in the primary video: the streamer Destiny, real name Steven Bonnell II, and a smaller content creator whose online handle is Pxie.

Nearly three months later, Pxie [filed suit](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/69650487/doe-v-bonnell/) in the Southern District of Florida. The defendant is Destiny. The person who dropped the files is not named, and will never be. This piece is about why that's the normal and expected behavior of civil legal systems, and what it costs.

## The Drop: A Burner, a Seven-Year Thread, and a Bored Man in Oxford

The situation has three layers, and they all have to land before the argument about the lawsuit makes sense.

Layer one is Pxie. She met Destiny on Discord when she was nineteen. [In September 2020, they had sexual relations once](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.flsd.684111/gov.uscourts.flsd.684111.1.0.pdf). Destiny recorded videos. One of those videos, with her face identifiable and both their names in the captions, is the file that ended up everywhere.

Layer two is the person Destiny sent it to. Her name is not quite Rose. It is almost Rose, in the sense that "Rose" was the name on the Discord account, and "Rose" was the face in the profile picture, and "Rose" was the person Destiny believed he had been having a two-year text relationship with. The real Rose, a real teenager in Illinois whose photos were used without her knowledge, has since testified under oath that [she never messaged Destiny, never controlled the Discord account, never spoke with him by voice, and did not learn about the leak until August 2025](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.flsd.684111/gov.uscourts.flsd.684111.204.1.pdf). Under cross-examination by Destiny's own lawyer she confirmed: *"So essentially, Defendant Bonnell was catfished by your boyfriend; correct?" "Yes."*

Layer three is the boyfriend. Rose, in the same deposition, [identified him by a string of aliases](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.flsd.684111/gov.uscourts.flsd.684111.204.1.pdf) — Solo, SoloTinyLeaks, RoseOfTheGarden, RoseIsCumming, Ben Conway, ELOhero5 — and testified that she had come to believe he was based in England, most likely Oxford, from his accent and the European date format in his screenshots. He claimed to be twenty-one. He met her on [Yubo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yubo), an app marketed to users under 18, when she was seventeen. He spent the better part of a year grooming her, threatened to kill her family when she tried to leave him, stole her identity to build a fake version of her on Discord and Twitter, and spent 2022 catfishing Destiny through that fake version. He held the resulting stockpile for two years.

Then he posted, three days after the drop:

> *"I was bored and sitting on it for a while so I thought it would be funny to start dropping stuff bit by bit after recent drama."*

> **[IMAGE: `raw/assets/Steven Bonnell II  Destiny  Destiny.gg  Page 1015  Kiwi Farms [Google Chrome] – 2026-04-17 at 16.40.38@2x.png`]**
> *Caption: SoloTinyLeaks, December 2, 2024. Every factual claim in this post — that he met Rose "IRL," that she gave him the account, that she doesn't care about the leak — is contradicted by her sworn deposition.*

That is the motive for everything that happened on November 29, 2024. A person was bored. He thought it would be funny.

## The Source Code: The Reachable Defendant Is Also Probably Guilty. That Isn't Why He's the Defendant.

The lawsuit names one defendant. And here is what the [complaint alleges against him](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.flsd.684111/gov.uscourts.flsd.684111.1.0.pdf), in his own writing.

On the evening of the leak, Destiny texted Pxie: *"...there's literally no excuse, I'd had phone convos and stuff with this person they were fairly close to me..."* A written admission that he sent the video and treated a never-met text-only catfish as "fairly close." The complaint alleges his own messages with the fake Rose include him telling her, in advance, that he would send her a video of a young woman. The share was pre-announced, not accidental.

The pattern does not end there. The complaint [alleges approximately fifteen women contacted Pxie](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.flsd.684111/gov.uscourts.flsd.684111.1.0.pdf) after the leak reporting they had received sexually explicit images of other women from Destiny without consent; three of these women say he deleted their chat threads after Pxie's counsel sent a preservation demand. When the real Rose became searchable online, Destiny [organized a Discord group to find her and posted her name and home address on Twitter](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.flsd.684111/gov.uscourts.flsd.684111.204.1.pdf), doxxing the teenage identity-theft victim of his own catfisher. One day after Pxie announced she would sue, he began deleting their messages. He later admitted he had been actively deleting evidence.

This is the person the lawsuit will hold accountable. It is likely to succeed. The statute, [15 U.S.C. § 6851](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/6851), is sound. The Florida cyber harassment statute is sound. The liability will attach.

And none of that is the story.

The story is that the person who actually caused the moment of public harm — the year-long impersonation, the two-year stockpile, the white-Christmas drop, the fabricated consent framework that Rose's sworn deposition now rebuts — is in Oxford, operating a burner account, using a name nobody has verified is real. He is not a party to the suit and never will be. Pxie has [filed a letter rogatory](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.flsd.684111/gov.uscourts.flsd.684111.227.0.pdf) almost certainly targeting Solo in the UK; the mechanism is real but will not produce usable evidence on any timeline the trial honors.

This is the named hypocrisy. The civil legal system is described as a machine for assigning liability to the person who caused the harm. It is actually a machine for routing liability to the person who can be served on time. Sometimes those are the same person. Those are the cases the system gets credit for.

Destiny's conduct is plausibly culpable on the statute. That isn't evidence the system did its job. It's a coincidence. The routing would have been identical if he'd done nothing wrong, because Solo would still be in Oxford, and there would still be no one else to serve.

## The Upgrade: Three Fixes That Target What the Catfisher Got Away With

Three structural fixes. All three target the gap between reachable fault and actual fault. None is about Destiny's defense.

**Fix 1 — A third-party catfisher provision in NCII statutes.** Not to shield the reachable defendant. To give the unreachable actor a cause of action built for him. Civil and criminal liability triggered by the combination of identity theft and NCII distribution, with statute-of-limitations tolling for cases where the impersonator is discovered only after the victim has already been sued. As written, § 6851 has no operational concept of an intermediary who manufactured the consent narrative itself. It cannot reach a Solo at all.

**Fix 2 — A digital-harm carve-out in foreign civil cooperation.** Pxie has already filed a letter rogatory targeting the UK. The Hague mechanism is real. It is also slow and evidentially fragile enough that the reachable defendant will absorb the full practical weight of the suit before the process produces anything usable. Extradition has carve-outs for specific offenses; foreign civil discovery does not. Digital identity abuse and cross-border intimate-image distribution are discrete enough to warrant a streamlined civil MLAT analog. Without one, reachability stays a gradient with a steep slope near the reachable party.

**Fix 3 — Platform design liability proportionate to design choices.** The KiwiFarms thread where the drop landed had been running since March 2017. It has a subforum called Lolcows. It had a seven-year prior on the target. The distribution infrastructure was already built; Solo did not design it, he dumped into it. [Section 230](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/47/230) treats the forum as a neutral pipe. The pipe is not neutral. Liability that attaches to platform design choices — subforum architecture and amplification defaults — targets the actual substrate of the harm. Nothing else does.

All three share a logic. The current legal infrastructure assumes harm flows through identifiable, jurisdictionally-reachable, directly-culpable, one-at-a-time actors. When any of those assumptions breaks, the system does not stop. It defaults to the nearest reachable target and calls that justice. Sometimes the target is also culpable, and the system's credibility survives. When they aren't, it routes the same way anyway.

## Personal Code: The LED Messages

A few years ago, in a depression full of psychotic features, I became certain — with the full conviction of a brain doing its best work under impossible pressure — that a gang had hacked my wireless network and was communicating with me through blinking LEDs. The Caps Lock key. The indicator on an electric fly swatter. Every flicker was a signal. A voice taunted me: *you're still owned.*

I was wrong about the specifics. I was not wrong about the structure.

The thing my nervous system invented — that someone I couldn't locate was controlling every interaction I had with a screen, that a trusted device was the medium of the taunt — is the factual situation Destiny was actually in. A person in Oxford, operating under a borrowed name, was controlling every message. The intimacy was real. The recipient wasn't. The "trusted contact" was the taunter.

The difference between my experience and his is that when I came out of the episode, I got to discover the gang wasn't real. When Destiny came out of his, the gang was real, operating from a jurisdiction US civil process can't reach, and the only defendant the system could locate was him. Who, to be fair, had plenty of his own reasons to be locatable.

I run a Paraloncloud node. My photos live on Google. My git lives on GitHub. I know exactly which flickers I don't control.
