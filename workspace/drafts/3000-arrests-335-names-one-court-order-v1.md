# 3,000 Arrests, 335 Names, One Court Order

## DHS's own "Worst of the Worst" registry lists 11% of the people Operation Metro Surge claims to have arrested. There is no public roster of the agents who arrested them. On May 1, a federal judge is forcing one piece of the record into the open.

The Department of Homeland Security says Operation Metro Surge made [more than 3,000 arrests](https://www.northernnewsnow.com/2026/02/09/i-mapped-every-confirmed-ice-arrest-minnesota-heres-what-i-found/) in the Minneapolis-St. Paul metro area between December 4, 2025 and February 2026. Its own ["Worst of the Worst" registry](https://www.northernnewsnow.com/2026/02/09/i-mapped-every-confirmed-ice-arrest-minnesota-heres-what-i-found/) lists 335 of them. There is no public roster of the agents who carried out any of those arrests.

On May 1, a federal judge will compel the Department to produce one ICE agent's [personnel file, body-cam footage, and fitness records](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/jan/18/justice-department-ice-renee-good-george-floyd-minneapolis). It will be the first piece of the operation's contemporaneous record any branch of the federal government has forced into public view. This piece is an audit of what is on the record and what is missing from it; May 1 will change both columns.

---

## The Glitch: A Federal Judge Is Doing the Investigation Nobody Else Will

On January 7, 2026, at 9:37 a.m., on Portland Avenue South in Minneapolis, ICE agent Jonathan Ross fired three shots in under one second at Renée Good. She was 37, a writer and poet, a U.S. citizen, and she had just dropped her son at school. All three shots hit. She died at the scene. Federal officers then [blocked a bystander identifying himself as a physician](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/jan/18/justice-department-ice-renee-good-george-floyd-minneapolis) from approaching her, and blocked ambulances when they arrived. The Department of Justice's Civil Rights Division [declined to open a criminal investigation](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/jan/18/justice-department-ice-renee-good-george-floyd-minneapolis). Six federal prosecutors in Minnesota's district resigned in protest, alongside the Civil Rights Division's criminal section chief, principal deputy chief, deputy chief, and acting deputy chief.

The May 1 court order does not arrive in any of those proceedings. It arrives in the defense motion of an unrelated case — the federal prosecution of a man named Roberto Carlos Muñoz-Guatemala, in which Jonathan Ross is the alleged victim of an assault during a separate June 2025 arrest. (Conflating Muñoz-Guatemala with the Good killing is how misinformation enters this story; they are two different proceedings, and Ross's role in each is opposite.) The court ordering Ross's records is doing the work that the federal accountability mechanism declined to do.

The structure is now visible from the outside. The agency publishes what it cannot prove, and walks back what it can no longer defend. The Worst of the Worst registry is the published version. The 89% of arrests not named in any public document are the part that is not on the public record. What follows is what is on the record so far, who put it there, and what May 1 will or will not add.

## The Source Code: What the Command Chain Actually Authorized

The federal government's constitutional position here is not frivolous. Federal preemption of state immigration enforcement is settled doctrine. The Supremacy Clause is real. The *In re Neagle* line of cases, decided in 1890, has held for 135 years that federal officers acting within the scope of official duty are not subject to state prosecution for that conduct. Any audit that does not concede this is not an audit; it is a brief. So: charitable read first. The doctrinal terrain is real. The conduct on it is what this piece audits.

[Gregory Bovino](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/border-patrol-gregory-bovino-retire-immigration-enforcement-rcna263751), the Border Patrol commander who ran Operation Metro Surge on the ground, reported directly to DHS Secretary Kristi Noem and to Noem's senior adviser Corey Lewandowski. That is not a normal chain of command for a Border Patrol commander; that is a political-operations reporting line. A federal judge in Chicago [found Bovino had "repeatedly lied"](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/border-patrol-gregory-bovino-retire-immigration-enforcement-rcna263751) about threats from immigrants and protesters during his prior operations there. In one specific incident, Bovino claimed he threw a gas canister after being hit by a rock; he had to walk the claim back after the video evidence contradicted him. He was [removed from his role as CBP commander-at-large](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/border-patrol-gregory-bovino-retire-immigration-enforcement-rcna263751) in January 2026 after the deaths of Renée Good and Alex Pretti, and is [retiring at the end of March 2026](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/border-patrol-gregory-bovino-retire-immigration-enforcement-rcna263751) with full pension and no charges. DHS, asked by NBC News about misconduct, replied with boilerplate: "When we discover any alleged or potential misconduct, we immediately refer it for investigation and cooperate fully." Bovino's referral resolved on his birthday.

Up the chain: Acting ICE Director Todd Lyons reported to Border Czar Tom Homan. Homan publicly linked the duration of the Minnesota surge to state and local "cooperation" and to the repeal of Minneapolis and St. Paul sanctuary ordinances. AG Pam Bondi sent Governor Walz a letter demanding access to state records and the same sanctuary repeal. The structural fact under the legal arguments is this: federal enforcement deployment was being conditioned on state policy change. Not enforcement. Coercion of policy through enforcement.

The state response was four layers, not four incidents. First, federal officers blocked the physician and the ambulances at the scene of the Good shooting. Second, state investigators were excluded from the scene the next day. Third, Hennepin County DA Mary Moriarty filed first-of-its-kind state assault charges against a federal ICE agent — [Gregory Donnell Morgan, on April 16, 2026](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/jan/18/justice-department-ice-renee-good-george-floyd-minneapolis), for second-degree assault stemming from a February 5 incident in which Morgan pointed a gun at civilian drivers. Fourth, on March 24, 2026, Minnesota AG Keith Ellison and Hennepin County filed a federal lawsuit, *Minnesota v. DOJ/DHS*, seeking evidence in three federal-officer shootings during the surge. Each layer is documented. Together, they are the federalism story under the immigration story.

The retcon ran on two timers, in two registers. On January 18, 2026, Deputy AG Todd Blanche told Fox News [that DOJ does not investigate killings like the Good shooting](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/jan/18/justice-department-ice-renee-good-george-floyd-minneapolis) because, as Blanche put it: "we don't just go out and investigate every time an officer is forced to defend himself against somebody putting his life in danger. We never do." Six years earlier, after George Floyd was killed in Minneapolis, the first Trump DOJ under AG William Barr [opened a federal civil rights investigation in three days](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/jan/18/justice-department-ice-renee-good-george-floyd-minneapolis); that probe led to four officers' federal civil rights convictions, including Derek Chauvin's. "We never do" is contradicted by the Department's own documented Floyd response in the same city, under the same political party, six years apart. Blanche was not asked to reconcile the two on Fox. The Noem retcon ran shorter. On January 24, the day Alex Pretti was killed by federal agents, Noem said at a press briefing that Pretti had "attacked" agents and was "wishing to inflict harm." By January 30, on Fox News, the scene was ["chaotic"](https://www.goodmorningamerica.com/news/story/former-officials-dhs-tactics-undermine-public-trust-after-129717921), no evidence offered for the original claim, and the investigative authority for the case had quietly shifted from "DHS investigating with FBI assistance" to "FBI is now leading." The walk-back was not announced. It was just incompatible with the original statement and was therefore replaced.

The structural retcon is older. DHS [disabled its automatic text-archiving system in April 2025](https://americanoversight.org/dhs-admits-it-provided-erroneous-information-on-texts-of-noem-and-dhs-brass-hasnt-confirmed-it-followed-law/) and told the watchdog group American Oversight in July that Noem's text messages could not be searched. In November, [DHS admitted in court](https://americanoversight.org/dhs-admits-it-provided-erroneous-information-on-texts-of-noem-and-dhs-brass-hasnt-confirmed-it-followed-law/) that the prior claim was "erroneous." NARA ordered DHS on September 3 to investigate potential unauthorized records destruction; as of October 30, DHS had not responded. The same agency's [Office of Civil Rights and Civil Liberties went from 147 staff to fewer than 40](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/mar/08/dhs-oversight-court-record-review); its Office of the Immigration Detention Ombudsman went from 118 to five. CRCL investigated one of approximately ten in-custody deaths in 2025, the [deadliest year in U.S. immigration detention in more than 20 years](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/mar/08/dhs-oversight-court-record-review). The acting ombudsman of the detention office [had never seen the standards manual](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/mar/08/dhs-oversight-court-record-review) for the facilities he was supposed to be overseeing.

## The Upgrade: Why Minnesota's Counter-Architecture Won't Stop Here

This section is not a political endorsement. It is a structural observation: when one accountability layer fails, other layers activate, and what they activate looks different from what the failed layer was doing.

Hennepin County DA Mary Moriarty has 14 open criminal investigations into federal-agent conduct during the surge. She is the first state DA in the modern era to bring state-level charges against federal ICE agents for on-duty conduct. Minnesota AG Keith Ellison's federal lawsuit names the doctrines at issue: Anti-Commandeering, Defensive Immunity, Supremacy Clause Immunity. Judge Katherine Menendez denied Minnesota's preliminary injunction in January, calling the consequences ["profound and even heartbreaking" and "difficult to overstate."](https://www.northernnewsnow.com/2026/02/09/i-mapped-every-confirmed-ice-arrest-minnesota-heres-what-i-found/) The denial was on a doctrinal gap, not the merits — Menendez noted that the Supreme Court has provided "little guidance" on applying anti-commandeering to enforcement-deployment cases as opposed to statutory commands. That gap is the legal terrain Minnesota is now testing.

The other layer is evidentiary. Seven federal circuit courts have affirmed the First Amendment right to record law enforcement; in December 2025, DHS [publicly stated](https://reason.com/2025/12/22/dhs-says-recording-or-following-law-enforcement-sure-sounds-like-obstruction-of-justice/) that doing so "sure sounds like obstruction of justice." The bystander video that contradicted Trump's claim that Renée Good "ran over" Ross only exists because of the doctrine DHS is now contesting. Federal "assault on ICE" prosecutions have been [collapsing in real time](https://reason.com/2025/12/22/dhs-says-recording-or-following-law-enforcement-sure-sounds-like-obstruction-of-justice/) — grand juries declining to indict, US Attorney's Offices dropping cases after reviewing the evidence. The judicial system is overruling the executive position one case at a time, while the Cato Institute's David Bier documents the [pattern of intimidation](https://reason.com/2025/12/22/dhs-says-recording-or-following-law-enforcement-sure-sounds-like-obstruction-of-justice/) in writing.

The operation has already failed by metrics that don't depend on the constitutional questions. The City of Minneapolis estimates [$203.1 million in one-month total impact](https://www.northernnewsnow.com/2026/02/09/i-mapped-every-confirmed-ice-arrest-minnesota-heres-what-i-found/), including $81M in business revenue losses and $47M in lost wages. An [independent econometric study](https://www.northernnewsnow.com/2026/02/09/i-mapped-every-confirmed-ice-arrest-minnesota-heres-what-i-found/) estimated $106.1M in lost wages in the metro from Jan 3 through Feb 17 alone. ICE favorability flipped from [+13 at inauguration to -9 by late January](https://www.dataforprogress.org/blog/2026/1/22/a-majority-of-voters-are-unfavorable-of-ice-are-divided-on-what-abolish-ice-means); 55% of voters say increased ICE funding is a bad use of taxpayer money; Independents are unfavorable to ICE by a 20-point margin. Data for Progress is a progressive pollster and the swing is large enough to invite scrutiny, but the methodology is in the deck and the numbers move in one direction across multiple polls. May 1 will produce one of two outcomes: DHS produces Ross's files, and the dossier gains a primary document; or DHS defies a federal court order, which is a different and larger constitutional confrontation. The piece doesn't depend on which.

## My Debug: What Happens When the Retcon Meets the Record

There is a difference between spin and retcon, and watching it play out in real time is the distinguishing feature of the past nine months. Spin works on the future. It tries to shape how the next thing will be received. Retcon tries to alter the past. It only fails when a primary document contradicts it — and the failure is total, immediate, and on the record. The Noem walk-back on Pretti is a retcon. The Blanche "we never do this" claim is a retcon, falsified by his own department's documented response in the same city six years earlier. Both attempts can be cited verbatim. Neither can be defended.

The institutional move that gives the rest of this away is the [April 2025 disabling of the DHS auto-archive](https://americanoversight.org/dhs-admits-it-provided-erroneous-information-on-texts-of-noem-and-dhs-brass-hasnt-confirmed-it-followed-law/). That was the same month the watchdog teams were ousted. It was the same month [Treasury Secretary Bessent and Secretary Noem signed the IRS-DHS data-sharing agreement](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/data-of-thousands-of-taxpayers-wrongly-shared-with-dhs-court-filing-says) that has now been found to have wrongly disclosed thousands of taxpayer records. Three federal records actions, in one month, all in the same direction. Reading these as separate stories was always going to age poorly.

The constitutional question this piece sits inside is broader than immigration. If a state cannot hold a federal agent accountable for on-duty conduct, and the federal mechanism declines to activate, there is a zone of unaccountability in American governance. The specific policy filling that zone today is immigration. The structural problem is not. We opened with a court order and a deadline. In a few days, we'll find out whether federal institutions still comply with federal courts. The documentation already exists either way. Mary Moriarty filed the first state assault charges against a federal ICE agent in the modern history of the United States. Who files the second?
