# DHS Showed 11%. FOIA Showed Everyone.

## DHS publicized 335 names from 3,789 arrests. ICE's own FOIA data for the full operation shows 63% had no criminal record.

When a bill arrives that's wildly out of proportion to the service rendered, you ask for the line items before you pay it. Minneapolis's February 2026 preliminary assessment read: lost business revenue, $81 million. Lost wages, $47 million (people afraid to leave home). Added rent assistance, $15.7 million. The city called it "very likely a significant underestimation." One month's total: $203.1 million. For an operation the city never authorized, from an agency that kept the evidence.

Operation Metro Surge, audited against ICE's own FOIA data, reads as enforcement built for maximum visibility. DHS named the target population: "vicious murderers, rapists, child pedophiles." ICE's own records show what the operation produced.

---

## The Glitch: Minneapolis got a $203 million bill it can't audit

The [bill grew](https://www.ag.state.mn.us/Office/Communications/2026/04/21_Metro-Surge.asp) before anyone called the invoice final. In April, Minnesota's attorney general filed an amended complaint using University of California San Diego survey data to expand the accounting metro-wide: over $240 million in lost wages, approximately $610 million in lost business revenue. The mechanism isn't complicated. Fear travels further than enforcement. Workers who stopped going to work showed up in the wage numbers. Customers who stopped crossing invisible lines showed up in the revenue numbers.

What the money bought: at least [3,789 arrests](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/03/31/ice-arrests-in-minnesota-three-quarters-of-arrestees-had-no-criminal-record-data-shows) over approximately 60 days. Two dead U.S. citizens: [Renée Good](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/live-updates/minneapolis-ice-agent-shooting-protesters-clash-fbi-investigation/), shot through her windshield by ICE agent Jonathan Ross after dropping her child at school, and Alex Pretti. One in-custody death. And by ICE's own data, fewer than one in four of those arrested had any criminal conviction at all.

When the yield is that far from the stated target, the auditor's job is to find out what was actually being produced.

## The source code: how "the worst of the worst" became a street sweep

Start with the charitable reading. [Secretary Noem's January 19 press release](https://www.dhs.gov/news/2026/01/19/ice-continues-remove-worst-worst-minneapolis-streets-dhs-law-enforcement-marks-3000) was specific: the operation targets "the worst of the worst," "criminal illegal aliens including vicious murderers, rapists, child pedophiles." The claim: 3,000 of them, arrested in six weeks. Take that at face value, then check it.

[ICE's own arrest records](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/03/31/ice-arrests-in-minnesota-three-quarters-of-arrestees-had-no-criminal-record-data-shows), obtained through FOIA litigation by the Deportation Data Project, cover the full 3,789 arrests. Fewer than 25% had any criminal conviction. About 13% had pending charges. About 63% had neither. The arrest method is its own story: 97% of all arrests were non-custodial street arrests, meaning people pulled from sidewalks, not removed from existing custody. ICE's conviction category counts misdemeanors down to traffic violations and unauthorized entry. Even the number that appears to support the stated rationale doesn't hold the "worst of the worst" claim.

A [widely-cited analysis](https://www.northernnewsnow.com/2026/02/09/i-mapped-every-confirmed-ice-arrest-minnesota-heres-what-i-found/) found 71% flagged for violent or sexual offenses. That figure is accurate. It covers 124 of 174 names, the metro slice of DHS's own published list, not the full operation. That published list runs 335 names: about 11% of the 3,000 arrests DHS claimed, under 9% of the 3,789 the FOIA data counted. DHS showed you the top 11%. The FOIA data shows you everyone.

Among everyone: [roughly 35% of arrests](https://minnesotareformer.com/2026/03/31/3700-immigrants-arrested-during-operation-metro-surge/) were ICE-labeled "collateral," meaning people encountered during targeted operations rather than the named targets. [Habeas filings (emergency court challenges to unlawful detention) confirmed 273 valid work-permit holders detained](https://minnesotareformer.com/2026/05/22/metro-surge-work-permits/): asylees, refugees, DACA recipients. That 273 is a floor. The FOIA dataset has no legal-status field and excludes U.S. citizens entirely.

Operations built to remove dangerous people are custodial: high record rates, narrow targets, people pulled from existing incarceration. Metro Surge ran 97% non-custodial street arrests, a swept population that was 63% no record by ICE's own accounting, and a publicly highlighted subset that bears no demographic resemblance to the full operation. Call it enforcement theater: maximum visibility, minimum precision, structural insulation from external review. The 97% street arrests are the visibility. The 63% no-record is the precision. The 11% it published, against the 89% it didn't, is the insulation.

[Chief U.S. District Judge Patrick Schiltz](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.mnd.230171/gov.uscourts.mnd.230171.10.0.pdf), presiding over *Juan T.R. v. Noem* (No. 0:26-cv-00107), documented 96 court-order violations in 74 cases on January 28. By February 26, his court had re-verified the count and added 113 more, reaching approximately 210 total. Schiltz threatened criminal contempt. His language: "ICE is not a law unto itself." The enforcement continued anyway.

## The upgrade: there's no law that makes them show you the evidence

When [ICE agent Jonathan Ross shot Renée Good through her windshield](https://www.propublica.org/article/minnesota-trump-ice-shooting-lawsuit-alex-pretti-renee-good), the FBI initially agreed to partner with Minnesota state investigators on the case. The FBI reversed course. It took exclusive control of the car, the forensic evidence, and the witness interviews, and locked Minnesota out without explanation. The reversal is documented. It was a deliberate change to an agreement that had already been made.

Minnesota's evidence request went to the same agency whose agent fired the gun. The agency decided what the state would receive.

There's no clean legal instrument that compels a federal agency to hand a state its evidence. The [Touhy regulations](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/340/462) (from *Touhy v. Ragen*, built on the Housekeeping Statute at 5 U.S.C. §301) let the federal agency head decide what evidence is released to outside parties, including states. DHS's response to Minnesota's request: Touhy doesn't apply here. The agency declared its own disclosure rules didn't apply to it. The Minnesota AG called that position legally baseless. The recourse is a federal lawsuit.

Joe Arpaio was pardoned in 2017 before his criminal contempt sentencing. Schiltz has the same two tools. Civil contempt is coercive: daily fines, and the president can't pardon it away. Criminal contempt is punitive and pardonable. You can use civil contempt to pressure compliance without that exposure; criminal contempt is ultimately within executive reach. Either way, enforcement of court orders runs through U.S. Marshals, who answer to the executive. The judiciary documents defiance. It can't unilaterally stop a federal enforcement operation.

The lever the president can't reach is a state criminal conviction. That's exactly why the administration moved to block state investigations before charges could be filed. Hennepin County Attorney [Mary Moriarty's assault charge against ICE officer Gregory Donnell Morgan](https://www.notus.org/immigration/minnesota-prosecutors-charge-ice-agent) tests whether the lever holds: the agent will almost certainly remove the case to federal court and assert [Supremacy Clause immunity](https://statecourtreport.org/our-work/analysis-opinion/when-can-states-prosecute-federal-agents), the doctrine that shields federal officers from state prosecution for on-duty actions. The cases that can defeat it are *Horiuchi* (Ruby Ridge) and *Drury v. Lewis* (1906, Pennsylvania): when the facts of the officer's use of force are genuinely disputed, immunity doesn't automatically attach at the pretrial stage.

Minnesota's building its record through what wasn't seized: civilian video, the medical examiner's findings, eyewitnesses, no statute of limitations on the death. States can weaponize process against federal operations, and that risk is real. It's not a reason to leave evidence custody self-administered by the agency that used the force.

## My debug: I moved back to Michigan to blend in

The newsletter keeps landing on the same logic in different registers. The AI grid premium on a power bill. A cost that arrives without a vote. Metro Surge runs that logic somewhere past money.

After the 2024 election, I left Seattle's Capitol Hill neighborhood and moved back to a suburb of Detroit. Michigan is a purple state. I moved there on purpose. The honest explanation: I moved back to Michigan to blend in. A suburb instead of a city center; objectively less likely to be anywhere near a demonstration. The result, as planned, is boring. Boring is the environment I wanted for this Trump term, not "excitement." (Turns out that's exactly what I got.)

What I was afraid of was retribution against the blue West Coast. Washington state specifically. What arrived was Metro Surge in Minnesota, the pattern I feared in someone else's state. The mechanism isn't identical: I expected something more like a military mobilization; what happened was an ICE and CBP operation. Close enough to confirm the fear. Different enough that I won't claim I called it.

Something I never wanted to be right about.

There are two responses to a bill you didn't vote for. You dispute it, or you get smaller so it might not find you. The move to Michigan was the second kind. I left because I wanted to be somewhere harder to find. That's a sentence I wouldn't have believed I'd write a decade ago.

---

The city of Minneapolis is still waiting for its itemized statement. The Good family is building its case from the evidence that wasn't seized: video, the medical examiner, witnesses, a civil lawsuit with no statute-of-limitations problem. ICE is sitting on the rest.

The city was billed for an operation it was never allowed to review. The agency set the terms of what the city could see. On its own authority, without a law that required it.

Minneapolis got the invoice. ICE kept the receipt.

What's a vote worth when the bill comes anyway and the receipt's sealed?
