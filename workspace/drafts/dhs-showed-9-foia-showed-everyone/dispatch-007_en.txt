… In January, the government named who it was hunting in Minneapolis. The worst of the worst. Vicious murderers, rapists, child pedophiles. They published a list of names. Then they arrested three thousand seven hundred eighty nine people.

Then ICE's own records came out, and sixty three percent had no criminal record…
Ninety seven percent were street arrests, pulled off sidewalks, not out of jail. These are ICE's own numbers…

It was called Operation Metro Surge, sixty days across the metro. The city never voted for it, never authorized it, and was never allowed to review it. When a bill shows up wildly bigger than what you supposedly bought, you ask for the line items first. Minneapolis got a two hundred three million dollar bill…for one month, and the agency that ran it is sitting on the evidence. Minneapolis got the invoice.
ICE kept the receipt…

Here's the itemized bill the city could put together. Lost business revenue, eighty one million dollars. Lost wages from people too scared to leave home, forty seven million dollars. Emergency rent help, fifteen point seven million dollars. One month, two hundred three million dollars.

By April, the state attorney general ran the whole metro, over two hundred forty million in lost wages, around six hundred ten million in lost business, fear travels further than the agents do, people stopped going to work, customers stopped crossing the street…

So what did the money buy? At least three thousand seven hundred eighty nine arrests. Two dead American citizens. Renee Goode shot through her windshield by an ICE agent after she dropped her child off at school, Alex Pretti, and one more who died in custody. Then the full records came out through a freedom of information lawsuit.
Fewer than one in four had any conviction. About thirteen percent had charges pending. Sixty three percent had neither, and the convictions they did count run down to traffic tickets. At least two hundred seventy three had valid work permits…

Now you might have seen a number going around. Seventy one percent flagged for violent or sexual offenses. That number is real, but it covers a hundred twenty four names. A slice of the list DHS published, three hundred thirty five names, out of three thousand seven hundred eighty nine arrests. That list is nine percent of the operation.
DHS showed you nine percent. It left out the other ninety one. The press release showed you nine percent. The data showed you everyone. Minneapolis got the invoice.
ICE kept the receipt…

A federal judge was watching all of this. Chief judge Patrick Schlitz. He was hearing the detention challenges. On January twenty eighth, he counted ninety six violations of his own orders. By late February, the count was around two hundred ten.
His words, ICE is not a law unto itself…
He threatened criminal contempt. The operation kept going anyway…

Go back to Renee Good. The FBI first agreed to investigate alongside Minnesota…then it reversed. It took the car, the forensic evidence, the witness interviews, and locked the state out. Minnesota's request went to the same agency whose agent fired the gun. There's an old rule, Tuohy, that lets a federal agency head decide what outsiders get.
Minnesota asked. DHS said Tuohy doesn't even apply here. The agency decided its own rules didn't bind it…
