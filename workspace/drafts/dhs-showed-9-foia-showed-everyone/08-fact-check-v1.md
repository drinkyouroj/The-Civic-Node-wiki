## Flagged Claims

### Claim #1 [HIGH]: The "11%" denominator — 335 is ~9% of 3,789, not 11%

**Article says (3 places):**
- Subhead: "DHS publicized 335 names from 3,789 arrests."
- §3: "It covers the 335 names DHS chose to publicize, approximately 11% of all 3,789 arrests. DHS showed you the top 11%."
- §3 close: "The 11%/89% gap between what DHS published and what FOIA showed is the insulation."

**Source says** (Davidson / Northern News Now, the `mapping-analysis` source): "DHS claims 3,000+ arrests... only 335 names appear in publicly-disclosed federal records (≈11%)." The 11% is **335 ÷ ~3,000 (what DHS *claimed*)**, not 335 ÷ 3,789 (what FOIA *documented*).

**Discrepancy:** 335 / 3,789 = **8.8%**, not 11%. The draft repeatedly pairs "335" with the 3,789 FOIA total and then calls it 11%. A reader who does the arithmetic (Marcus does) gets 8.8% and the headline number stops reconciling. The 11% only holds against the 3,000 DHS *claimed*.

**Recommendation:** Anchor the 11% to the 3,000 DHS claim everywhere, and stop implying 335 ÷ 3,789. The honest version actually *strengthens* the piece because both denominators are damning:
> "...the 335 names DHS chose to publicize — about 11% of the 3,000 arrests DHS claimed, and under 9% of the 3,789 the FOIA data counted."

The headline "DHS Showed 11%" stays valid (named ÷ claimed). The subhead is fine as raw counts (335 and 3,789 are both correct) — just don't let the body compute "11% of 3,789." For the "11%/89% gap" line, frame it as the disclosure gap against DHS's own claim, not against the FOIA total.

### Claim #2 [HIGH]: The "71% violent" covers a 174-person subset, not all 335

**Article says:** "A widely-cited analysis after the operation found 71% violent among those arrested. That figure is accurate. It covers the 335 names DHS chose to publicize..."

**Source says** (Davidson): the 71% is **124 of 174** individuals — the Twin Cities metro arrests plus the 87 with no location listed. Across the *full* 335 named, the violent/sexual rate is **57% (190 of 335)**. The 71% is a subset of the 335, not the whole 335 — and certainly not "those arrested" (the 3,789).

**Discrepancy:** "71% violent among those arrested... covers the 335 names" overstates the 71%'s reach two ways: it's not 71% of all 335 (that's 57%), and "among those arrested" reads as the full population. The rhetorical move is still valid and strong — the 71% describes only a sliver of the disclosed slice — but the attribution has to be precise.

**Recommendation:**
> "A widely-cited analysis found 71% violent offenders. That figure is accurate — and it covers the 124-of-174 Twin Cities names in DHS's own published list, a sliver of the 3,789 the FOIA data documents."

This keeps the punch ("the scary stat describes a tiny disclosed subset") while making the denominator honest.

### Claim #3 [MED]: "Stated operational target: 3,000 dangerous criminals"

**Article says:** "Stated operational target: 3,000 dangerous criminals. Take that at face value, then check it."

**Source says** (DHS release, Jan 19): Noem claimed ICE "arrested **3,000 criminal illegal aliens** including vicious murderers, rapists, child pedophiles" in the last 6 weeks. The 3,000 is a **claimed accomplishment** (arrests already made), not a pre-stated operational *target* or goal.

**Discrepancy:** "Operational target" mischaracterizes a primary source. DHS didn't announce a goal of 3,000; it claimed it had *arrested* 3,000 worst-of-the-worst. The distinction matters because the draft's next move ("check it against the FOIA data") works better against a *claim* than a *target*.

**Recommendation:**
> "DHS's claim: 3,000 'criminal illegal aliens,' the worst of the worst. Take that at face value, then check it."

(This also feeds Claim #1 cleanly — the 3,000 DHS *claimed* is the right denominator for the 11%.)

### Claim #4 [MED]: "National Guard support" — not in any wiki source

**Article says (§4):** "what happened was an ICE and CBP operation with National Guard support."

**Source says:** The Operation Metro Surge entity page records "2,000 ICE agents + 1,000 CBP officers deployed" at peak. **No wiki source mentions National Guard involvement in Metro Surge.**

**Discrepancy:** This is a checkable operational fact embedded in the personal-reflection section. The "ICE and CBP" half is fully verified; the "National Guard support" half has no wiki corroboration. If it can't be sourced, it's an unverified factual claim sitting in an otherwise-honest reflection — exactly the kind of detail a hostile reader checks.

**Recommendation:** Either source the National Guard involvement (a single cite) or cut to the verified version: "what happened was an ICE and CBP operation." The §4 argument doesn't need the National Guard detail; the ICE/CBP-vs-military-mobilization contrast survives without it.

---

### Claim #5 [LOW]: Touhy citation reads as if the case is codified at §301

**Article says:** "The Touhy regulations (from *Touhy v. Ragen*, codified at 5 U.S.C. §301)..."

**Source says** (Touhy concept page): the doctrine comes from *Touhy v. Ragen*, 340 U.S. 462 (1951), **read together with** the Housekeeping Statute, 5 U.S.C. §301. The *case* isn't codified; §301 is the *statute* the regulations rest on.

**Discrepancy:** As written, "(from *Touhy v. Ragen*, codified at 5 U.S.C. §301)" can be read as "Touhy v. Ragen is codified at §301," which is wrong. Low severity — most readers won't parse it that finely — but it's a precision miss in a legal passage where precision is the credibility.

**Recommendation:**
> "The Touhy regulations (from *Touhy v. Ragen*, built on the Housekeeping Statute at 5 U.S.C. §301)..."

Claims #3 (3,000 "target"→"claim") and #4 (National Guard) are quick corrections. Claim #5 (Touhy phrasing) is polish.
