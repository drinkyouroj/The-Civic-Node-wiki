### Section 1: The Glitch — the bill a city got for an operation it never authorized (~400 words)
*Tone: sardonic precision; name what's broken without rage*

- Open on the invoice logic, then narrow to the line items (see Opener Strategy). The headline number: the City of Minneapolis's Feb 12 preliminary assessment put one month's impact at **$203.1M** — $81M business revenue, $47M lost wages (people afraid to leave home), $15.7M added rent assistance. The city called it "very likely a significant underestimation." [[City of Minneapolis — Operation Metro Surge results in $203 million impact]]
- The bill grew. The AG's April amended complaint, using UC San Diego survey data, put it metro-wide at **over $240M in lost wages and ~$610M in lost business revenue**. [[Minnesota AG — Metro Surge Cost $240M in Wages, $610M in Business Revenue]]

### Section 2: The Source Code — how "the worst of the worst" became a street sweep (~500 words)
*Tone: building analytical weight; the densest section. Charitable reading, then audit.*

- The charitable reading (state it fairly first): DHS said the operation targeted "the worst of the worst" — Secretary Noem's Jan 19 release claimed 3,000 "criminal illegal aliens including vicious murderers, rapists, child pedophiles." That is the stated rationale; take it at face value, then check it. [[ICE Continues to Remove the Worst of the Worst — DHS - 2026-01-19]]
- The audit: ICE's own arrest data, pried loose by [[Deportation Data Project]] FOIA litigation, shows **fewer than 25% had any criminal conviction, ~13% pending charges, ~63% neither** — and ICE's "conviction" category counts misdemeanors down to **traffic violations and unauthorized entry**, so even the <25% overstates serious criminality. **97% were non-custodial street arrests.** [[MPR News — Three-Quarters of Metro Surge Arrestees Had No Criminal Record - 2026-03-31]], [[Minnesota Reformer — More Than 3,700 Arrested in Metro Surge Per New Data - 2026-03-31]]
- The reconciliation (defuses an apparent contradiction Marcus might catch): a widely-cited map found a "71% violent" rate — but only across the **335 names DHS chose to publicize (~11% of arrests)**. The highlight reel ran 71% violent; the full population ran 63% no record at all. Naming that gap *is* the argument. [[I Mapped Every Confirmed ICE Arrest in Minnesota Heres What I Found]]
- Who actually got swept up: ~35% ICE-labeled "collateral"; **273 valid-work-permit holders** (asylees, refugees, DACA) confirmed from habeas filings — a floor, since the dataset has no status field and excludes citizens entirely. [[Minnesota Reformer — Work Permit Holders Detained in Metro Surge - 2026-05-22]]
- The structural cause (earn the concept here, do not assert it): an operation optimized for *removing dangerous people* would not look like this. An operation optimized for *visibility and presence* would. That second output — name it [[Enforcement Theater]] only after the numbers have made the case. The second proof: ICE operating outside judicial constraint — Chief Judge [[Patrick Schiltz]] documented **96 court-order violations in 74 cases**, then re-verified and added 113 more (≈210), threatening criminal contempt: "ICE is not a law unto itself." [[Juan T.R. v. Noem — Schiltz Orders on ICE Court-Order Violations]]

### Section 3: The Upgrade — there's no law that makes them show you the evidence (~400 words)
*Tone: measured, evidence-grounded; honest about what doesn't exist yet*

- The demonstration's final act: when ICE agent Jonathan Ross shot Renée Good, the FBI reversed an initial agreement and seized exclusive evidence control, locking out Minnesota's investigators. The accountability mechanism that should have activated was switched off at the source. [[Frozen Accountability — Renée Good Investigative Report - 2026-04-28]]
- The honest upgrade finding: there is **no clean legal instrument** to compel a federal agency to hand a state its evidence. [[Touhy Doctrine|Touhy regulations]] (the rules letting an agency decide what evidence it releases) plus the Housekeeping Statute are a shield for the agency, not a sword for the state; DHS simply asserted "Touhy doesn't apply," and the AG's only recourse is a federal lawsuit. Gloss inline. [[Touhy Doctrine]]
- The courts' ceiling: civil contempt (coercive daily fines, pardon-proof) vs. criminal contempt (punitive, but **pardonable** — the Arpaio precedent), and enforcement ultimately runs through U.S. Marshals, who answer to the executive. The judiciary can document defiance; it can't unilaterally end it. [[Juan T.R. v. Noem — Schiltz Orders on ICE Court-Order Violations]]
- The one real lever, and its cost: a **state** criminal conviction is beyond the president's pardon power — which is exactly why the administration moved to block investigations *before* charges. Moriarty's first charge against a surge officer (Morgan, assault) tests it; the agent will remove to federal court and raise [[Supremacy Clause Immunity|Supremacy Clause immunity]] (the shield protecting federal officers from state charges for on-duty acts). Precedent (Horiuchi, Drury) says disputed facts can defeat it. [[Supremacy Clause Immunity]]
- The upgrade that already exists, at the civic layer: mandatory evidence-sharing protocols don't exist in statute yet, so Minnesota is building the record by other means — civilian video, the medical examiner, eyewitnesses, no statute of limitations on the death — plus the sanctuary counter-network. Name the trade-off honestly (states can weaponize process against federal operations; that risk is real, and it's still not a reason to let evidence custody be self-administered).
- Sources: [[Frozen Accountability — Renée Good Investigative Report - 2026-04-28]], [[Touhy Doctrine]], [[Supremacy Clause Immunity]], [[Juan T.R. v. Noem — Schiltz Orders on ICE Court-Order Violations]]

- The personal turn (real, from the 2026-06-08 story session): after the 2024 election I left Seattle's Capitol Hill — a dense, protest-heavy core — and moved back to Michigan, a purple state, on purpose.
