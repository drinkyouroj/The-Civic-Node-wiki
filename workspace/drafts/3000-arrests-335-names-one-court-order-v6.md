# 3,000 Arrests, 335 Names, One Court Order

## DHS claims 3,000+ Metro Surge arrests. Its own registry names 11% of them. No public record of the agents. A federal judge changed that on May 1.

The Department of Homeland Security says Operation Metro Surge made [more than 3,000 arrests](https://www.northernnewsnow.com/2026/02/09/i-mapped-every-confirmed-ice-arrest-minnesota-heres-what-i-found/) in the Minneapolis-St. Paul metro area between December 4, 2025 and February 2026. Its own ["Worst of the Worst" registry](https://www.northernnewsnow.com/2026/02/09/i-mapped-every-confirmed-ice-arrest-minnesota-heres-what-i-found/) lists 335 of them. There's no public roster of the agents who carried out any of those arrests.

On May 1, 2026, an order from federal Judge Jeffrey M. Bryan required DHS to hand over one ICE agent's [personnel file, body-cam footage, witness statements, medical records, and cell phone data](https://theintercept.com/2026/04/09/renee-good-killing-minneapolis-jonathan-ross-videos/). It's the first piece of the operation's original record any part of the federal government has put on the public record. This piece is an audit of what is on the record and what is missing from it. The deadline arrived yesterday.

*Operation Metro Surge will be remembered as an immigration story. The more interesting question is what it reveals about accountability infrastructure: what happens when the system designed to investigate federal misconduct stops cooperating with itself. That question doesn't resolve when the policy changes. The machinery either works or it doesn't.*

## The Glitch: A Federal Judge Is Doing the Investigation Nobody Else Will

On January 7, 2026, at 9:37 a.m., on Portland Avenue South in Minneapolis, ICE agent Jonathan Ross [fired three shots in under one second](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killing_of_Ren%C3%A9e_Good) at Renée Good. She was 37, a writer and poet, a U.S. citizen, and she had just dropped her son at school. All three shots hit. She died at the scene. On Ross's own cellphone video, the agent can be heard [muttering "f---ing b---h"](https://www.thedailybeast.com/ice-agent-jonathan-ross-who-shot-dead-umarmed-mom-renee-good-quietly-reassigned-as-fbi-probe-stalls/) as Good's Honda Pilot rolled into a parked car with her dying inside. Federal officers then [blocked a bystander identifying himself as a physician](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/jan/18/justice-department-ice-renee-good-george-floyd-minneapolis) from approaching her, and blocked ambulances when they arrived. The Department of Justice's Civil Rights Division [declined to open a criminal investigation](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/jan/18/justice-department-ice-renee-good-george-floyd-minneapolis). Six federal prosecutors in Minnesota's district [resigned in protest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killing_of_Ren%C3%A9e_Good), alongside the Civil Rights Division's criminal section chief, principal deputy chief, deputy chief, and acting deputy chief.

The May 1 court order does not arrive in any of those proceedings. It arrives in the defense motion of an unrelated case: the federal prosecution of a man named [Roberto Carlos Muñoz-Guatemala](https://theintercept.com/2026/04/09/renee-good-killing-minneapolis-jonathan-ross-videos/), in which Jonathan Ross is the alleged victim of an assault during a separate June 2025 arrest. (Mixing up Muñoz-Guatemala with the Good killing is how misinformation enters this story; they are two different cases, and Ross's role in each is opposite.) The court ordering Ross's records is doing what the Civil Rights Division declined to do.

The agency publishes what it cannot prove, and walks back what it can no longer defend. The Worst of the Worst registry is the published version. The 89% aren't named in any public document. What follows is what is on the record so far, and what May 1 added to it.

## The Source Code: What the Command Chain Actually Authorized

The federal government's legal position here has real weight. Federal law overrides state law on immigration. That's what the Supremacy Clause means, and courts have upheld it for over a century. A ruling from 1890 has held for 135 years that federal officers acting in their official capacity cannot be prosecuted by states for what they do on the job. Any honest accounting of Metro Surge has to say that up front. Those legal protections are real. This piece examines what happened under them.

[Gregory Bovino](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/border-patrol-gregory-bovino-retire-immigration-enforcement-rcna263751), the Border Patrol commander who ran Operation Metro Surge on the ground, reported directly to then-DHS Secretary Kristi Noem and to Noem's senior adviser Corey Lewandowski. That is not a normal chain of command for a Border Patrol commander. That is a political chain of command. A federal judge in Chicago [found Bovino had "repeatedly lied"](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/border-patrol-gregory-bovino-retire-immigration-enforcement-rcna263751) about threats from immigrants and protesters during his prior operations there. In one specific incident, Bovino claimed he threw a gas canister after being hit by a rock; he had to walk the claim back after the video evidence contradicted him. He was [removed from his role as CBP commander-at-large](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/border-patrol-gregory-bovino-retire-immigration-enforcement-rcna263751) in January 2026 after the deaths of Renée Good (January 7) and Alex Pretti (January 24), and [retired at the end of March 2026](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/border-patrol-gregory-bovino-retire-immigration-enforcement-rcna263751) on schedule, with no charges. DHS, asked by NBC News about misconduct, replied with a form statement: "When we discover any alleged or potential misconduct, we immediately refer it for investigation and cooperate fully." Bovino's referral resolved on his retirement schedule.

Up the chain: Acting ICE Director Todd Lyons reported to Border Czar Tom Homan. Homan [publicly linked the duration](https://www.jurist.org/news/2026/02/us-federal-court-denies-minnesota-bid-to-stop-operation-metro-surge/) of the Minnesota surge to state and local "cooperation" and to the repeal of Minneapolis and St. Paul sanctuary ordinances. AG Pam Bondi sent Governor Walz a letter [demanding access to state records and the same sanctuary repeal](https://www.jurist.org/news/2026/02/us-federal-court-denies-minnesota-bid-to-stop-operation-metro-surge/). The bottom line, under all the legal arguments, is this: federal enforcement was being used to force state policy changes. Not enforcement. Coercion of policy through enforcement.

The state response was four layers, not four incidents. First, federal officers blocked the physician and the ambulances at the scene of the Good shooting. Second, [the FBI revoked the Minnesota Bureau of Criminal Apprehension's access to evidence the day after the shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killing_of_Ren%C3%A9e_Good); [on February 13, the FBI separately notified Minnesota state investigators that it would not share materials in the Pretti case either](https://www.propublica.org/article/minnesota-trump-ice-shooting-lawsuit-alex-pretti-renee-good). Third, Hennepin County DA Mary Moriarty filed first-of-its-kind state assault charges against a federal ICE agent, [Gregory Donnell Morgan, on April 16, 2026](https://www.notus.org/immigration/minnesota-prosecutors-charge-ice-agent), for second-degree assault stemming from a February 5 incident in which Morgan pointed a gun at civilian drivers. Fourth, on March 24, 2026, [Minnesota AG Keith Ellison and Hennepin County filed a federal lawsuit](https://www.propublica.org/article/minnesota-trump-ice-shooting-lawsuit-alex-pretti-renee-good), *Minnesota v. DOJ/DHS*, seeking evidence in three federal-officer shootings during the surge. Each layer is documented. Together, they are the federalism story under the immigration story.

The retcon ran on two timers, in two registers. On January 18, 2026, Deputy AG Todd Blanche told Fox News [that DOJ does not investigate killings like the Good shooting](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/jan/18/justice-department-ice-renee-good-george-floyd-minneapolis) because, as Blanche put it: "we don't just go out and investigate every time an officer is forced to defend himself against somebody putting his life in danger. We never do." Six years earlier, after George Floyd was killed in Minneapolis, the first Trump DOJ under AG William Barr [opened a federal civil rights investigation in three days](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/jan/18/justice-department-ice-renee-good-george-floyd-minneapolis); that probe led to four officers' federal civil rights convictions, including Derek Chauvin's. "We never do" is contradicted by his own department's documented Floyd response in the same city. Blanche was not asked to reconcile the two on Fox.

The same Department also [opened a civil rights investigation into the killing of Alex Pretti](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/nation/minnesota-sues-to-obtain-evidence-in-shootings-by-federal-officers-during-ice-surge), Metro Surge's second civilian fatality, killed by federal agents 17 days after Renée Good. Same city. Same operation. Same agency. Two weeks apart. If a civil rights investigation was the right response to one of those killings, refusing to open one for the other is not a principled legal position. It's a choice. The investigation opened for Pretti carries its own structural flag: DOJ sidelined the career prosecutors from its Civil Rights Division who handle excessive-force cases and [assigned an attorney from the employment litigation section](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/doj-civil-rights-division-career-prosecutors-alex-pretti/) with no prior criminal case experience to lead it.

The Noem retcon ran shorter. On January 24, the day Pretti was shot, Noem said at a press briefing that he had "attacked" agents and was "wishing to inflict harm." By January 29, on Fox News, the scene was ["chaotic"](https://www.goodmorningamerica.com/news/story/former-officials-dhs-tactics-undermine-public-trust-after-129717921), no evidence offered for the original claim, and the investigative authority for the case had quietly shifted from "DHS investigating with FBI assistance" to "FBI is now leading." The walk-back was not announced. It was just incompatible with the original statement and was therefore replaced. Noem was subsequently replaced as DHS Secretary by Markwayne Mullin. Her retcon became someone else's problem.

The structural retcon is older. DHS [turned off its automatic text-message archiving system in April 2025](https://americanoversight.org/dhs-admits-it-provided-erroneous-information-on-texts-of-noem-and-dhs-brass-hasnt-confirmed-it-followed-law/) and told the watchdog group American Oversight in July that Noem's text messages could not be searched. In November, [DHS admitted in court](https://americanoversight.org/dhs-admits-it-provided-erroneous-information-on-texts-of-noem-and-dhs-brass-hasnt-confirmed-it-followed-law/) that the prior claim was "erroneous." The National Archives (NARA) ordered DHS on September 3 to investigate potential unauthorized records destruction; as of October 30, DHS had not responded. The same agency's [Office of Civil Rights and Civil Liberties went from 147 staff to fewer than 40](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/mar/08/dhs-oversight-court-record-review); its office for overseeing immigration detention facilities went from 118 staff to five. CRCL investigated [one of 32 in-custody deaths in 2025](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/mar/08/dhs-oversight-court-record-review), the deadliest year in U.S. immigration detention in more than 20 years. The acting head of the detention oversight office [had never seen the standards manual](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/mar/08/dhs-oversight-court-record-review) for the facilities he was supposed to be overseeing.

## The Upgrade: Why Minnesota's Counter-Architecture Won't Stop Here

When one accountability layer fails, other layers activate. What they activate looks different from what the failed layer was doing.

Hennepin County DA Mary Moriarty has [14 open criminal investigations](https://www.propublica.org/article/minnesota-trump-ice-shooting-lawsuit-alex-pretti-renee-good) into federal-agent conduct during the surge. She is the first state DA in the modern era to bring state-level charges against federal ICE agents for on-duty conduct. Minnesota AG Keith Ellison's federal lawsuit names the legal doctrines at issue: Anti-Commandeering (the argument that the federal government cannot use states as tools to carry out federal programs), Defensive Immunity, and Supremacy Clause Immunity. Judge Katherine Menendez denied Minnesota's preliminary injunction in January, calling the consequences ["profound and even heartbreaking" and "difficult to overstate."](https://www.jurist.org/news/2026/02/us-federal-court-denies-minnesota-bid-to-stop-operation-metro-surge/) The denial was on a legal gap, not the substance of Minnesota's case. Menendez noted that the Supreme Court has offered "little guidance" on how anti-commandeering applies when the federal government deploys its own officers directly, rather than ordering states to enforce federal law for them. That gap is the legal terrain Minnesota is now testing. A federal hearing in *Minnesota v. DOJ/DHS* is scheduled for May 4.

The other layer is evidentiary. Seven federal circuit courts have affirmed the First Amendment right to record law enforcement; in December 2025, DHS [publicly stated](https://reason.com/2025/12/22/dhs-says-recording-or-following-law-enforcement-sure-sounds-like-obstruction-of-justice/) that doing so "sure sounds like obstruction of justice." The bystander video that contradicted Trump's claim that Renée Good "ran over" Ross only exists because of the doctrine DHS is now contesting. The same is true of [the videos that allegedly show Alex Pretti disarmed and restrained on the ground](https://statecourtreport.org/our-work/analysis-opinion/when-can-states-prosecute-federal-agents) when federal officers shot him. The doctrine produces the record. The record contradicts the official account. DHS would like to litigate the doctrine. Federal "assault on ICE" prosecutions have been [collapsing in real time](https://reason.com/2025/12/22/dhs-says-recording-or-following-law-enforcement-sure-sounds-like-obstruction-of-justice/): grand juries declining to indict, US Attorney's Offices dropping cases after reviewing the evidence. The judicial system is overruling the executive position one case at a time, while the Cato Institute's David Bier documents the [pattern of intimidation](https://reason.com/2025/12/22/dhs-says-recording-or-following-law-enforcement-sure-sounds-like-obstruction-of-justice/) in writing.

The operation has already failed by metrics that don't depend on the constitutional questions. The City of Minneapolis estimates [$203.1 million in one-month total impact](https://www.minneapolismn.gov/news/2026/february/oms-impact/), including $81M in business revenue losses and $47M in lost wages. An [independent economic analysis](https://minnesotareformer.com/2026/03/02/measuring-the-economic-damage-of-minnesotas-ice-surge-is-hard/) estimated $106.1M in lost wages in the metro from Jan 3 through Feb 17 alone. The MN AG's April 2026 amended complaint put the broader picture at [over $240 million in lost wages and over $600 million in lost business revenue](https://www.ag.state.mn.us/Office/Communications/2026/04/21_Metro-Surge.asp) across the metro area. Polling on ICE flipped from [13 points favorable at inauguration to 9 points unfavorable by late January](https://www.dataforprogress.org/blog/2026/1/22/a-majority-of-voters-are-unfavorable-of-ice-are-divided-on-what-abolish-ice-means); 55% of voters say increased ICE funding is a bad use of taxpayer money; Independents are unfavorable to ICE by a 20-point margin. Data for Progress is a progressive pollster, but the methodology is in the deck and the numbers move in one direction across multiple polls.

## My Debug: What Happens When the Retcon Meets the Record

Spin and retcon are different tools. The past nine months have been a demonstration of both. Spin works on the future: it tries to shape how the next thing will be received. Retcon tries to alter the past. It only fails when a primary document contradicts it; the failure is total, immediate, and on the record. The Noem walk-back on Pretti is a retcon. The Blanche "we never do this" claim is a retcon, falsified by his own department's documented response in the same city six years earlier, and falsified again by the same Department opening a civil rights investigation into the second Metro Surge killing two weeks after declining the first. Both attempts can be cited verbatim. Neither can be defended.

The most recent addition to this list: [DOJ told Fox News Digital](https://www.foxnews.com/us/justice-department-declines-civil-rights-investigation-minneapolis-ice-officer-killing) that ICE's Office of Professional Responsibility was "running its own internal review" of Ross, "as with any officer-involved shooting." Senior DHS officials told PunchUp the same week that is not what is happening. [ICE OPR cannot begin its administrative review until the FBI probe closes](https://www.thedailybeast.com/ice-agent-jonathan-ross-who-shot-dead-umarmed-mom-renee-good-quietly-reassigned-as-fbi-probe-stalls/), and the FBI probe has not closed. Ross was placed on [administrative leave for three days](https://www.thedailybeast.com/ice-agent-jonathan-ross-who-shot-dead-umarmed-mom-renee-good-quietly-reassigned-as-fbi-probe-stalls/), then quietly moved to another state and returned to active enforcement duty. The DHS Use of Force Directive requires that leave extended pending all investigative proceedings. The proceedings haven't concluded. The leave ended.

Acting ICE Director Todd Lyons, asked at a House subcommittee hearing whether he would apologize to Good's family for how she was publicly characterized, replied: ["I welcome the opportunity to speak to the family in private, but I'm not going to comment on any active investigation."](https://www.thedailybeast.com/ice-agent-jonathan-ross-who-shot-dead-umarmed-mom-renee-good-quietly-reassigned-as-fbi-probe-stalls/) His resignation was announced hours later, effective May 31. The rhetorical move of invoking an investigation's integrity to avoid accountability isn't, in this case, available to the people who dismantled the investigation's integrity.

The institutional move that gives the rest of this away is the [April 2025 disabling of the DHS auto-archive](https://americanoversight.org/dhs-admits-it-provided-erroneous-information-on-texts-of-noem-and-dhs-brass-hasnt-confirmed-it-followed-law/). That was the same month the watchdog teams were ousted. It was the same month [Treasury Secretary Bessent and Secretary Noem signed the IRS-DHS data-sharing agreement](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/data-of-thousands-of-taxpayers-wrongly-shared-with-dhs-court-filing-says) that has now been found to have wrongly disclosed thousands of taxpayer records. Three federal records actions, in one month, all in the same direction. Reading these as separate stories was always going to age poorly.

The constitutional question underneath all of this is broader than immigration. If a state cannot hold a federal agent accountable for what he did on duty, and the federal government refuses to investigate, nobody is in charge. That gap exists in American law right now. Today the policy filling that gap is immigration. The structural gap predates this operation and will outlast it. By the time you read this, May 1 has passed. DHS either produced Jonathan Ross's records to a federal magistrate for in camera review (the first primary document the operation has yielded, even if it enters under seal) or it refused a federal court order, which is a different and larger confrontation. The documentation already exists either way. Mary Moriarty filed [the first state assault charges against a federal ICE agent](https://www.propublica.org/article/minnesota-trump-ice-shooting-lawsuit-alex-pretti-renee-good) in the modern history of the United States. Who files the second?
