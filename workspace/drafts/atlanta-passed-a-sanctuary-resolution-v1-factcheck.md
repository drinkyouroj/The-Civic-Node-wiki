### Flagged Claims

#### Flag #1 — DATE DISCREPANCY (critical)
**Article says:** "Atlanta City Council passed two resolutions on April 21, 2026"
**Source says:** Atlanta News First (the linked source) reports "Monday, April 20, 2026"
**Discrepancy:** April 20 is a Monday in 2026; April 21 is a Tuesday. The Atlanta Civic Circle coverage (from original research) also reported the vote as April 20. The draft date is wrong by one day.
**Recommendation:** Change "April 21, 2026" to "April 20, 2026" in both the article text and the subheadline ("The city passed two new anti-ICE resolutions last week"). This is a factual error — Marcus will check it.

---

#### Flag #2 — TRUNCATED QUOTE (medium)
**Article says:** CEO Garrett Langley "acknowledged...that 'some of our public statements inadvertently provided inaccurate information. We clearly communicated poorly.'"
**Source says (full quote from NPR wiki source page):** "[T]here have been conflicting reports in the media about Flock's relationship with federal agencies, and some of our public statements inadvertently provided inaccurate information. We clearly communicated poorly."
**Discrepancy:** The draft begins the quotation mid-sentence without an ellipsis, presenting a truncated version as a complete quote. The quoted portion is accurate, but starting with "some of our public statements" without "[...]" or a complete sentence is a sourcing presentation problem — if a reader checks the source, they see the quote began differently.
**Recommendation:** Either use the full quote, or add an opening ellipsis: "...'some of our public statements inadvertently provided inaccurate information. We clearly communicated poorly.'" Alternatively, frame as: "Langley wrote that 'some of our public statements inadvertently provided inaccurate information. We clearly communicated poorly'" — and verify whether the statement was written or spoken. Also: the Atlanta News First article is linked as the source, but the NPR wiki source page has the fuller quote. Recommend linking to the NPR article instead for this claim.

#### Flag #3 — NOT FOUND IN SOURCE
**Article says:** "The contractual prohibition on immigration use of Flock data [existed before 2025]" — linked to Flock Search Safeguards blog

**Discrepancy:** The "existed before 2025" framing is a meaningful structural claim (it's the setup for "contractual is not technical"). The linked source doesn't support it. The claim likely comes from the California class action context (the lawsuit implies prohibitions existed that were violated), but that source isn't linked here.
**Recommendation:** Either (a) link this sentence to the SFist class action article, which implies pre-existing contractual prohibitions by framing violations as unlawful, or (b) rephrase to "Flock's terms of service prohibited agencies from using the platform for immigration enforcement" and link to Flock's "Does Flock Share Data With ICE?" blog, which states agencies are contractually prohibited. The "existed before 2025" framing needs a source that can specifically confirm the prohibition's history.
