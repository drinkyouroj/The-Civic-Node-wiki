### Flagged Claims

#### Flag #1 — NOT SUPPORTED BY SOURCE (critical)

**Article says:** "A [class action filed in February 2026](https://sfist.com/...) alleges Flock's contractual prohibition was violated 1.6 million times in seven months via SFPD cameras."

**Source says (SFist, ingested):** "Alleged Violation: **California state law** prohibiting sharing of California driver information with out-of-state law enforcement and the federal government — NOT a contractual prohibition." The law firm (Gibbs Mura) is cited as saying "the problem...is that Flock is sharing this information on California drivers with out-of-state law enforcement." No contractual violation is alleged.

**Discrepancy:** The article attributes the legal theory to Flock's contract terms. The lawsuit's actual legal theory is California state law. These are meaningfully different: Flock's contractual prohibition governs all agencies nationally; California state law only governs California. The article's framing implies Flock's own terms were violated, which is not what the lawsuit alleges. This is a factual error about the legal basis for the lawsuit.

**Recommendation:** Replace "alleges Flock's contractual prohibition was violated" with "alleges California law prohibiting out-of-state data sharing was violated." Suggested rewrite:

> "A [class action filed in February 2026](https://sfist.com/...) alleges California's law prohibiting the sharing of driver data with federal and out-of-state agencies was violated 1.6 million times in seven months via SFPD cameras."

This also slightly strengthens the article's argument: the violation is of state law, not just a vendor contract — which means the camera network ran afoul of California's legislature, not just Flock's terms of service.

#### Flag #2 — MEDIUM UNCONFIRMED (low)

**Article says:** "CEO Garrett Langley [acknowledged](NPR URL)...writing that '[t]here have been conflicting reports...'"

**Source says (NPR wiki):** The NPR wiki attributes the statement as "CEO Garrett Langley, statement" — the word "statement" does not specify written or verbal medium.

**Discrepancy:** "Writing that" implies a written document (blog post, letter, or press release). The NPR source does not confirm this. The Flock blog is a written document, but the August 2025 statement acknowledged in NPR reporting may have been an email to customers, a blog post, or a press statement — the medium is unconfirmed by the NPR source alone.

**Recommendation:** Change "writing that" to "saying" or simply remove the attribution verb: "...and that '[t]here have been conflicting reports...'" The substance of the quote is verified; only the medium characterization is uncertain. Removing "writing that" eliminates the unconfirmed claim without losing anything meaningful.
