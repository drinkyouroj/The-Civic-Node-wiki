## Top Comprehension-Breaking Violations

1. **Cross-audit (redundancy + cut)** — "He summoned the entire FOMC to the White House" (Analysis ¶2, the 1951 Accord paragraph)
   - Why: The v2 opener now narrates this exact scene ("A sitting president summoned the entire rate-setting committee to the White House and demanded it keep his borrowing costs low. The committee said no"). The Analysis paragraph re-narrates the same summons a third of the way down the page. The reader notices the repeat, and it costs ~15 words the piece is over budget on.
   - Revision (Analysis ¶2): keep the Truman *reveal* and the "only time" fact, drop the re-narration —
     > The [March 1951 Treasury-Fed Accord](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/treasury-fed-accord) ended the Federal Reserve's wartime obligation to peg Treasury bond yields and restored its authority to set monetary policy independently of Treasury debt-management needs. The president in that opening scene was Harry Truman, and his summons was the only time one has ever called the full FOMC to the White House. After the Accord, the Fed could raise rates without Treasury permission and the Treasury had to issue debt at market-clearing yields. Before it, neither was true. Modern Fed independence is 75 years old, not constitutional, and it began as a refusal.

2. **Paragraph length (wall)** — "It was cut to roughly 130 words" (June FOMC tell, ¶3) — ~150 words, five stacked items
   - Why: The "everywhere else" accumulation (word count → guidance → balance-sheet line → withheld dot → task forces) is a deliberate pile-up, but at ~150 words in the payoff section it reads as a wall right where the reader should be landing the point.
   - Revision: split at the conceptual seam (what the statement *says* vs. where the decision *went*). Break into two paragraphs, the second beginning:
     > And the actual redesign of what the Fed holds and how it lines up with the Treasury's debt calendar was moved into newly announced task forces, which issue no statement, take no FOMC vote, and leave nothing to FOIA.

3. **Paragraph length (wall)** — "When the Treasury Secretary says he 'understands'" (Implications, ¶1) — ~145 words ending on the Faust quote
   - Why: Two distinct ideas share one block — the Bessent-cover mechanism, then the market-is-watching-the-wrong-instrument turn. The Faust quote lands deep in a long paragraph and loses force.
   - Revision: split before the market turn; second paragraph begins:
     > Wall Street is currently [pricing minimal probability](https://www.adn.com/nation-world/2026/04/25/dont-count-on-rate-cuts-just-yet-warsh-as-fed-chair-may-not-lead-to-big-policy-changes/) of rate cuts before October 2027. Jon Faust, a former Powell adviser, told the AP…

4. **Paragraph length (borderline — pre-existing)** — "The first piece is the rough draft. Stephen Miran held…" (Problem, ¶2) — ~150 words, highest density in the piece
