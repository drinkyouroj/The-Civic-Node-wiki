# June 17, 2026 FOMC — verified facts (Warsh's first meeting as chair)

Captured 2026-06-17 for the "Accord That Wasn't Signed" finishing pass. These facts feed the
"June FOMC tell" rewrite and will face the tcn-fact-check pass. Sources below.

## The decision
- **Rate held** at **3.5%–3.75%** target range, "in support of the Federal Reserve's dual mandate."
- **Vote: unanimous (12–0)**, no dissenters recorded (per official statement). Contrast: March 2026 was 11–1 (Miran the lone dissent, for cuts).

## The dot plot (Summary of Economic Projections)
- Median end-2026 fed funds rate revised UP to **3.8%, from 3.4% in March** — signals at least one **hike** this year.
- This is a reversal: in March the median projected a quarter-point **cut** in 2026.
- Distribution among the 18 who submitted: **9 anticipate at least one hike, 8 expect no change, 1 sees a cut.**
- **Warsh did NOT submit a dot** — only 18 of 19 officials submitted projections. The chair withheld his own rate view from the public grid.
- Inflation cited at a **three-year high**.

## The statement itself
- **Radically shortened to ~130 words**, down from 300+ in recent meetings.
- **Forward guidance removed** — prior easing-slant language deleted; Warsh's stated reason: forward guidance "not well suited for the current policy conjuncture."
- **Balance-sheet language collapsed to one line**: the Committee "reaffirmed its policy of maintaining ample reserves in the banking system." No detail on securities holdings, reinvestment, or composition.

## Balance-sheet context
- **QT (quantitative tightening) already ended Dec 1, 2025** — before this meeting; only ~half of pandemic balance-sheet growth was reversed.
- Going forward: Fed buys/sells Treasuries to match trend reserve demand; rolls maturing Treasuries + MBS into new Treasuries (MBS holdings gradually decline). The live lever is now **composition + reserve management**, not runoff.

## Warsh's structural move
- Announced **task forces to overhaul major Federal Reserve operations** — relocating the operational redesign off the FOMC statement and outside the FOMC vote.

## Thesis implication (for the rewrite)
The draft predicted: "read the balance-sheet paragraph; that tells you whether the accord is being
negotiated." Warsh's first act emptied that paragraph — one ample-reserves line, no forward guidance,
no chair dot, a unanimous vote. The contested signal migrated to the dot plot (a hawkish hike surprise =
the loudest alibi yet), and the operational redesign migrated to "task forces" with no transcript or vote.
This INVERTS 1951: that accord was struck in the open and put on the record; this one is assembled by
making the public record say less. Maps directly onto the June 12 teaser ("struck in the open / nothing to FOIA").
