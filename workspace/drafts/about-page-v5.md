# Who controls power — and why the answer keeps changing.

That's the question behind everything published here. The Civic Node covers the mechanics of institutional power: how it accumulates and what it looks like when it cracks. Monetary policy, surveillance infrastructure, AI, civil litigation, supply chains: one story about who holds the chokepoints and who doesn't.

One to two articles per week, typically 1,500 words, built from primary sources. Every claim links to its source. If I'm wrong, you can check.

## Who this is for

You follow the news but don't trust the framing. You've noticed the interesting question is rarely the one the headline asks. You want analysis that connects across domains: why a city council resolution can be made meaningless by a vendor contract, why an efficiency breakthrough sometimes increases demand instead of reducing it, why a central banker can swear to independence while redefining the word, why institutions that fail publicly almost never face consequences.

You read footnotes. You follow source links. You forward articles to one specific person, not to your whole feed.

---

## What you'll find here

**Power audits.** Who controls what: surveillance networks, monetary policy, supply chains, legal process. And what happens when that control is used against the people it's supposed to serve. The cameras don't attend council meetings. That's the story.

**Institutional mechanics.** How the gap between official language and observable effect works. The four-part machine that processes accountability claims until they expire: evidence custody, procedural substitution, exhaustion, toothless laws. It's working.

**The decentralization stress test.** Where decentralized infrastructure solves problems centralized systems structurally can't, and where it's marketing with a whitepaper. I run DePIN hardware. The analysis has skin in it.

**Policy in plain language.** Federal Reserve decisions, trade policy, sanctions design: explained through what they actually do to markets and people, not what the press release says. The Warsh confirmation and the Powell investigation weren't separate stories.

**Cross-domain pattern recognition.** The same dynamic (institutional capture, leverage concentration, chokepoint control) appears across energy, finance, AI, and geopolitics, run by people who don't know they're running the same play. This newsletter names the play.
