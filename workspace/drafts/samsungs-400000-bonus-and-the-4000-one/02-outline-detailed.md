### OPENER APPROACH MAP

**Analogy to use:** Not a metaphor — a concrete pair. Two workers leaving the *same* Pyeongtaek plant gate at the end of the *same* shift. Broad thing: "who gets the money the AI boom is throwing off?" Specific thing: these two people. Bridging property: they do different jobs for the same employer and walk out with payouts ~100× apart. The pair *is* the narrowing device, and it literally seeds the article's asymmetry (capture vs. absorption) in two human bodies.

**Paragraph structure:**
- ¶1 — the two workers at the gate; the $400K vs $4K gap inside one company; Bloomberg's term "AI aristocrats." Establishes that the windfall has a face and an internal fault line.
- ¶2 — the deal that produced it + thesis: a fight *among the winners* over a windfall, captured at the one chokepoint where workers had a hand on the valve. The sentence that must exist by end of ¶2: a plain statement that this is a distribution fight, not a displacement fight, and that *where* the windfall got captured is the whole story.

**Marcus 30-second test:** Passes. Concrete names/numbers in sentence one, a thesis he doesn't already have in this framing (capture-requires-a-valve), and it's visibly going somewhere. The hook that earns the next 30 seconds: the $400K/$4K gap is inside ONE company — that's the "wait, what?" that pulls him down.

**What to avoid (specific to this opener):** Don't open with "you" (voice rule — reads as self-help). Don't editorialize the gap as outrage; let the two numbers do it (sardonic precision, not rage). Don't gloss HBM/operating-profit in ¶1 — keep it in plain dollars and "the memory chip division"; the glosses come in §1 ¶2–3.

### The Spark: The $400,000 bonus and the $4,000 one
Word target: ~400 words | Tone: concrete, grounded, sardonic precision
Argument job: put a human face and an internal fault line on the AI windfall, and land the thesis that this is a distribution fight whose outcome depended on *where* the leverage sat.

**¶1 — Open on the two workers (the narrowing image)**

Facts to deploy (in order):
- Two workers leave the same Samsung plant in Pyeongtaek at the end of the same shift; one is in the memory-chip division, one makes phones/appliances (the DX division).
  Source: [[Samsung AI Bonuses Prompt Korea Debate — Bloomberg - 2026-05-30]]
- The memory worker's 2026 bonus runs toward ~$400,000 (up to 600M won); the DX worker's is ~$4,000 (6M won). ~100× gap, same employer.
  Source: [[Samsung Wage Deal Ratified — Korea Herald - 2026-05-27]], [[Samsung Chip Workers Accept $340K Average Bonus — Tom's Hardware - 2026-05-27]]
- Bloomberg's name for the top tier: "AI aristocrats" — attribute it.
  Source: [[Samsung AI Bonuses Prompt Korea Debate — Bloomberg - 2026-05-30]]

Argument move: introduces the windfall as a concrete scene, not a percentage; plants the internal fault line that previews the whole-economy fault line.
Grounding plan: concrete throughout — two named-role workers, one place (Pyeongtaek), two dollar figures.
Statistic framing: $400K vs $4K isn't decoration — it's the K-shaped split made visible at human scale; the ~100× gap is *inside one company*, which is what makes it land (the boom didn't just split capital from labor, it split labor from labor).
Key sentence to land: the image that converts "windfall" from an abstraction into two people at a gate.

**¶2 — The deal that did it, and the thesis**

Facts to deploy:
- Ratified May 27, 2026; 73.7% of 62,616 ballots cast (95.5% turnout).
  Source: [[Samsung Wage Deal Ratified — Korea Herald - 2026-05-27]]
- Terms: 10.5% of the chip division's operating profit (gloss inline: the division's profit from its core business), paid in company stock, + 1.5% cash + a 6.2% raise — for ten years.
  Source: [[Samsung Chip Workers Accept $340K Average Bonus — Tom's Hardware - 2026-05-27]]

Argument move: pivots from image to mechanism, then lands the thesis.
Grounding plan: concrete throughout — vote tally, the four deal terms, "ten years."
Statistic framing: 10.5%-of-profit-for-ten-years is not a bonus, it's a *standing claim* — frame the number as durability, not size. The point isn't $400K this year; it's that a slice of every profitable year for a decade now belongs to labor by contract.
Key sentence to land: the thesis — this is a fight among the winners over a windfall, and it got captured at the one chokepoint where the workers had a hand on the valve.

**¶3 — Why these workers had the leverage**

Facts to deploy:
- Samsung is 22.8% of South Korea's exports and 26% of its stock market; a strike there is national-scale leverage.
  Source: [[Samsung AI Labor Showdown Haves Versus Have-Mores — Bloomberg - 2026-05-21]]
- HBM (gloss: the stacked high-speed memory AI chips need) is sold out; a walkout would hit the AI buildout directly. Q-profit jumped 755% the prior quarter — the prize was huge and the timing was theirs.
  Source: [[Samsung AI Labor Showdown Haves Versus Have-Mores — Bloomberg - 2026-05-21]]

Argument move: proves the leverage by scale — the "valve" is real because the whole economy runs through it.
Grounding plan: concrete throughout — two percentages, "sold out," 755%.
Statistic framing: 22.8% of exports / 26% of the market = why a strike at *one firm* is a lever on the whole country; frame the numbers as the source of the workers' bargaining power, not as trivia.
Key sentence to land: the workers could win this because the country's growth runs through their fab — leverage, not generosity, wrote the check.

**¶4 — The question the reader isn't asking (pivot)**

Facts to deploy:
- None new — this is the turn.

Argument move: pivots to §2 by asking where the cost went.
Grounding plan: one-word/short — keep this paragraph tight (2–3 sentences).
Key sentence to land: if the workers captured the upside, the cost of the boom didn't evaporate — so who's absorbing it?

### The Pattern: The same boom, pointing the other way
Word target: ~500 words | Tone: analytical, building — the reveal section
Argument job: drop one layer down to the grid chokepoint, show the same boom landing as a *cost* there, and name the asymmetry without collapsing it into "same money."

**¶1 — Drop to the transformer layer**

Facts to deploy:
- The thing that physically energizes a data center is a large transformer (gloss: the equipment that steps electricity up or down to move it onto the grid). High-capacity lead times have stretched to four years (PwC).
  Source: [[US Transformer Lead Times Extend to Four Years — pv magazine USA - 2026-05-11]]
- The gate inside the gate: transformers run on grain-oriented electrical steel (gloss: the specialized steel a transformer's core requires), and one domestic mill makes it — Cleveland-Cliffs, in Butler, Pennsylvania.
  Source: [[Cleveland-Cliffs Weirton Transformer Plant — Utility Dive - 2024-08-06]], [[US Transformer Lead Times Extend to Four Years — pv magazine USA - 2026-05-11]]

Argument move: introduces the second chokepoint and establishes it's as hard a bottleneck as the fab.
Grounding plan: named entity (Cleveland-Cliffs, Butler PA) + "four years."
Statistic framing: the four-year lead time is the buildout's physical schedule-setter — frame it as "the transformer, not the chip, now sets when a data center turns on."
Key sentence to land: the grid is a chokepoint exactly like the memory fab — except there's no hand on this valve.

**¶2 — The hinge quote**

Facts to deploy:
- Cleveland-Cliffs CEO Lourenco Goncalves: "there will be no AI without electricity, and there will be no electricity without transformers."
  Source: [[Cleveland-Cliffs Weirton Transformer Plant — Utility Dive - 2024-08-06]]

Argument move: uses the operator's own line to make the grid-as-chokepoint claim land with credibility.
Grounding plan: concrete throughout — named CEO + direct quote.
Key sentence to land: the quote does the work; the surrounding sentence just frames it as the physical floor under the whole boom.

**¶3 — The reveal: the same boom is on your power bill**

Facts to deploy:
- PJM (gloss: the grid operator for 13 eastern states) Q1 2026 capacity auctions (gloss: where the grid pays to guarantee future supply) put $13.77 billion of data-center-driven cost onto customer bills across two auctions.
  Source: [[Quarterly State of the Market Report for PJM Q1 2026 — Monitoring Analytics - 2026-05-14]]
- Wholesale power ran $77.78 → $136.53/MWh, +75.5% year over year.
  Source: [[Quarterly State of the Market Report for PJM Q1 2026 — Monitoring Analytics - 2026-05-14]]

Argument move: the reveal — the same AI demand that paid the Samsung workers is, one layer down, raising household power bills.
Grounding plan: concrete throughout — $13.77B, +75.5%, PJM.
Statistic framing: $13.77B isn't a market stat — it's cost moved onto households who never voted on the data centers driving it; frame the number as the *absorption* side of the same boom that funded the *capture* side.
Key sentence to land: the connection — the boom that wrote the workers a check is writing ratepayers an invoice.

💡 [RHETORICAL SUGGESTION — USE OR SKIP]
Type: Structural move (parallel framing)
Location: transition into ¶4
Suggestion: set the two chokepoints side by side in parallel construction — same boom, two valves, two outcomes — to make the asymmetry visible before naming it.
Example shape: "Same boom. At one valve, the people holding it got paid. At the other, the people standing near it got billed."
Why it might work: the parallelism does rhythmic work (anaphora, per voice rules — encouraged when it carries the argument) and sets up the "not same money" discipline.
Why it might not: risks sounding like the banned negative-parallelism tic if phrased as "not X, but Y" — keep it as two positive parallel statements, not a reframe.

**¶4 — Name the asymmetry; resist the merge (the discipline paragraph)**

Facts to deploy:
- Same cause (the AI capex boom), opposite directions: at the memory chokepoint a group that could organize *captured* a share; at the grid chokepoint a group that can't *absorbs* the cost.
  Source: [[AI Cost Incidence]], [[AI Windfall Sharing]]

Argument move: the analytical core — names what's shared (the cause) AND what differs (the direction), explicitly refusing the "same money" collapse.
Grounding plan: the two chokepoints named concretely (Samsung fab / the power grid); "captured" vs "absorbs" as the one-word punches.
Key sentence to land: the same pressure found the path of least resistance in two different places — and the difference between them is whether anyone there could push back.
⚠️ Craft caution (from seed): do NOT write "the same money" or "the same fight." This paragraph is where the false symmetry would sneak in. Two positive parallel claims, never a reframe.

**¶5 — The tell that they're connected, not identical (the Unexpected-Connection payoff)**

Facts to deploy:
- In both places the boom hit a chokepoint and tested who had leverage. The chip workers had a union and a valve. The ratepayer has neither.
  Source: [[AI Windfall Sharing]], [[AI Cost Incidence]]

Argument move: lands the connection Marcus has been feeling — the spine joining two stories he reads separately (chip-labor headlines, his power bill).
Grounding plan: "union," "valve," "ratepayer" — concrete roles.
Key sentence to land: the payoff — these aren't two stories, they're one boom meeting two chokepoints with two very different answers to "who can say no?"
⚠️ Craft caution: the chip-cost pass-through ("hyperscalers pay for the bonuses") does NOT go here — the grid cost is documented (PJM), the chip-cost pass-through is not a flow. Hold it for §3 ¶3 and name it as inference there.

### The Protocol: Labor wrote a claim, the grid got a bill
Word target: ~400 words | Tone: forward-looking, grounded — implications, not predictions
Argument job: name what kind of instrument the deal is (bounded Accord parallel), tell the truth about where its cost lands, bound the one inference, and land on recourse.

**¶1 — What kind of instrument this is (the bounded Accord parallel)**

Facts to deploy:
- The Samsung deal isn't a raise; it's a written, profit-indexed claim on a decade of surplus — thin on the page, structural in effect.
  Source: [[Samsung Wage Deal Ratified — Korea Herald - 2026-05-27]]
- The 1951 Treasury-Fed Accord (gloss: the 1951 agreement that freed the Federal Reserve from propping up wartime Treasury bond prices) was also a famously thin document — roughly one paragraph — that permanently moved who controlled a surplus, and it happened because the weaker party refused to keep accommodating the stronger one.
  Source: [[1951 Treasury-Fed Accord]]

Argument move: reframes the deal as an instrument with a historical precedent on a specific axis.
Grounding plan: "1951," "one paragraph," "refused" — concrete anchors.
Key sentence to land: a thin written instrument, won by refusal, can permanently move a surplus — that's what the workers just did.
⚠️ Craft caution (from seed): the parallel transfers ONLY on the thin-instrument-won-by-refusal axis. Do NOT extend it to central-bank "independence" — that's the June 19 piece's axis. One or two sentences on the Accord, then back to Samsung. (Section 3 is the most load-bearing; if it crowds, this is the most compressible beat.)

**¶2 — Where the cost actually lands (the honest part)**

Facts to deploy:
- Because the bonus is 10.5% *of* profit, not a charge stamped on every chip, it's "a distribution of earnings, not an upfront cost burden."
  Source: [[Samsung Strike Risk Gone Now the Real Test Is HBM — Investing.com - 2026-05-27]]
- Its incidence falls on shareholders and reinvestment: SK Hynix's combined shareholder + employee rewards rose ~+450% to ~100 trillion won while spending on factories and R&D grew only +20–30%.
  Source: [[SK Hynix 100 Trillion Won Reward Burden — Seoul Economic Daily - 2026-05-05]]

Argument move: complicates the naive "passed to customers" read before the reader can assume it.
Grounding plan: +450% vs +20–30%, "shareholders," "R&D."
Statistic framing: +450% rewards vs +20–30% reinvestment = the windfall crowding out the spending that keeps the chokepoint competitive; the number is evidence that the cost lands on owners and future capacity, not on the chip buyer.
Key sentence to land: a profit-share comes out of the profit — it lands on shareholders and on the money that would have built the next fab, not on your chip invoice.

**¶3 — The one inference, named as inference**

Facts to deploy:
- Samsung *could* use its chokepoint pricing power (≈70% of memory with SK Hynix, HBM sold out) to rebuild the margin it just shared — and if it does, the cost reaches the hyperscalers (gloss: the big cloud buyers — Microsoft, Amazon, Google, Oracle). "Could," not "does."
  Source: [[Samsung Strike Risk Gone Now the Real Test Is HBM — Investing.com - 2026-05-27]]
- JPMorgan's Jay Kwon put the labor cost at a 7–12% downside to Samsung's 2026 operating profit.
  Source: [[Samsung HBM Strike Could Wrench AI Boom — Fortune - 2026-05-17]]

Argument move: bounds the one place the argument reaches past the documents, and flags it as the writer's inference.
Grounding plan: named analyst (Jay Kwon), 7–12%, named hyperscalers.
Statistic framing: 7–12% OP downside = the margin Samsung might try to win back through pricing; frame it as the size of the temptation, not a settled outcome.
Key sentence to land: the honest-verb sentence — Samsung *could* charge more to make the margin back; whether it does is a choice, not an accounting law. Keep the verb "could."
⚠️ Craft caution: this is the GAP 4 sentence. Never "does," never "passes through," never "hyperscalers are paying for the bonuses." The whole credibility of the piece rests on this verb.

**¶4 — Where it goes, and the recourse landing**

Facts to deploy:
- Korea is already debating a "citizen dividend" and a sovereign wealth fund, forced by the fact that Samsung + SK Hynix could owe more corporate tax than the government expected from every company combined.
  Source: [[Samsung AI Bonuses Prompt Korea Debate — Bloomberg - 2026-05-30]]
- In the US, Axios named the boardroom version — the "AI productivity dividend" as a 2026 proxy-season demand; UAW and CWA are studying the Samsung playbook.
  Source: [[The Coming AI Profit Revolt — Axios - 2026-05-25]]

Argument move: escalates the claim up the ladder (contract → national policy → US boardroom), then lands on the domain's core question (recourse).
Grounding plan: named programs ("citizen dividend," "AI productivity dividend"), named unions (UAW, CWA).
Statistic framing: "more corporate tax than the government expected from every company combined" = the scale that forces a policy response; frame it as why this can't stay a private labor story.
Key sentence to land: the recourse landing — at the chokepoint you can organize, labor wrote itself a claim; at the chokepoint you can't, the bill just arrives. The open question is whether anyone ever writes a claim for the second.

💡 [RHETORICAL SUGGESTION — USE OR SKIP]
Type: Structural callback (sets up the close)
Location: end of ¶4
Suggestion: phrase the recourse landing as the two chokepoints answering the same question ("who can say no?") differently — this hands the close its callback to the two workers.
Example shape: "One chokepoint had a union. The other has a billing address."
Why it might work: compresses the whole asymmetry into one dry line and tees up the personal close.
Why it might not: if §4 then restates it, it's redundant — let the close *extend* it, not repeat it.
