## Section 1: The Spark — "The $400,000 Bonus and the $4,000 One" (~400 words)
*Tone: concrete, grounded — names, numbers, one gate*

- Open on two workers leaving the same Pyeongtaek plant at the end of the same shift: a memory-division engineer whose 2026 bonus runs toward **$400,000**, and a phone/appliance (DX) colleague whose payout is **$4,000** — a ~100× gap *inside one company*. Bloomberg's word for the top tier: "AI aristocrats." (Sources: [[Samsung AI Bonuses Prompt Korea Debate — Bloomberg - 2026-05-30]], [[Samsung Wage Deal Ratified — Korea Herald - 2026-05-27]])
- The deal that did it: ratified **May 27, 73.7% of 62,616 ballots** — Samsung chip workers locked in **10.5% of the division's operating profit** (paid in stock) + 1.5% cash + a 6.2% raise, for **ten years**. ([[Samsung Chip Workers Accept $340K Average Bonus — Tom's Hardware - 2026-05-27]])
- Why these workers could: the memory chokepoint *is* the leverage. Samsung is **22.8% of Korean exports and 26% of the stock market**; HBM is sold out; a strike would have hit the AI buildout directly. ([[Samsung AI Labor Showdown Haves Versus Have-Mores — Bloomberg - 2026-05-21]])
- Thesis to plant by paragraph 2: this isn't workers-vs-robots. It's a fight *among the winners* over a windfall — captured at the one chokepoint where the workers had a hand on the valve.
- End on the question Marcus isn't asking: *if the workers captured the upside, the cost of the boom didn't vanish — so where did it go?*

## Section 2: The Pattern — "The Same Boom, Pointing the Other Way" (~500 words)
*Tone: analytical, building — the reveal section*

- Drop one layer down the same buildout, to the thing that physically powers it: the transformer. Lead time for a high-capacity unit has stretched to **four years** (PwC), gated by a single material — grain-oriented electrical steel — made by exactly **one domestic mill, Cleveland-Cliffs in Butler, PA**. ([[US Transformer Lead Times Extend to Four Years — pv magazine USA - 2026-05-11]], [[Cleveland-Cliffs Weirton Transformer Plant — Utility Dive - 2024-08-06]])
- Goncalves's line is the hinge: "there will be no AI without electricity, and there will be no electricity without transformers." The grid is as much a chokepoint as the memory fab — but with no union on the valve.
- The reveal: the same AI demand that handed Samsung workers a decade of profit-share is, one layer down, landing on **household power bills** — PJM's Q1 2026 capacity auctions put **$13.77B** of data-center cost onto customers. ([[Quarterly State of the Market Report for PJM Q1 2026 — Monitoring Analytics - 2026-05-14]], [[AI Buildout Grid Constraint]])
- Name the asymmetry plainly and resist the merge: same **cause** (the boom), opposite **directions**. At the memory chokepoint, a group that could organize *captured* a share. At the grid chokepoint, a group that can't *absorbs* the cost. This is **not** "the same money" — it's the same pressure finding the path of least resistance in two different places. ([[AI Cost Incidence]], [[AI Windfall Sharing]])
- The tell that they're connected, not identical: in both places the boom met a chokepoint and tested who had leverage. The chip workers had it. The ratepayer doesn't. (The Unexpected-Connection payoff — Marcus read both stories separately; here's the spine.)
- Tone discipline: per the seed, never write "hyperscalers pay for the bonuses." The grid cost is documented (PJM); the chip-cost pass-through is *not* a flow — held for Section 3.

## Section 3: The Protocol — "Labor Wrote Itself a Claim. The Grid Got a Bill." (~400 words)
*Tone: forward-looking, grounded — implications, not predictions*

- What kind of instrument the Samsung deal actually is: not a raise, a **written, profit-indexed claim on a decade of surplus** — thin on the page, structural in effect. The bounded **1951 Treasury-Fed Accord** parallel: that too was a famously thin document that permanently moved who controlled a surplus, and it happened because the weaker party *refused to keep accommodating the stronger one*. Transferable axis: **a thin written instrument won by refusal** — not central-bank independence. ([[1951 Treasury-Fed Accord]])
- Where the cost of the labor claim actually lands (the honest part): because it's **10.5% *of* profit**, not a charge per chip, it's "a distribution of earnings, not an upfront cost burden." Its incidence falls on **shareholders and reinvestment**, not customers — SK Hynix's reward burden grew **+450%** while CAPEX/R&D grew ~+20–30%. ([[Samsung Strike Risk Gone Now the Real Test Is HBM — Investing.com - 2026-05-27]], [[SK Hynix 100 Trillion Won Reward Burden — Seoul Economic Daily - 2026-05-05]])
- The one inference, named as inference: Samsung *could* use its chokepoint pricing power to rebuild that margin — and if it does, the cost reaches the hyperscalers. "Could," not "does." (JPMorgan's Jay Kwon: 7–12% OP downside from the labor cost.) Keep the verb honest.
- Where it goes next: the claim is already climbing. Korea is debating a **"citizen dividend"** and a sovereign wealth fund — forced by Samsung + SK Hynix potentially owing **more corporate tax than the government expected from every company combined**. ([[Samsung AI Bonuses Prompt Korea Debate — Bloomberg - 2026-05-30]]) In the US, Axios named the boardroom version: the **"AI productivity dividend"** as a 2026 proxy-season demand; UAW and CWA are studying the Samsung playbook. ([[The Coming AI Profit Revolt — Axios - 2026-05-25]])
- Land on recourse (the domain's core question): at the chokepoint you can organize, labor wrote itself a claim. At the chokepoint you can't, the bill just arrives. The open question isn't whether AI's winners get paid — it's whether anyone ever writes a claim on behalf of the people standing at the chokepoint with no valve.

## Section 4: Personal Code — "I Read the Wrong Variable" (~250 words)
*Tone: first-person, direct, a little rueful — voice at its most human*

- Honest self-correction: in April ([[The Bluff Is Over. The Price Isn't.]]) I treated the chip-layer labor risk as a *strike* variable — would they walk out and choke supply? I had the chokepoint right and the mechanism wrong. They didn't strike. They did something more durable: wrote themselves a ten-year claim on the profit. The strike was the threat; the contract was the point.
- Grid-side personal stake: the part of this I actually feel isn't the bonus — it's the line on my own power bill, and I'm not the $400K worker in this story. Most readers aren't.
- Close (callback): return to the two workers at the gate. By now they're the two chokepoints. The reader figures out which one they're standing at. One-more-thing the close adds: the $4,000 worker at least got *something*; the ratepayer is the version of him with no union and no vote — and that's the chokepoint the next fight has to find.
