# Who controls power — and what can you do about it?

That's the question behind everything published here. The Civic Node covers the collision between centralized institutions and the technology that's starting to compete with them. Monetary policy, supply chain politics, AI infrastructure, decentralization — not as separate beats, but as one story about who holds leverage and who doesn't.

One to two articles per week, typically 1,500 words, built from primary sources. Every claim links to its source. If I'm wrong, you can check.

## Who this is for

You follow the news but don't trust the framing. You've noticed that the interesting question is rarely the one the headline asks. You want analysis that connects the dots between domains — why a war in the Middle East affects your RAM prices, why a non-binding letter of intent can reshape a global supply chain, why an efficiency breakthrough might increase demand instead of reducing it.

You read footnotes. You follow source links. You forward articles to one specific person, not to your whole feed.

## Who this is not for

If you want price predictions, ticker alerts, or "when moon" — this isn't the place. If you want confirmation that your side is winning — this isn't the place either. The Civic Node doesn't wear a jersey. It audits the game.

If a single factual error on a topic you know well would make you unsubscribe — good. That's the standard I hold myself to.

## What you'll find here

**Power audits.** Who controls critical infrastructure — memory chips, shipping lanes, energy grids, data networks — and what happens when that control is weaponized or disrupted. One man's signature on a non-binding letter reshaped a $71 billion supply chain. That's not a metaphor; that's the DRAM crisis series.

**Policy mechanics.** Federal Reserve decisions, trade policy, sanctions design — explained through what they actually do to markets and people, not what the press release claims. The gap between official language and observable effect is where the interesting writing lives.

**The decentralization stress test.** Where decentralized technology solves problems that centralized systems structurally can't — and where it's marketing with a whitepaper. I run DePIN infrastructure. I have hardware in the game.

**Cross-domain pattern recognition.** The same dynamic — institutional capture, leverage concentration, chokepoint control — appears across energy, finance, AI, and geopolitics without the people involved noticing they're running the same play. This newsletter names the play.
