# Beliefs & Brain Dump
*Surfaced via interview, 2026-05-01*

---

## What I Want to Be Known For

Insightful, intelligent, informative, helpful. Deeply competent within my field. A good storyteller and interesting person to follow. Funny and entertaining in a "smart" way — never overly academic or preachy. A "man of the people." A cherished teacher of technology.

The key tension: accessible smart. The kind of person who makes you feel like you could've figured it out yourself if someone had just explained it right.

---

## Creators I Want to Emulate

- **Linus Tech Tips** — deeply competent but never stuffy
- **Steven "Destiny" Bonnell** — researches openly, thinks out loud, makes the *process* of figuring things out compelling
- **Hunter S. Thompson** — favorite writer ever. The gonzo lens: the reporter is part of the story, makes everything more vivid and true. Never self-indulgent though — always in service of illuminating something larger.
- **ContraPoints** — cinematic, theatrical, deeply produced video essays. Well-researched and well-sourced. Not politically aligned but genuinely good content.
- **Turkey Tom** — scrappy, internet-native, covers topics you'd never seek out yourself. The mark of a strong voice: the subject matter almost doesn't matter, you're there for *them*. Consistently well-sourced.

What ContraPoints and Turkey Tom share despite being opposites in style: **rigor**. They do the work.

## How I Want My Audience to Feel

Like they understand something better — more deeply. Something they've always "kind of" known already but didn't have the words to describe. The feeling of **recognition, not revelation**. I synthesize ideas they might never have been able to put together themselves, though they've definitely tried.

Not blowing their minds with something alien — giving them the *language* for something they've been circling around.

## Core Beliefs & Positions

### On Technology
- Tech progress is generally good. The trade-offs are worth it.
- The proliferation of the internet was one of humanity's greatest achievements.
- Social media is the pain we must suffer as a result.
- AI can democratize learning the same way the internet democratized access to information.
- **Contrarian position**: AI is awesome AND we should heavily restrict children's access to it during developmental years. Social media showed us what happens when we don't. We shouldn't repeat that mistake.
- The crypto skeptics were mostly right — almost everything has been scams. Bitcoin is the notable exception.

### On Best Practices & Knowledge
- Best practices exist for a reason. It's easier to follow them than to figure out why and break something simpler.
- Every time I've ignored advice from someone wiser, I've regretted it and eventually understood why the best practice exists.
- **We all stand on the shoulders of giants. Accept that with grace. Don't fight it.**
- Same wisdom applies to life: your body already knows what it needs. Stop trying to optimize your way around it.

### On Politics & Nuance
- Can't fall cleanly into any "camp" because I see the nuance in all sides.
- This feels like a curse of intelligence — you can't unsee the complexity once you've noticed it.
- The person who *can't* pick a camp because they genuinely understand all of them is exactly who the audience needs.
- "Both sides" isn't wishy-washy if it's backed by actual knowledge and confidence.

### On the Tech Industry & Gender
- Was genuinely bothered by the sexism/misogyny in tech — had only one female colleague in a peer role across an entire career.
- Gamergate and the Me Too era created a strange guilt-by-association dynamic for men in tech who weren't part of the problem.
- The anger isn't just about the sexism itself — it's about the **lack of nuance** in how it gets discussed. The conversation collapses into teams, and that's its own kind of failure.

## What Makes Me Angry About My Industry

The sexism in tech is real, visible, and self-defeating — and the industry acts surprised when women don't want to be there. But the public conversation about it lacks nuance, and that nuance collapse is its own problem.

More broadly: the **grift culture**. Making money for money's sake is one thing. Successfully building an audience on pure grift makes me question my own value. The implicit contract of "cherished teacher" is: I'm giving you something real. Grift is the exact opposite of that contract.

---

## What I've Figured Out the Hard Way

**Follow best practices. Stop reinventing wheels.**

Every time someone with more experience gave me a best practice and I ignored it, I eventually came to regret it — after exhausting every other option. The hard way teaches you why the easy way was right.

Applied to life: "just do more" is a trap. The real problem wasn't the amount of work — it was ignoring what my nervous system actually needed. Sleep, recovery, emotional processing time. These aren't optional for me.

- **Grift / hustle culture** — the "I made $47k in passive income" pipeline. Making money for money's sake, especially if it means the product is hollow.
- **Contrarianism for its own sake** — disagreeing with mainstream views *because* they're mainstream, not because you've thought it through.
- **The "well actually" guy** — was probably this when younger. Desperately trying not to be now.
- **Clickbait** — understood it has a purpose, would use it if necessary for success, but prefer to be less sensational by default.
- **Doomerism** — I want to help people see bright sides and appreciate the world, not do the opposite.

## Tagline / Brand Direction

Phrases that kept coming back:
- "Mapping out how power filters through the world"
- "Tracing power to its roots"
- "How does power and influence manifest in society?"

The instinct is right — **power** is the through-line. It contains tech, politics, money, institutions, why ideas win, why industries develop the way they do.

### Publication Name: The Civic Node

Already established. Originally set up with a DePIN-heavy influence, but the name has broader range than that and has grown into something more fitting. "Node" implies a point where information flows through — civic information, systems, power. It works.

### Tagline: *Dispatches from inside the machine.*

- **"Inside the machine"** — tech connotation, systemic/power connotation, slightly ominous without being doomer-y. Positions the author as someone who's *been inside* the systems they're explaining, not just observing from outside.
- **"Dispatches"** — journalistic, field-reporter quality. Each piece is a transmission from the inside. Very Thompson.

Together: *The Civic Node — dispatches from inside the machine.*

This answers the question the name raises: *what kind of node? What's being transmitted?* Hard-won perspective from someone who's been inside the systems everyone else is trying to understand from the outside.
