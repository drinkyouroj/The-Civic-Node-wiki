I Had the Wrong Thread

*My first draft joined the bonus and the power bill with the same money. Following the dollars killed the line and left the truer thread: leverage.*

*Process note — analytical backstage for [Samsung's $400,000 Bonus, and the $4,000 One](https://drinkyouroj.substack.com/p/samsungs-400000-bonus-and-the-4000-one).*

There were two analytical errors in this piece. They happened at different stages, and they're different enough that they're worth naming separately.

The first was from April. In ["The Bluff Is Over. The Price Isn't."](https://drinkyouroj.substack.com/p/the-bluff-is-over-the-price-isnt) I flagged Samsung's chip workers as a supply risk and tracked them for a walkout. Right chokepoint, wrong variable. They didn't walk out; they signed. The strike was the threat. The contract was the point. I'd been watching the threat instead of the thing the threat was for.

The second error happened in the first draft of this piece, and it was subtler.

The piece has two halves, and the whole thing rests on joining them. A Samsung chip worker wins a bonus that runs toward $400,000, at the one spot in the AI economy where the people doing the work could choke the supply the boom depends on. A household in Ohio opens a power bill that went up because of data centers it never voted on, somewhere out on the same grid. One boom, two outcomes.

My first draft joined them with money. The closing line said, more or less, that the bonus and the bill come out of the same AI pot. It was the most shareable sentence in the draft. It was also wrong, and what showed me was writing it down in plain words.

Once "the same money" was on the page, I had to follow the dollars, and they don't meet. The Samsung bonus is [a slice of the division's profit](https://www.investing.com/analysis/samsungs-strike-risk-is-gone-now-the-real-test-is-hbm-200680992), paid out after the chips sell, which means it lands on shareholders and on the budget for the next factory. The power bill is a different pot: PJM's capacity auction [added $13.77 billion to customer bills](https://www.monitoringanalytics.com/reports/PJM_State_of_the_Market/2026/2026q1-som-pjm.pdf) across 13 states. I went looking for the dollar that travels from the chip worker's contract to the ratepayer's bill. There isn't one. Nothing moves from one story to the other.

What the two halves actually share is the cause. One buildout hit two bottlenecks. That part is symmetric, and the piece is allowed to compress it. What is not symmetric is the direction. At the bottleneck labor could choke, labor took a cut of the upside. At the bottleneck nobody can choke, the cost just shows up. "Same money" would have flattened the one fact that makes the story worth telling: the outcomes run opposite because the leverage runs opposite.

So the thread isn't dollars. It's the question each bottleneck asks, which is who here can say no. The chip workers had a union and a product nobody could source elsewhere. The ratepayer has a billing address. Same boom, and the answer to that one question sorts who captures the upside from who absorbs the cost.

"Same money" was the better headline and the worse sentence. A false symmetry reads as analysis and falls apart the moment you write it plainly. When the parallel is real, compress it. When it isn't, name the asymmetry. The plain sentence is the test that tells you which.
