## Editorial notes on the variable choices

- **Anchor `1 RULING`**: this note is verbal, not numeric — it has no "128 weeks" or "50%." Rather than fall back to the template's phrase exception, the anchor inverts the convention: the headline claims `'NO RULING'`; the lower-right supplies the one ruling that existed. It plays directly off the note's pivot — *"There was a ruling. I had told you there wasn't."* A count of `1` still travels as a number, so the editorial signature holds.
- **Anchor gloss `the one I told you didn't exist`**: lifts the note's confession (the writer told the reader there was no ruling; there was). Keeps the first-person voice that defines the series.
- **Underline on `'NO RULING'`**: the false phrase being corrected. The brick-red underline reads as the editor's correction mark on the exact sentence that got cut — the cover crosses out the claim the way the v4 draft did.
- **Margin note `there was one.`**: three words, pairs with the underlined `'NO RULING'` — reader sees the false claim, gets the correction in the margin, and the `1 RULING` anchor confirms the count. Well under the cursive legibility ceiling.
- **Italic `ALMOST`**: the near-miss signal. This is the sanctioned title-exception case (a sentence caught pre-publish, not a published error), and `ALMOST` is the word carrying that distinction — italicizing it, rather than the underlined `'NO RULING'`, gives the cover two separate emphasis points instead of stacking both on one phrase.
