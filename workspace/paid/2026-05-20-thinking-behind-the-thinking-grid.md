I Had the Wrong Bottom of the Stack

*The five-forces piece named the right cast in the wrong order. Grid capacity is the floor that sits underneath all five.*

*Process note — analytical backstage for [12 Gigawatts Were Announced. 4 Are Being Built.](https://drinkyouroj.substack.com/p/12-gigawatts-were-announced-4-are-being-built)*

I wrote two pieces about DRAM concentration in April. They were correct on their own terms. They were also looking at the second-most-binding constraint and treating it as the first.

The original piece named five forces holding DRAM prices at roughly double where the models say they should be. Force 4 was the data center delay: transformers, switchgear, batteries, all on procurement timelines longer than the financing horizon. I had it sitting at fourth in the stack.

The thing that broke that read was Microsoft moving in.

I read the Stargate Abilene cancellation in March as a demand failure. Oracle and OpenAI walked away from an 800 MW expansion when the financing collapsed and the demand forecast softened. That fit the existing DRAM narrative cleanly: Force 4 confirming itself. I wrote it as a confirmation. It should have been a discovery.

A demand failure leaves the site sitting. This site didn't sit. Oracle out, OpenAI out, Microsoft in, Crusoe building generation on-site, Meta in talks for the excess. Three handoffs in ninety days, each one an upgrade rather than a write-down. That's not how a stranded asset moves.

What got inherited was the existing 1.2 GW of grid position. The substation. Once I saw that, the rest of the analysis reorganized around it.

The cast I built around DRAM was right. The order was wrong. Chip supply is concentrated and slow. Grid capacity is more concentrated and slower. The chip layer reverts when Chinese fab capacity lands in 2028. The grid layer reverts in seven years if everything breaks right. The gating element at hyperscaler scale is a circuit breaker manufactured in South Korea on a 128-week order book.

The two April pieces still hold. They were just naming the wrong floor.

When the private analysis produces a correction, the published work should say so.
