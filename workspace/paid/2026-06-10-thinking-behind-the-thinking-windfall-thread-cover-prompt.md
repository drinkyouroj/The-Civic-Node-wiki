## Editorial notes on the variable choices

- **Headline staircase `I HAD THE / WRONG / THREAD`**: isolating `WRONG` alone on its own line gives the correction word maximum visual weight — the reader's eye drops from the setup (`I HAD THE`) onto the self-diagnosis (`WRONG`) before landing on the thing that was wrong (`THREAD`). The 3-1-1 structure mirrors the note's own beat: the setup, the admission, the object.
- **Italic `WRONG`**: standard correction-frame pattern for the series (same as the May 20 grid note). The italic signals self-correction rather than accusation — the writer catching their own move.
- **Anchor `13.77 BILLION` / `what the bonus never reached`**: the flagship's headline puts `$400,000` front and center. The note's discovery is that $13.77 billion in PJM capacity-auction costs is a *different pot* — the bonus dollars never arrived there. Anchoring the cover on the "different pot" rather than the shared headline number makes the note's specific analytical move visible at a glance, not just the flagship's subject.
- **Underline on `THREAD`**: the word being corrected in the title. The brick-red underline reads as the writer's own correction mark on the exact noun the note dismantles.
- **Margin note `the thread is leverage.`**: four words + period, well under the cursive legibility ceiling. Pairs directly with `THREAD` underlined — reader sees the false noun, margin supplies the replacement. Echoes the note's own corrected-read sentence: "So the thread isn't dollars. It's the question each bottleneck asks, which is who here can say no."
