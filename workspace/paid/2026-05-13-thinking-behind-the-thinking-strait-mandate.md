I Had the Wrong Protagonist

*Two Fed pieces in two weeks, both organized around the chair. The third piece is about what the chair can't reach.*

*Process note — analytical backstage for [The Strait Is the Mandate]()*

I wrote two Fed pieces in two weeks treating the chair as the protagonist. That was the wrong protagonist.

The first was about the Powell pressure campaign. The second was about the Warsh confirmation. Both organized around the person: who gets the seat, what they'd publicly said about tariffs.

The analysis reorganized when I started looking at what rate policy can actually reach.

Two of the Fed's biggest constraints right now aren't domestic. The Strait of Hormuz is closed to 95% of pre-war traffic. The effective tariff rate sits at 19.7%, highest since Smoot-Hawley. Rate hikes don't reopen shipping lanes. Rate cuts don't remove executive tariff policy. The FOMC has one lever and two active supply shocks that lever can't address.

Warsh inherits that. He holds one of twelve FOMC votes. His credibility arrives pre-loaded with everything the confirmation process required him to say. None of that changes what the tool is built for.

The piece ended up being about the tool. That's what I missed in the earlier framing.

When the private analysis produces a correction, the published work should say so.
