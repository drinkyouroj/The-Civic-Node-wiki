# 12 GIGAWATTS WERE ANNOUNCED. 4 ARE BEING BUILT.
## Narration Script — The Civic Node №003
## 11 slides · teaser/introduction

---

**[SLIDE 01 — TITLE]**

Every AI earnings call tells the same story — chips, GPUs, capex
denominated in billions. That's the layer the financial press has
bookmarked. It's also the wrong layer.

Twelve gigawatts of US data center capacity were planned for 2026.
Only four are under construction. 2027 looks worse from here.

The binding constraint is electrical. It shows up in the
interconnection queue, not the earnings call.

---

**[SLIDE 02 — CONTEXT INTRO]**

Interconnection queue filings and transformer procurement contracts —
public, updated every quarter, and dispositive: a data center doesn't
get power until they say it does.

---

**[SLIDE 03 — DELIVERY VS. ANNOUNCEMENT]**

Four gigawatts under construction against twelve announced for 2026.
One-third delivery rate.

The bottleneck named in the data isn't chips. It's electrical gear —
transformers, batteries, circuit breakers — less than ten percent of
project cost, but the only thing that decides whether the campus
actually comes online.

The median wait for a US grid connection last year was fifty-five
months. In California, nine years. In 2008, under two.

---

**[SLIDE 04 — STARGATE ABILENE]**

In March 2026, the 800 MW Stargate Abilene expansion died. Oracle and
OpenAI's financing fell apart, and the press called it a demand
failure.

Then Microsoft moved in. Crusoe started building a power plant
on-site. Meta entered talks for the excess capacity with NVIDIA
brokering — each handoff an upgrade, not a write-down.

What Microsoft inherited wasn't compute. It was 1.2 gigawatts of grid
position — the queue clearance the original Stargate build had already
burned through. They weren't buying the building.
They were buying the time already spent getting it powered.

---

**[SLIDE 05 — FRAME INTRO]**

Between the announcement and the megawatt, there are three nested
calendars, and they don't sequence. They run in parallel. The longest
one wins.

---

**[SLIDE 06 — THE GRID STACK]**

First calendar: interconnection. Four and a half years, US median,
for projects completing in 2024. Seven years in Northern Virginia,
where most of the hyperscaler capacity is competing for the same queue
position. Nine-plus in California. Seventy-seven percent of projects
in the queue between 2000 and 2019 withdrew before energization — the
wait outlived the financing.

Second: transformer procurement. 128-week lead time on large power
transformers as of Q2 2025. Demand is up 274 percent since 2019, and
Wood Mackenzie projects a thirty percent supply gap.

Third: substation and transmission. Seven to ten years for permitting
and siting. That's Microsoft's own framing from January 2026 — and
it's not a regulatory fix. Transmission permitting takes the time it
takes.

---

**[SLIDE 07 — DECONGESTION DATES]**

The chip supply story loosens by 2028. Chinese fabs are targeting 2027
production; competitive pricing pressure arrives the year after. The
market reads this layer, prices this layer, and the headlines live
here.

The grid story reverts in seven years, if everything goes right. The
transformer gap widens before it narrows. The interconnection queue
can't be reformed to fit a five-year financing window.

The press release is in dollars. The building delivers in megawatts,
on a timeline that doesn't fit in a financial model.

---

**[SLIDE 08 — FIG. 02: THE THREE CALENDARS]**

Queue: four and a half years. Transformers: two and a half. Substation:
seven to ten — all three running concurrently, and the project isn't
done until the last one clears.

The on-site generation deals are the tell. Crusoe building its own
power plant at Abilene, Google paying four and three-quarter billion
for Intersect Power — those aren't strategy calls. Those are
hyperscalers who've looked at the queue and decided they can't wait
for grid power on any viable timeline, so they're routing around it.

---

**[SLIDE 09 — ANDREW LIKENS QUOTE]**

Andrew Likens, energy and infrastructure lead at Crusoe:

*"If one piece of your supply chain is delayed, then your whole
project can't deliver."*

He's talking about the transformer. Not the GPU.
