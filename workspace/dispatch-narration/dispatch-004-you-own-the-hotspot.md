# YOU OWN THE HOTSPOT. NOVA LABS OWNS WHAT IT EARNS.
## Narration Script — The Civic Node №004
## 11 slides · teaser/introduction

---

**[SLIDE 01 — TITLE]**

If you bought a McDonald's franchise, you'd read the disclosure document
first. Federal law requires it. The pricing. The exit terms. What happens
if McDonald's changes the menu in a way that costs you money.

Three hundred eighty-five thousand people bought hardware that earns four
to eight dollars a month, at rates someone else sets. They thought they
were buying a business. They bought a franchise. Nobody handed them the
disclosure document until three years later.

This is what a DePIN franchise looks like when the contract gets
documented in hindsight.

---

**[SLIDE 02 — PART ONE: CONTEXT]**

Operators bought a franchise. They were sold a business.

The hardware they paid for is theirs. The pricing authority that
determines what their hardware earns is not.

That's the whole argument. The rest is just verifying it.

---

**[SLIDE 03 — UNIT ECONOMICS]**

The math is on the slide. Nine forty-nine for a pro outdoor unit. Four to
eight dollars a month after the August 2025 halving. Payback: ten to
twenty years.

Ten years is what asphalt roof shingles take to wear out. Twenty is most
of a mortgage. The hardware earns on a timeline that doesn't fit any
normal investment horizon.

At rates Nova Labs sets. They negotiate the carrier deals with T-Mobile
and AT&T. The operator ran the box.

---

**[SLIDE 04 — HIP-143 / THE AUTO-EXTENSION]**

In April 2025, a governance vote called HIP-143 gave Nova Labs the
authority to negotiate carrier pricing without routing each deal through
governance. The proposal was sold as a one-year sunset.

Read the text closer. It says the authority extends for one additional
year if no overriding HIP passes.

In April 2026, no overriding HIP passed. The authority auto-extended
itself. It now runs through April 2027.

The headline was a one-year sunset. The contract was a two-year clause.
Nobody overrode it.

---

**[SLIDE 05 — PART TWO: FRAME]**

Two halves of the network. Two votes, six months apart. Three named
proxies controlling the outcomes.

Each vote starts from a more concentrated baseline than the last.

---

**[SLIDE 06 — WHERE THE MONEY IS, WHERE THE VOTES WERE]**

Here's where the money is.

The IoT side of Helium — all three hundred and eighty-five thousand
hotspots — generates one hundred and twenty-four dollars in data transfer
revenue per day. That's the entire network.

The Mobile side — routing carrier offload for T-Mobile and AT&T —
generates fifty-six thousand, six hundred and thirty-five dollars per
day. Same protocol. Different revenue ladder.

One half is the revenue. The other half is the hardware that gives the
project something to point at.

HIP-143 in April 2025 gave Nova Labs the authority to negotiate the
Mobile revenue. Nova Labs's own proxy held 26% of the yes votes.
ferebee — a co-author of the proposal — held 24%. Half the yes votes
came from two wallets, one of which proposed the change.

Six months later, HIP-148 killed Mobile Mapping rewards and routed 24%
of Mobile emissions directly to Nova Labs. ferebee held 31% by then.
Nova Labs 26%. A third labeled proxy — Jay M. — held 5%. Top three
combined: 62%.

---

**[SLIDE 07 — THE MISSING DOCUMENT]**

A McDonald's franchisee reads a Franchise Disclosure Document before
signing. Federal law requires it. The pricing, the exit terms, the
geographic provisions, all in writing, before any capital moves.

The Helium operator received the equivalent document as a sequence of
HIPs — written after the hardware was already plugged in. Works the deal
out three years later, from a Messari sector report.

That's the deal that needs to be the marketing. Right now it's the deal
the operator pieces together from a third-party research note.

---

**[SLIDE 08 — TOP SINGLE VOTER SHARE]**

Three votes. Top single proxy share rose: 15%, 26%, 31%. November 2024
to October 2025. Eleven months.

Between the two big votes — HIP-143 and HIP-148 — ferebee's holdings
grew fifty-five and a half percent. Total network participation grew
eighteen percent in the same six months. The proposing-entity co-author
accumulated voting power at three times the network average while
authoring the proposals that consolidated revenue.

The architecture has a direction. Each future HIP starts from a more
concentrated baseline than the last.

---

**[SLIDE 09 — HIP-148 PROPOSAL TEXT]**

This is the proposal text, word for word.

*"To simplify technical implementation, we propose to emit the full
allocation to the single Service Provider Nova Labs."*

Passed ninety-six point seven two percent. October 2025. Ratified by
the same proxies that benefit from it.

---

**[SLIDE 10 — CALL / FOUR DISCLOSURES]**

Do the math before the hardware ships. Four things you'd want in
writing — the same things federal law requires before a McDonald's
franchise.

One: a written floor on material reward changes, plus a right to exit
at that floor.

Two: aggregate revenue disclosure, audited annually.

Three: geographic acknowledgment — rural coverage and urban traffic are
different goods, priced differently.

Four: sunsets that require an active re-vote to extend, not ones that
auto-extend by default when the network does nothing.

That's a fixable architecture. The fix is structural, not regulatory.

---

**[SLIDE 11 — END]**

Standard disclosure. No token positions, no advisory roles, no
allocation letters, no sponsored content.

I ran more than twelve Datagram nodes for a few months. I moderated
their community for seven. Without pay. That project collapsed. The
piece names where my own framing on Datagram was wrong, and what I
should have caught earlier.

If I'm wrong on Helium, the primary sources are linked. Every number
you saw can be checked. Vote records, proposal text, financial reports.

385,000 Helium hotspot operators paid $249 to $949 for hardware, plugged it in, and started earning $4 to $8 a month at rates Nova Labs sets through carrier negotiations with T-Mobile and AT&T. In April 2025, HIP-143 delegated pricing authority to Nova Labs. The vote was sold as a 1-year sunset. The proposal's own text said the authority would extend for one additional year if no overriding HIP passed. In April 2026, no overriding HIP passed.
